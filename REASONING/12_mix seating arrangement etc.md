# Reasoning Ability — Topic 12: Seating Arrangements, Puzzles and Non-Verbal Reasoning

## 1. Seating Arrangement (बैठने की व्यवस्था)

Seating arrangement questions provide clues about people sitting in a row, circle, or other formation. You must determine their positions or answer questions about who sits next to whom.

### A. Linear Arrangement

People sit in a straight row.

**Rules:**

- Left and right depend on the person's facing direction.
    
- If everyone faces north, their left and right correspond to the usual left and right when viewed from behind them.
    
- If everyone faces south, their left and right are reversed from the observer's perspective.
    
- “Immediate left” means the adjacent seat, not any seat farther away.
    

**Example:**

Five people A, B, C, D and E sit in a row. C sits in the middle, A is immediately left of C, and B is immediately right of C.

`_ A C B _`

A and B are immediate neighbours of C. The two remaining positions are unassigned.

### B. Circular Arrangement

People sit around a circular table.

**Rules:**

- Facing the centre: a person's left is clockwise, and their right is anticlockwise.
    
- Facing outward: these directions reverse.
    
- Fix one person in a position first to avoid rotating the entire arrangement repeatedly.
    

**Solving strategy:**

1. Draw a circle and mark the available positions.
    
2. Place any person whose position is definitely known.
    
3. Establish who is opposite whom, if applicable.
    
4. Fill immediate neighbours using the clues.
    
5. Check every clue before finalising the arrangement.
    

**Exam tip:** Always confirm whether people are facing the centre or facing outward before interpreting left and right.

## 2. Puzzles (पहेलियाँ)

Puzzles combine several clues involving people, days, floors, jobs, colours, or objects.

### Common Types

- Floor-based puzzles
    
- Day and month scheduling
    
- Person–profession matching
    
- Age or height comparisons
    
- Box or object ordering
    
- Multiple-variable puzzles
    

### Solving Method

1. List all people or objects.
    
2. Create a table for the variables.
    
3. Write definite clues first.
    
4. Add negative clues such as “A does not live on floor 2.”
    
5. Test possible arrangements against every clue.
    

### Solved Example: Floor-Based Puzzle

Three people A, B and C live on floors 1, 2 and 3, one per floor. A lives above B, and C lives on floor 3.

**Solution:**

- C occupies floor 3.
    
- A lives above B.
    
- A cannot live on floor 3 because C already occupies it.
    
- Therefore, A must live on floor 2 and B on floor 1.
    

|Floor|Person|
|---|---|
|3|C|
|2|A|
|1|B|

**Answer: A lives on floor 2.**

## 3. Cube and Dice (घन और पासा)

Dice questions involve identifying opposite faces, adjacent faces, or the face that appears after rotation.

### Key Rules

- A standard die has six faces.
    
- Opposite faces do not share an edge.
    
- Adjacent faces share an edge.
    
- Rotating a die changes the visible faces but does not change which faces are opposite.
    
- Do not assume opposite numbers sum to 7 unless the question specifies a standard numbered die.
    

### Solved Example

A die has faces numbered 1 through 6, and the question explicitly states that it is a standard die with opposite pairs summing to 7.

Therefore:

|Face|Opposite Face|
|---|---|
|1|6|
|2|5|
|3|4|

If face 2 is on top, face 5 must be at the bottom.

**Answer: 5.**

**Exam tip:** For two views of the same die, compare the faces that remain common. Their arrangement and relative positions can help reveal the opposite face.

## 4. Paper Folding (कागज मोड़ना)

A paper-folding question shows a sheet folded one or more times, then punched or cut. You must predict the unfolded pattern.

### Rules

- Each fold creates symmetry across the fold line.
    
- A hole away from a fold line usually produces a reflected hole at each corresponding position when unfolded.
    
- Repeated folds multiply the number of reflected copies, provided the hole does not lie on a fold line.
    
- A cut on a fold can produce a different pattern from a cut in the interior.
    

### Solved Example 1

Fold a square sheet in half once, punch one hole away from the fold, and unfold it.

**Answer:** The result contains two holes, mirrored across the fold.

### Solved Example 2

Fold a square sheet in half horizontally and then in half vertically. Punch a hole away from both fold lines and unfold the sheet.

**Answer:** Generally, four corresponding holes appear, one for each reflected position.

**Exam tip:** Unfold the paper one crease at a time, reversing the folds in the opposite order to which they were made.

## 5. Mirror Image (दर्पण प्रतिबिंब)

A mirror-image question asks what a figure or text looks like when reflected in a vertical mirror.

### Main Rule

A vertical mirror reverses left and right. It does not reverse top and bottom.

**Check the following:**

- Direction of arrows
    
- Which side contains a dot or shaded region
    
- Order of letters and numbers
    
- Orientation of asymmetric shapes
    

**Exam trick:** Do not confuse a mirror image with rotating the figure by 180°.

---

## 6. Water Image (जल प्रतिबिंब)

A water image is the reflection of a figure in a horizontal surface, such as still water.

### Main Rule

A horizontal reflection reverses top and bottom. Left and right remain unchanged.

**Example:** An upward-pointing arrow becomes a downward-pointing arrow in its water image.

### Mirror Image vs Water Image

|Feature|Mirror Image|Water Image|
|---|---|---|
|Reflection|Vertical|Horizontal|
|Reverses|Left and right|Top and bottom|
|Left and right|Reversed|Unchanged|
|Top and bottom|Unchanged|Reversed|

**Exam trick:** For a mirror image, think left–right reversal. For a water image, think top–bottom reversal.

## 7. Figure Completion (आकृति पूर्ण करना)

You are given an incomplete pattern and must select the option that correctly completes it.

### What to Check

- Continuity of lines
    
- Matching curves and edges
    
- Symmetry
    
- Repeated shapes or symbols
    
- Rotation and size
    
- Shading or dot patterns
    

### Example

If a square is missing its lower-right corner, the correct option must continue the two adjoining edges and preserve the intended pattern.

**Method:**

1. Identify the incomplete boundary.
    
2. Observe the direction and length of the missing lines.
    
3. Compare the options against the shape, orientation, and line continuity.
    
4. Eliminate options that break the pattern.

## 8. Embedded Figures (अंतर्निहित आकृतियाँ)

An embedded-figure question asks you to find a small target shape hidden inside a more complex drawing.

### How to Solve

1. Memorise the target shape's essential edges and angles.
    
2. Ignore extra lines in the larger figure.
    
3. Rotate the target mentally if the question permits rotated orientations.
    
4. Trace the outline rather than relying on the overall appearance.
    

**Example:** A triangle may be hidden inside a star or a collection of intersecting lines. It is enough for the required three edges to be present, even if extra lines cross the triangle.

**Exam tip:** Focus on the target's angles and line connections instead of the entire drawing.

## 9. Figure Counting (आकृतियों की गणना)

These questions ask you to count how many triangles, squares, rectangles, or other shapes are present in a diagram.

### Triangle-Counting Method

- Count the smallest triangles first.
    
- Count larger triangles made from multiple small ones.
    
- Check every possible orientation.
    
- Avoid counting the same triangle twice.
    

### Solved Examples

**Example 1:** A square divided by one diagonal.

**Answer:** 2 triangles — the two halves.

**Example 2:** A square divided by both diagonals.

**Answer:** 8 triangles — four small triangles and four larger triangles formed by combining adjacent small triangles.

**Exam tip:** Count systematically by size and orientation rather than guessing from the picture.

## 10. Space Visualization (स्थानिक दृश्यीकरण)

Space visualization tests your ability to imagine objects being rotated, rearranged, or viewed from different directions.

### Common Questions

- Rotating a cube or object
    
- Identifying a 2D view of a 3D object
    
- Matching an object from different viewing angles
    
- Predicting the shape after rotation
    
- Folding a flat pattern into a solid
    

### Example

A cube is rotated by 90°. The face on top may change, but the cube still has six faces and its opposite-face relationships remain unchanged.

**Solving strategy:**

1. Identify a distinctive face or feature.
    
2. Track its position during rotation.
    
3. Check which faces become adjacent or opposite.
    
4. Compare the final orientation with the options.
    

---

## 11. Visual Memory (दृश्य स्मृति)

Visual memory questions test your ability to remember a diagram, sequence, symbol arrangement, or pattern and identify it later.

### Useful Techniques

- Divide the image into smaller regions.
    
- Remember distinctive features such as a shaded corner or unusual symbol.
    
- Note relative positions rather than memorising every detail independently.
    
- Compare the answer options systematically.
    

### Example

If a figure has a black dot in the upper-left corner and a triangle at the bottom, remember those anchor positions. They help distinguish the original from rotated or reflected versions.

**Exam tip:** Remember distinctive features and their relative positions first.

## 12. Non-Verbal Reasoning (अशाब्दिक तर्क)

Non-verbal reasoning uses figures rather than words or numerical statements. It includes several of the preceding topics and also pattern-based questions.

### Common Types

- Figure series
    
- Figure analogy
    
- Odd figure out
    
- Rotation and reflection
    
- Missing figures in matrices
    
- Shape and symbol transformations
    

### Figure-Series Example

Suppose an arrow rotates 90° clockwise at each step:

Up → Right → Down → Left → ?

**Answer: Up.**

The rotation continues by 90° clockwise.

### Figure-Analogy Example

If a shape is reflected horizontally in the first pair, apply the same reflection to the second shape to find the missing figure.

**Golden rule:** Identify the transformation — rotation, reflection, addition, removal, or change in shading — before choosing an option.

## 13. Comparison of Major Question Types

|Topic|Main Skill|Best Technique|
|---|---|---|
|Linear arrangement|Order people in a row|Draw numbered positions|
|Circular arrangement|Arrange people around a table|Fix one person and track facing direction|
|Puzzles|Combine several clues|Make a table and eliminate impossible cases|
|Cube and dice|Track faces and rotations|Mark opposite and adjacent faces|
|Paper folding|Predict reflected holes or cuts|Unfold one crease at a time|
|Mirror image|Reverse left and right|Imagine a vertical mirror|
|Water image|Reverse top and bottom|Imagine a horizontal reflection|
|Figure completion|Complete an incomplete pattern|Match edges and symmetry|
|Embedded figures|Find a hidden shape|Trace the target outline|
|Figure counting|Count shapes systematically|Count by size and orientation|
|Space visualization|Mentally rotate objects|Track faces and orientation|
|Visual memory|Recall positions and features|Remember distinctive anchor points|
|Non-verbal reasoning|Detect visual rules|Check rotation, reflection, and repetition|

## 14. Final Exam Tips

1. Draw a diagram instead of keeping long seating clues in your head.
    
2. For circular arrangements, check whether people face inward or outward.
    
3. In puzzles, place definite information first and eliminate impossible cases.
    
4. In dice questions, use the opposite-face information given by the question.
    
5. For paper folding, reverse each fold in the opposite order.
    
6. For mirror and water images, remember which axis is reversed.
    
7. For figure counting, count systematically and avoid double-counting.
    
8. For non-verbal series, test rotation, reflection, addition, removal, and shading changes.
    
9. Always compare the final arrangement against every clue before selecting an answer.

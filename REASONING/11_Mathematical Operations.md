# Reasoning Ability — Topic 11: Mathematical Operations (गणितीय संक्रियाएँ)

## 1. Symbol Replacement (प्रतीकों का प्रतिस्थापन)

In these questions, mathematical symbols are assigned different meanings. You must replace them with their given meanings before calculating.

### Standard BODMAS Rule

- **B — Brackets**
    
- **O — Orders** (powers and roots)
    
- **D — Division**
    
- **M — Multiplication**
    
- **A — Addition**
    
- **S — Subtraction**
    

**Important rules:**

- Division and multiplication have equal precedence and are evaluated from left to right.
    
- Addition and subtraction also have equal precedence and are evaluated from left to right.
    
- Always replace the symbols before applying BODMAS.
    

### Solved Example 1

If `+` means `×` and `−` means `+`, evaluate:

8 + 3 − 2

**Step 1: Replace the symbols.**

8 × 3 + 2

**Step 2: Apply BODMAS.**

24 + 2 = 26

**Answer: 26.**

### Solved Example 2

If `×` means `+`, `+` means `−`, and `−` means `×`, evaluate:

6 × 4 + 2 − 3

**Step 1: Replace each symbol.**

6 + 4 − 2 × 3

**Step 2: Apply multiplication first.**

6 + 4 − 6 = 4

**Answer: 4.**

### Solved Example 3

If `÷` means `+` and `+` means `÷`, evaluate:

12 ÷ 6 + 2

**Step 1: Replace the symbols.**

12 + 6 ÷ 2

**Step 2: Apply division first.**

12 + 3 = 15

**Answer: 15.**

## 2. Arithmetic Operators (अंकगणितीय संक्रियाएँ)

These questions use normal arithmetic operations but may ask you to choose the correct operation, identify an incorrect equation, or make an equation true by interchanging signs or numbers.

### Type A: Interchanging Mathematical Signs

**Example 1:** Which two signs should be interchanged to make the equation correct?

8 + 4 × 2 = 24

Try interchanging `+` and `×`:

8 × 4 + 2 = 34

This does not give 24, so that interchange is incorrect.

**Method:** Substitute the proposed interchange, then evaluate using BODMAS. Never assume that swapping two symbols will automatically make the equation correct.

### Type B: Choosing the Correct Operation

**Example 2:** Which operation should replace `?`?

18 ? 6 = 3

Since 18 ÷ 6 = 3, the required operation is division.

**Answer: ÷**

### Type C: Making an Equation Correct

**Example 3:** Insert mathematical signs between 4, 4 and 4 to obtain 8.

Using addition alone:

4 + 4 + 4 = 12

This does not produce 8.

The expression

4 + 4 × (4 − 4) = 4

also does not produce 8.

**Important:** The supplied example does not establish a valid expression that equals 8. To solve this type of question uniquely, the allowed operations and any restrictions on brackets or repeated signs must be specified.

## 3. Equation-Based Operations (समीकरण आधारित संक्रियाएँ)

These questions give equations involving unknown numbers or unusual operators. You must determine the missing value or identify the rule.

### Type A: Find the Missing Number

**Example 1:**

7 × ? = 42

? = 42 ÷ 7 = 6

**Answer: 6.**

### Type B: Find the Operator Rule

Suppose an operation `★` is defined as:

a ★ b = a² + b

Find 4 ★ 3.

**Step 1: Apply the defined rule.**

4 ★ 3 = 4² + 3

**Step 2: Calculate.**

16 + 3 = 19

**Answer: 19.**

### Type C: Solve a Two-Step Operation

Suppose:

a ◆ b = 2a + b

Find 5 ◆ 4.

**Solution:**

5 ◆ 4 = 2(5) + 4

= 10 + 4

= 14

**Answer: 14.**

### Type D: Identify a Pattern from Examples

Suppose:

- 2 ★ 3 = 7
    
- 3 ★ 4 = 13
    
- 4 ★ 5 = 21
    

A possible rule is:

a ★ b = a² + b

**Check the rule against all examples:**

- 2² + 3 = 7
    
- 3² + 4 = 13
    
- 4² + 5 = 21
    

The rule fits all three examples.

Therefore:

5 ★ 6 = 5² + 6

= 25 + 6

= 31

**Answer: 31.**

**Exam tip:** Verify a proposed rule against every example given. Do not infer a rule from just one equation if multiple patterns may fit.

## 4. Quick-Solving Strategy

|Question Type|Best Method|
|---|---|
|Symbol replacement|Replace every symbol, then use BODMAS|
|Interchanging signs|Test the permitted swap and recalculate|
|Missing operator|Identify which operation gives the required result|
|Defined operation|Substitute the numbers into the given rule|
|Equation with unknown|Isolate the unknown using inverse operations|
|Pattern-based equations|Test one rule against every example|

## 5. Common Mistakes

1. Applying BODMAS before replacing the symbols.
    
2. Replacing one symbol but forgetting another occurrence.
    
3. Calculating multiplication before division when division appears first from the left.
    
4. Treating a custom operator like ordinary multiplication or addition.
    
5. Choosing a pattern that fits only one example.
    
6. Forgetting that multiplication and division have equal precedence.
    
7. Forgetting that addition and subtraction have equal precedence.
    
8. Assuming an equation has a valid solution without checking the permitted operations and restrictions.
    

## 6. Final Revision Points

- Replace all custom symbols before calculating.
    
- Apply BODMAS after replacement.
    
- Evaluate multiplication and division from left to right when they have equal precedence.
    
- Evaluate addition and subtraction from left to right when they have equal precedence.
    
- For custom operators, follow the exact definition given in the question.
    
- For missing-number questions, use inverse operations.
    
- For pattern questions, verify the rule against every provided example.
    
- For sign-interchange questions, test each permitted pair systematically.

# 1. Number Series

A number series follows a mathematical rule. The rule may involve addition, subtraction, multiplication, division or a combination of operations.

## A. Addition or Subtraction Series

**Example:** 5, 9, 13, 17, ?

Calculate the differences:

- 9 − 5 = 4
    
- 13 − 9 = 4
    
- 17 − 13 = 4
    

The difference is consistently +4.

Next term = 17 + 4 = 21

**Answer: 21**

## B. Increasing Differences

**Example:** 2, 5, 9, 14, 20, ?

|Consecutive Terms|Difference|
|---|---|
|2 → 5|+3|
|5 → 9|+4|
|9 → 14|+5|
|14 → 20|+6|

The differences increase by 1 each time. The next difference is +7.

Next term = 20 + 7 = 27

**Answer: 27**

## C. Decreasing Differences

**Example:** 40, 34, 29, 25, 22, ?

|Consecutive Terms|Difference|
|---|---|
|40 → 34|−6|
|34 → 29|−5|
|29 → 25|−4|
|25 → 22|−3|

The differences are −6, −5, −4 and −3. The next difference is −2.

Next term = 22 − 2 = 20

**Answer: 20**

# 2. Difference Series

When the pattern is not immediately obvious, subtract each term from the next. If necessary, calculate the differences again.

## A. First-Difference Method

**Example:** 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?

First differences:

- 6 − 2 = 4
    
- 12 − 6 = 6
    
- 20 − 12 = 8
    
- 30 − 20 = 10
    

The differences increase by 2 each time. The next difference is +12.

Next term = 30 + 12 = 42

**Answer: 42**

## B. Second-Difference Method

**Example:** 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, ?

First differences:

3, 5, 7, 9

Second differences:

2, 2, 2

The second difference is constant. This indicates a quadratic pattern.

The next first difference is 11.

Next term = 25 + 11 = 36

**Answer: 36**

**Exam tip:** Always check first differences before attempting a complicated formula.

# 3. Multiplication and Division Series

These series usually involve multiplication or division, sometimes combined with addition or subtraction.

|Series|Rule|Next Term|
|---|---|---|
|3, 6, 12, 24, ?|Multiply by 2|48|
|2, 6, 18, 54, ?|Multiply by 3|162|
|160, 80, 40, 20, ?|Divide by 2|10|
|2, 5, 11, 23, 47, ?|Multiply by 2, then add 1|95|
|3, 7, 15, 31, ?|Multiply by 2, then add 1|63|
|5, 11, 23, 47, ?|Multiply by 2, then add 1|95|

## Worked Example

**Series:** 4, 9, 19, 39, ?

Apply the rule: Multiply by 2 and add 1.

- 4 × 2 + 1 = 9
    
- 9 × 2 + 1 = 19
    
- 19 × 2 + 1 = 39
    
- 39 × 2 + 1 = 79
    

**Answer: 79**

# 5. Alternating Series

An alternating series contains two or more interleaved patterns. Separate the odd-position terms from the even-position terms.

## A. Worked Example 1

**Series:** 2, 10, 4, 20, 6, 30, ?

Odd-position terms: 2, 4, 6, ?

- Add 2 each time.
    
- Next odd-position term = 8.
    

Even-position terms: 10, 20, 30

- Add 10 each time.
    

The next term is in an odd position.

**Answer: 8**

## B. Worked Example 2

**Series:** 1, 4, 3, 8, 5, 12, ?

Odd-position terms: 1, 3, 5, ?

- Add 2 each time.
    
- Next odd-position term = 7.
    

Even-position terms: 4, 8, 12

- Add 4 each time.
    

**Answer: 7**

**Exam tip:** If the entire series looks irregular, separate odd and even positions before assuming a complicated rule.

# 6. Alphabet Series

Alphabet series questions use letter positions, forward or backward movement, and sometimes reverse-alphabet relationships.

Remember the positions:

A = 1, B = 2, C = 3, ..., Z = 26.

## A. Common Alphabet Series

|Series|Rule|Next Term|
|---|---|---|
|A, C, E, G, ?|Advance by 2 letters|I|
|B, E, H, K, ?|Advance by 3 letters|N|
|Z, W, T, Q, ?|Move backward by 3 letters|N|
|A, D, H, M, ?|Advance by 3, 4, 5, then 6 positions|S|
|AZ, BY, CX, ?|First letter +1; second letter −1|DW|

## B. Worked Example

**Series:** C, F, J, O, ?

Convert letters into alphabetical positions:

- C = 3
    
- F = 6
    
- J = 10
    
- O = 15
    

Differences:

+3, +4, +5

The next difference is +6.

15 + 6 = 21

The 21st letter is U.

**Answer: U**

## C. Reverse Alphabet

# 7. Alphanumeric Series

Alphanumeric series combine letters and numbers. You may need to find the next term, identify a missing term or determine an element's position.

## A. Worked Example 1

**Series:** A1, C3, E5, G7, ?

Letters:

A, C, E, G → advance by 2 positions.

Numbers:

1, 3, 5, 7 → increase by 2.

Next letter = I

Next number = 9

**Answer: I9**

## B. Worked Example 2

**Series:** B2, D4, G7, K11, ?

Letter pattern:

- B → D = +2
    
- D → G = +3
    
- G → K = +4
    
- Next shift = +5, giving P.
    

Number pattern:

- 2 → 4 = +2
    
- 4 → 7 = +3
    
- 7 → 11 = +4
    
- Next shift = +5, giving 16.
    

**Answer: P16**

## C. How to Solve Alphanumeric Series

1. Track the letters separately.
    
2. Track the numbers separately.
    
3. Look for a common rule or separate rules.
    
4. Check whether the question asks for a missing term, next term or position.
    

# 8. Missing Number and Missing Term

A missing term appears in a sequence, whereas a missing number may appear inside a box, triangle, circle or table.

## A. Missing Term

**Series:** 6, 11, 18, 27, ?, 51

Calculate the differences:

+5, +7, +9, +11, +13

The differences increase by 2 each time.

Missing term = 27 + 11 = 38

**Answer: 38**

## B. Missing Number in a Table

Consider the following pattern:

|First Number|Second Number|Third Number|
|---|---|---|
|2|3|5|
|4|6|10|
|7|8|?|

Rule: Third number = First number + Second number.

- 2 + 3 = 5
    
- 4 + 6 = 10
    
- 7 + 8 = 15
    

**Answer: 15**

## C. Common Rules for Diagram-Based Questions

Try these rules first:

- Addition or subtraction.
    
- Multiplication or division.
    
- Squares or cubes.
    
- Row-wise relationships.
    
- Column-wise relationships.
    
- Relationships between the outer numbers and the central number.
    

**Exam tip:** Test simple relationships before using complicated calculations.

# 9. Wrong Term

In a wrong-term question, one number violates the rule followed by the rest of the series.

## A. Worked Example 1

**Series:** 3, 6, 12, 24, 49, 96

The intended rule is multiplication by 2.

- 3 × 2 = 6
    
- 6 × 2 = 12
    
- 12 × 2 = 24
    
- 24 × 2 = 48
    
- 48 × 2 = 96
    

The fifth term should be 48, not 49.

**Answer: 49 is the wrong term.**

## B. Worked Example 2

**Series:** 2, 5, 10, 17, 26, 38

The intended rule is n² + 1, starting with n = 1.

- 1² + 1 = 2
    
- 2² + 1 = 5
    
- 3² + 1 = 10
    
- 4² + 1 = 17
    
- 5² + 1 = 26
    
- 6² + 1 = 37
    

The final term should be 37, not 38.

**Answer: 38 is the wrong term.**

**Important:** A valid rule should make the entire sequence consistent. Do not identify a wrong term merely because another possible pattern can be imagined.

# 10. Mixed-Pattern Series

Mixed-pattern series combine mathematical operations or use different rules at different stages.

## A. Multiplication Plus Addition

**Series:** 2, 5, 11, 23, 47, ?

Rule: Multiply by 2 and add 1.

47 × 2 + 1 = 95

**Answer: 95**

## B. Increasing Differences

**Series:** 4, 8, 14, 22, 32, ?

Differences:

+4, +6, +8, +10

The next difference is +12.

Next term = 32 + 12 = 44

**Answer: 44**

## C. Prime-Number Series

**Series:** 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, ?

These are consecutive prime numbers.

The next prime number is 17.

**Answer: 17**

## D. Fibonacci-Type Series

**Series:** 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, ?

Rule: Each term is the sum of the previous two terms.

Next term = 5 + 8 = 13

**Answer: 13**

## E. Multiplication by Increasing Integers

**Series:** 2, 4, 12, 48, 240, ?

- 2 × 2 = 4
    
- 4 × 3 = 12
    
- 12 × 4 = 48
    
- 48 × 5 = 240
    

The next multiplier is 6.

Next term = 240 × 6 = 1440

**Answer: 1440**

# 11. Fast-Solving Strategy for DSSSB

## Step 1: Check First Differences

Subtract consecutive terms. Constant differences usually indicate an arithmetic sequence.

## Step 2: Check Ratios

Try multiplication or division if the differences do not reveal a pattern.

## Step 3: Look for Special Numbers

Check for:

- Perfect squares.
    
- Perfect cubes.
    
- Prime numbers.
    
- Fibonacci-type sequences.
    
- Other familiar mathematical patterns.
    

## Step 4: Split Alternating Terms

Separate odd-position and even-position terms to identify interleaved patterns.

## Step 5: Verify the Whole Sequence

A good rule should explain all the given terms, not just the last two.

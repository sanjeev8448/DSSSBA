# Reasoning Ability — Topic 8: Blood Relations (रक्त संबंध)

## 1. Basic Family Relationships (परिवार के मूल संबंध)

Learn these relationships before solving complex family-tree questions.

|Relationship|Meaning|
|---|---|
|Father / Mother|Male / female parent|
|Son / Daughter|Male / female child|
|Brother / Sister|Male / female sibling|
|Husband / Wife|Married partners|
|Grandfather / Grandmother|Parent's father / mother|
|Grandson / Granddaughter|Child's son / daughter|
|Uncle|Parent's brother; sometimes an aunt's husband|
|Aunt|Parent's sister; sometimes an uncle's wife|
|Nephew / Niece|Brother's or sister's son / daughter|
|Cousin|Child of an aunt or uncle|
|Father-in-law / Mother-in-law|Spouse's father / mother|
|Brother-in-law|Husband's or wife's brother, or sister's husband|
|Sister-in-law|Husband's or wife's sister, or brother's wife|

**Important:** In reasoning questions, follow the exact relationship stated. Terms such as uncle and aunt can include relatives by marriage, depending on the question.

## 2. Family Tree (पारिवारिक वृक्ष)

A family tree is a diagram showing how family members are related. It is useful for solving long statements involving multiple people.

### Example Family Structure

A grandfather and grandmother have two children: a father and an uncle. The father has a son and a daughter.

Representing the family in a simple tree helps identify parents, siblings, and cousins without repeatedly rereading the statement.

### Solved Example 1

Pointing to a boy, Rahul says, “He is the son of my father's only daughter.” How is the boy related to Rahul?

**Step 1:** Rahul's father's daughter is Rahul's sister, assuming Rahul is male and the daughter is another child.

**Step 2:** The sister's son is Rahul's nephew.

**Answer: Nephew.**

### Solved Example 2

A woman says, “The man in the photograph is the father of my mother's only son.” How is the man related to the woman?

**Step 1:** The woman's mother's only son is her brother, assuming the woman is not that son.

**Step 2:** The brother's father is also the woman's father, assuming the stated family structure.

**Answer: Father.**

## 3. Maternal and Paternal Relations (मातृ पक्ष और पितृ पक्ष)

These terms are frequently used in family-tree questions.

- **Maternal:** Related through the mother.
    
- **Paternal:** Related through the father.
    

|Relationship|Meaning|
|---|---|
|Maternal grandfather|Mother's father|
|Maternal grandmother|Mother's mother|
|Maternal uncle|Mother's brother|
|Maternal aunt|Mother's sister|
|Paternal grandfather|Father's father|
|Paternal grandmother|Father's mother|
|Paternal uncle|Father's brother|
|Paternal aunt|Father's sister|

### Solved Examples

**Example 1**

Question: Your mother's brother is related to you as your?

**Answer:** Maternal uncle.

**Reason:** The relationship is through your mother.

**Example 2**

Question: Your father's mother is your?

**Answer:** Paternal grandmother.

**Example 3**

Question: A woman says, “The girl is the daughter of my father's son.” How is the girl related to the woman?

**Answer:** Niece, assuming the woman's father's son is her brother.

**Note:** In standard exam questions, use the explicitly stated relationships and genders. If the family structure permits more than one interpretation, check whether the question provides enough information to identify a unique relationship.

## 4. Coded Relations (कूटबद्ध रक्त संबंध)

In coded blood-relation questions, symbols represent relationships. First decode each symbol, then follow the chain in the correct order.

Suppose a question defines:

|Symbol|Meaning|
|---|---|
|A + B|A is the father of B|
|A − B|A is the sister of B|
|A × B|A is the brother of B|
|A ÷ B|A is the mother of B|

These definitions are only for this example. Always use the definitions provided in the actual question.

### Solved Example 1

What does P + Q − R tell us?

**Step 1:** P + Q means P is the father of Q.

**Step 2:** Q − R means Q is the sister of R.

Therefore, P is the father of R as well, assuming Q and R share the same father.

**Answer:** P is R's father under the usual shared-parent assumption.

### Solved Example 2

Given:

- A ÷ B means A is the mother of B.
    
- B × C means B is the brother of C.
    

What is A's relationship to C?

**Step 1:** A is B's mother.

**Step 2:** B is C's brother, so B and C are siblings.

**Answer:** A is C's mother, assuming B and C share the same mother.

**Exam tip:** Decode one symbol at a time. Do not assume that +, −, × or ÷ has its usual arithmetic meaning.

## 5. Generation-Based Questions (पीढ़ी आधारित प्रश्न)

These questions involve grandparents, parents, children, grandchildren, and several generations.

### Generation Map

|Relative Generation|Common Relationships|
|---|---|
|Two generations above you|Great-grandparents|
|One generation above you|Parents and their siblings|
|Your generation|You and your siblings|
|One generation below you|Children, nephews and nieces|
|Two generations below you|Grandchildren|

This is a simplified map relative to one person. The exact relationship also depends on the family branch.

### Solved Example 1

A is the father of B. B is the mother of C. How is A related to C?

- A is B's father.
    
- B is C's mother.
    
- Therefore, A is C's maternal grandfather.
    

**Answer: Maternal grandfather.**

### Solved Example 2

P is the son of Q. Q is the daughter of R. How is P related to R?

- Q is R's daughter.
    
- P is Q's son.
    
- Therefore, P is R's grandson.
    

**Answer: Grandson.**

### Solved Example 3

A is the brother of B. B is the father of C. How is A related to C?

- B is C's father.
    
- A is B's brother.
    
- Therefore, A is C's uncle.
    

**Answer: Paternal uncle**, assuming A is male and B is C's father.

## 6. Quick-Solving Strategy (तेजी से हल करने की रणनीति)

1. **Identify gender:** Note the gender of every person wherever it is stated.
    
2. **Trace the relationship:** Start with the person mentioned at the end of the sentence and work backwards if that makes the chain easier.
    
3. **Draw a family tree:** Use a diagram for questions with three or more relationships.
    
4. **Separate maternal and paternal branches:** Maternal refers to the mother's side; paternal refers to the father's side.
    
5. **Decode symbols carefully:** Follow the exact definitions given in coded-relation questions.
    
6. **Count generations:** For generation questions, count parent-child steps before naming the relationship.
    
7. **Avoid unsupported assumptions:** Do not assume two siblings share both parents unless the question or standard exam convention supports it.

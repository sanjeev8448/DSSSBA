
# 1. Number Classification

In number classification, identify the mathematical property shared by most of the numbers.

## Common Patterns to Check

- Odd and even numbers.
    
- Prime and composite numbers.
    
- Perfect squares and cubes.
    
- Divisibility by 2, 3, 5, 7 and other numbers.
    
- Multiples of a number.
    
- Sum of digits.
    
- Number patterns and arithmetic relationships.
    

## Solved Examples

**Example 1: Find the odd one out.**

9, 16, 25, 36, 48

Analysis:

- 9 = 3²
    
- 16 = 4²
    
- 25 = 5²
    
- 36 = 6²
    
- 48 is not a perfect square.
    

**Answer: 48**

**Example 2: Find the odd one out.**

11, 13, 17, 21

Analysis:

- 11, 13 and 17 are prime numbers.
    
- 21 = 3 × 7, so it is composite.
    

**Answer: 21**

**Example 3: Find the odd one out.**

15, 21, 27, 32

Analysis:

- 15: 1 + 5 = 6
    
- 21: 2 + 1 = 3
    
- 27: 2 + 7 = 9
    
- 32: 3 + 2 = 5
    

The digit sums of 15, 21 and 27 are divisible by 3, whereas the digit sum of 32 is not.

**Answer: 32**

**Example 4: Find the odd one out.**

8, 27, 64, 100, 125

Analysis:

- 8 = 2³
    
- 27 = 3³
    
- 64 = 4³
    
- 125 = 5³
    
- 100 is not a perfect cube.
    

**Answer: 100**

**Exam tip:** Check squares, cubes, primes, parity and divisibility before trying complicated operations.

# 2. Alphabet Classification

Alphabet classification uses alphabetical positions, forward and backward sequences, vowels, consonants or relationships between letters.

Remember: A = 1, B = 2, ..., Z = 26.

## Solved Examples

**Example 1: Find the odd one out.**

ACE, BDF, CEG, DHL

Analysis:

- ACE: Positions 1, 3, 5 — gaps of +2.
    
- BDF: Positions 2, 4, 6 — gaps of +2.
    
- CEG: Positions 3, 5, 7 — gaps of +2.
    
- DHL: Positions 4, 8, 12 — gaps of +4.
    

The first three have consecutive letters separated by two alphabet positions. DHL does not follow this pattern.

**Answer: DHL**

**Example 2: Find the odd one out.**

A, E, I, B

Analysis:

- A, E and I are vowels.
    
- B is a consonant.
    

**Answer: B**

**Example 3: Find the odd one out.**

AZ, BY, CX, DU, EW

Analysis:

The first letters advance in alphabetical order:

A, B, C, D, E

The second letters should move backward:

Z, Y, X, W, V

The correct pairs would be AZ, BY, CX, DW and EV. The pairs DU and EW break the reverse-alphabet relationship.

**Answer: The question contains two incorrect pairs, DU and EW.** Therefore, as written, it does not have a unique odd-one-out answer under the stated pattern.

**Exam tip:** Check letter positions, gaps, vowels, consonants and reverse-alphabet relationships.

# 3. Word Classification

Word classification relies on meaning, category, function, spelling or the relationship between words.

## Common Categories

- Animals, birds, fruits, vegetables and flowers.
    
- Professions and workplaces.
    
- Tools and their uses.
    
- Synonyms and antonyms.
    
- Parts and wholes.
    
- Living and non-living things.
    
- Spelling and grammatical properties.
    

## Solved Examples

**Example 1: Find the odd one out.**

Apple, Mango, Potato, Banana

Analysis:

Apple, mango and banana are commonly classified as fruits, while potato is commonly classified as a vegetable in everyday food usage.

**Answer: Potato**

**Example 2: Find the odd one out.**

Doctor, Teacher, Engineer, Hospital

Analysis:

Doctor, teacher and engineer are professions. Hospital is an institution or workplace.

**Answer: Hospital**

**Example 3: Find the odd one out.**

Pen, Pencil, Eraser, Chair

Analysis:

Pen, pencil and eraser are stationery items. A chair is furniture.

**Answer: Chair**

**Example 4: Find the odd one out.**

Rose, Lotus, Lily, Mango

Analysis:

Rose, lotus and lily are flowers. Mango is a fruit.

**Answer: Mango**

**Important:** Use the category intended by the question. For example, potato is botanically a plant tuber, while in everyday food classification it is commonly grouped as a vegetable.

# 4. Figure Classification

Figure classification uses shapes, diagrams, symbols or visual patterns. One figure differs from the others in a specific property.

## Common Properties to Check

- Number of sides or corners.
    
- Open versus closed figures.
    
- Number of shapes inside a figure.
    
- Shading or filled regions.
    
- Rotation or reflection.
    
- Direction of arrows.
    
- Number of lines, dots or intersections.
    
- Symmetry.
    
- Shape combinations.
    

## Solved Examples

**Example 1: Shape classification**

Triangle, Square, Pentagon, Circle

Analysis:

- Triangle, square and pentagon have straight sides and corners.
    
- A circle has neither straight sides nor corners.
    

**Answer: Circle**

**Example 2: Open versus closed figures**

Suppose four figures are shown, of which three are closed polygons and one is an open, incomplete polygon.

Analysis:

The three closed figures have completely enclosed boundaries. The open figure does not.

**Answer: The open figure**

**Example 3: Count the elements**

Suppose four similar boxes contain different numbers of dots.

Method:

1. Count the dots in each figure.
    
2. Compare the counts.
    
3. Identify the figure that does not follow the common count or numerical pattern.
    

**Answer:** Depends on the actual figures and their dot counts.

**Exam tip:** Figures may be rotated, reflected, shaded or combined. Do not assume that a rotated shape is different unless orientation is part of the rule.

# 5. Logical Classification

Logical classification asks you to identify an item that does not fit a group based on a conceptual or mathematical relationship.

## Common Categories

- Objects grouped by purpose or function.
    
- Animals grouped by habitat or biological category.
    
- Units grouped by measurement type.
    
- Numbers grouped by mathematical properties.
    
- Items grouped by cause, effect or relationship.
    

## Solved Examples

**Example 1: Measurement**

Metre, Kilometre, Centimetre, Kilogram

Analysis:

- Metre measures length.
    
- Kilometre measures length.
    
- Centimetre measures length.
    
- Kilogram measures mass.
    

**Answer: Kilogram**

**Example 2: Animal category**

Lion, Tiger, Leopard, Cow

Analysis:

Lion, tiger and leopard are wild cats. Cow is a domesticated bovine animal.

**Answer: Cow**

**Example 3: Technology**

Keyboard, Mouse, Scanner, Monitor

Analysis:

- Keyboard is an input device.
    
- Mouse is an input device.
    
- Scanner is an input device.
    
- Monitor is an output device.
    

**Answer: Monitor**

# 6. Systematic Method for Solving Classification Questions

Use this order to avoid overthinking.

## Step 1: Identify the Category

Determine whether the options contain numbers, letters, words or figures.

## Step 2: Test Simple Rules First

- Numbers: Odd/even, prime/square, divisibility.
    
- Letters: Alphabet positions, vowels, consonants.
    
- Words: Meaning, category, profession or function.
    
- Figures: Sides, shading, symmetry or orientation.
    

## Step 3: Compare All Options

A valid rule should explain why three items belong together and why one does not.

## Step 4: Check Alternative Explanations

Make sure another equally strong rule does not contradict your answer.

## Step 5: Choose the Clearest Rule

Avoid unnecessarily complicated explanations when a simple common property works.

# Quick Revision Table

|Classification Type|First Thing to Check|
|---|---|
|Number Classification|Prime numbers, squares, cubes, parity and divisibility|
|Alphabet Classification|Letter positions, gaps, vowels and reverse alphabet|
|Word Classification|Meaning, category, profession and function|
|Figure Classification|Sides, corners, shading, symmetry and orientation|
|Logical Classification|Common purpose, measurement, category or relationship|

**Golden rule for DSSSB:** Identify the property shared by the majority of options, then verify that the remaining option genuinely breaks that rule. If two options break the same rule, the question may be ambiguous or incorrectly constructed.

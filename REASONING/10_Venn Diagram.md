# Reasoning Ability — Topic 10: Venn Diagrams (वेन आरेख)

## 1. Two-Set Venn Diagrams (दो समुच्चयों के वेन आरेख)

A two-set Venn diagram represents the relationship between two categories, A and B.

There are three common cases.

### Case 1: Partial Overlap

Some members belong to both groups, while other members may belong to only one group.

**Example:** Students and athletes.

Some students may be athletes, while others are not. Some athletes may also be non-students.

### Case 2: One Set Inside Another

Every member of A belongs to B.

**Example:** Squares and rectangles.

Every square is a rectangle, but not every rectangle is a square.

### Case 3: No Overlap

The two groups have no members in common.

**Example:** Odd integers and even integers.

An integer cannot be both odd and even.

### Solved Examples

**Example 1: Doctors and Women**

The groups can partially overlap: some doctors are women, and some women are not doctors. Some doctors may also not be women.

**Answer:** Partially overlapping sets.

**Example 2: Mangoes and Fruits**

Every mango is a fruit.

**Answer:** The mango set lies entirely inside the fruit set.

**Example 3: Odd Numbers and Even Numbers**

No integer can be both odd and even.

**Answer:** Two separate sets.

## 2. Three-Set Venn Diagrams (तीन समुच्चयों के वेन आरेख)

A three-set Venn diagram represents three categories, usually with three overlapping circles. It can show members belonging to one, two, or all three groups.

### Understanding the Regions

Suppose:

- A = People who play cricket
    
- B = People who play football
    
- C = People who play badminton
    

The diagram can represent the following regions:

|Region|Meaning|
|---|---|
|Only A|People who play cricket but neither football nor badminton|
|A and B only|People who play cricket and football, but not badminton|
|A and C only|People who play cricket and badminton, but not football|
|B and C only|People who play football and badminton, but not cricket|
|A, B and C|People who play all three sports|
|Outside all circles|People who play none of the three sports|

The central intersection represents members common to all three sets.

### Solved Example 1

In a class, some students study Mathematics, some study Science, and some study English. A student studies all three subjects.

Where should the student be placed?

**Answer:** In the central intersection of all three circles.

### Solved Example 2

A person likes both cricket and football but does not like badminton.

Where should the person be placed?

**Answer:** In the overlap of the cricket and football circles, but outside the badminton circle.

### Counting with Two Sets

Suppose a survey records:

- 20 people like tea.
    
- 15 people like coffee.
    
- 8 people like both.
    

Find the number of people who like tea or coffee.

**Formula:**

n(T ∪ C) = n(T) + n(C) − n(T ∩ C)

**Solution:**

n(T ∪ C) = 20 + 15 − 8

n(T ∪ C) = 27

**Answer: 27 people.**

We subtract the 8 people who like both because they were counted twice.

### Inclusion–Exclusion Formula for Three Sets

Use this formula when the question asks for the total number belonging to at least one of three groups.

n(A ∪ B ∪ C) = n(A) + n(B) + n(C) − n(A ∩ B) − n(B ∩ C) − n(A ∩ C) + n(A ∩ B ∩ C)

**Remember the pattern:**

1. Add the sizes of all three sets.
    
2. Subtract the three pairwise intersections.
    
3. Add the common intersection of all three sets back once.

## 3. Classification Using Venn Diagrams (वर्गीकरण)

Classification questions ask you to choose the diagram that best represents the relationship between the given words or categories.

### Common Examples

**Example 1: Roses, Flowers and Plants**

Roses are flowers, and flowers are plants.

**Relationship:** Three nested sets.

Roses ⊆ Flowers ⊆ Plants

**Answer:** Roses lie inside flowers, which lie inside plants.

**Example 2: Men, Teachers and Women**

Under the usual binary classification used in basic aptitude questions:

- Men and women are separate groups.
    
- Teachers can overlap with either group.
    
- Some teachers may be men, while others may be women.
    

**Answer:** The teacher circle intersects both the men and women regions, while the men and women sets do not intersect.

**Example 3: Cars, Buses and Trains**

When these are treated as distinct vehicle categories, the sets are separate.

**Answer:** Three non-overlapping circles.

### How to Choose the Correct Diagram

1. Identify whether any category is a subset of another.
    
2. Check whether the categories can share members.
    
3. Determine whether all three categories overlap, only two overlap, or none overlap.
    
4. Check whether any category lies completely outside the others.
    
5. Select the diagram that matches the relationship, not just the apparent similarity of the words.
    

**Important:** Real-world categories can sometimes overlap in unexpected ways. Use the definitions and assumptions provided by the question.

## 4. Logical Relationships

These questions test how categories are connected logically, even when there are no numerical values.

### Relationship Type A: Subset

**Statements:**

- All parrots are birds.
    
- All birds are animals.
    

**Relationship:**

Parrots ⊆ Birds ⊆ Animals

**Answer:** Three nested circles, with parrots inside birds and birds inside animals.

### Relationship Type B: Partial Overlap

**Statements:**

- Some students are singers.
    
- Some students are dancers.
    

The students, singers and dancers may overlap in several ways. Some students may be both singers and dancers, but the statements do not guarantee this.

**Answer:** Use overlapping sets only to the extent permitted by the statements. Do not assume every singer is a student or every dancer is a singer.

### Relationship Type C: Separate Sets

**Statement:** No square is a circle.

**Answer:** Two separate circles for squares and circles.

### Relationship Type D: One Category Overlaps Two Others

**Example:** Teachers, women and men.

Under the usual binary classification used in basic aptitude questions, men and women are separate, while teachers may overlap with both groups.

**Answer:** A three-set diagram in which the teacher circle intersects both the men and women regions, while the men and women circles remain separate.

### Important Symbols

|Symbol|Meaning|
|---|---|
|A ⊆ B|Every member of A belongs to B|
|A ∩ B|Members common to A and B|
|A ∪ B|Members belonging to A, B, or both|
|A ∩ B = ∅|A and B have no members in common|
|n(A)|Number of members in set A|

## 5. Quick-Solving Strategy

- **Two-set questions:** Check subset, overlap, or separation.
    
- **Three-set questions:** Look for the central intersection and the pairwise overlaps.
    
- **Classification questions:** Translate the words into category relationships before selecting a diagram.
    
- **Counting questions:** Subtract overlaps that have been counted twice.
    
- **Logical questions:** Distinguish between “all,” “some,” and “none.”
    
- **Possibility questions:** Check whether the proposed relationship is compatible with the given information.
    

## 6. Final Revision Table

|Relationship|Diagram Pattern|
|---|---|
|All A are B|A lies inside B|
|No A is B|Separate circles|
|Some A are B|Overlapping circles|
|A, B and C are all related through common members|Three circles with a central intersection, if supported by the question|
|Roses, flowers and plants|Three nested circles|
|Men, teachers and women|Teachers may overlap with both separate groups|
|Cars, buses and trains|Separate circles when treated as distinct categories|

**Exam reminder:** First identify the logical relationship between the categories, then select or draw the Venn diagram. Do not assume that categories overlap or contain one another unless the question supports that relationship.

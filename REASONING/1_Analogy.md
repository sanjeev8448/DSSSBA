# GENERAL INTELLIGENCE & REASONING — TOPIC 1: ANALOGY (सादृश्य)

Analogy questions test your ability to identify a relationship between two things and apply the same relationship to another pair.

**Example:**

Book : Reading :: Fork : ?

A book is used for reading, and a fork is used for eating.

**Answer: Eating**

For DSSSB preparation, study the following seven types of analogy questions, from basic relationships to mixed-operation problems.

## 1. Number Analogy

In number analogy, the relationship between the first two numbers must also hold between the next pair.

### A. Common Number Patterns

|Pattern|Example|Relationship|
|---|---|---|
|Addition|7 : 12 :: 9 : 14|Add 5|
|Subtraction|18 : 11 :: 15 : 8|Subtract 7|
|Multiplication|6 : 24 :: 8 : 32|Multiply by 4|
|Division|42 : 7 :: 54 : 9|Divide by 6|
|Square|5 : 25 :: 8 : 64|Square the number|
|Cube|3 : 27 :: 4 : 64|Cube the number|
|Double and add|4 : 9 :: 7 : 15|Multiply by 2, then add 1|
|Mixed operation|3 : 10 :: 5 : 26|Square the number, then add 1|

### B. Worked Examples

**Example 1: 8 : 64 :: 11 : ?**

- 8² = 64
    
- 11² = 121
    

**Answer: 121**

**Example 2: 4 : 20 :: 6 : ?**

Rule: n² + n

- 4² + 4 = 20
    
- 6² + 6 = 42
    

**Answer: 42**

**Example 3: 7 : 50 :: 9 : ?**

Rule: n² + 1

- 7² + 1 = 50
    
- 9² + 1 = 82
    

**Answer: 82**

### C. Exam Strategy

Test simple operations first. If they do not work, check:

1. Squares and cubes.
    
2. Multiplication followed by addition or subtraction.
    
3. Sum or product of digits.
    
4. Relationships between individual digits.
    

**Important:** Verify that the rule works for the complete given pair before applying it to the next number. Avoid complicated rules when a simpler rule explains the relationship.

## 2. Alphabet Analogy

Alphabet analogy questions test relationships between letters using alphabetical positions, letter shifts, reverse alphabets and word patterns.

### A. Alphabet Positions

|Letter|Position|Letter|Position|
|---|---|---|---|
|A|1|N|14|
|B|2|O|15|
|C|3|P|16|
|D|4|Q|17|
|E|5|R|18|
|F|6|S|19|
|G|7|T|20|
|H|8|U|21|
|I|9|V|22|
|J|10|W|23|
|K|11|X|24|
|L|12|Y|25|
|M|13|Z|26|

Remember: A = 1, B = 2, C = 3, ..., Z = 26.

### B. Common Alphabet Patterns

- **Forward movement:** A → D means moving forward by 3 positions.
    
- **Backward movement:** M → J means moving backward by 3 positions.
    
- **Reverse alphabet:** A ↔ Z, B ↔ Y, C ↔ X.
    
- **Word shifting:** Every letter moves by the same number of positions.
    
- **Alternating shifts:** Letters in odd and even positions follow different rules.
    

### C. Worked Examples

**Example 1: C : F :: H : ?**

C → F means moving forward by 3 positions.

H → K also means moving forward by 3 positions.

**Answer: K**

**Example 2: AZ : BY :: CX : ?**

- First letters: A → B → C, moving forward by one position.
    
- Second letters: Z → Y → X, moving backward by one position.
    

The next pair follows the same pattern.

**Answer: DW**

**Example 3: ACE : BDF :: MOQ : ?**

Each letter advances by one position:

- M → N
    
- O → P
    
- Q → R
    

**Answer: NPR**

### D. Exam Strategy

For alphabet analogy questions:

1. Write down the alphabetical positions if needed.
    
2. Check forward and backward shifts.
    
3. Check reverse-alphabet relationships.
    
4. Look for different patterns in odd and even positions.
    
5. Verify the pattern for every letter.
    

## 3. Word Analogy

Word analogy tests relationships based on meaning, purpose, category, function or association.

### A. Common Word Relationships

|Relationship|Example|Explanation|
|---|---|---|
|Object and function|Pen : Writing|A pen is used for writing|
|Worker and workplace|Doctor : Hospital|A doctor commonly works in a hospital|
|Animal and young one|Cow : Calf|A calf is a young cow|
|Country and capital|India : New Delhi|New Delhi is India's capital|
|Part and whole|Wheel : Car|A wheel is a part of a car|
|Synonyms|Happy : Joyful|Similar meanings|
|Antonyms|Victory : Defeat|Opposite meanings|
|Material and product|Wood : Furniture|Furniture can be made from wood|
|Tool and user|Stethoscope : Doctor|A doctor commonly uses a stethoscope|
|Cause and effect|Disease : Illness|A disease can cause illness|

### B. Worked Examples

**Example 1: Bird : Nest :: Bee : ?**

A bird lives in a nest, and a bee lives in a hive.

**Answer: Hive**

**Example 2: Eye : Seeing :: Ear : ?**

The eye is associated with seeing, and the ear is associated with hearing.

**Answer: Hearing**

**Example 3: Author : Book :: Composer : ?**

An author writes a book, while a composer creates music.

**Answer: Music**

### C. Exam Strategy

Identify the precise relationship before looking at the answer options.

For example, Doctor : Hospital represents a worker–workplace relationship, not a part–whole relationship.

Ask yourself:

- What is the connection between the first pair?
    
- Is the relationship based on function, category, location, meaning or cause?
    
- Does the same relationship fit the second pair?
    

## 4. Figure Analogy

Figure analogy questions use shapes, symbols, patterns or diagrams instead of words and numbers.

### A. Common Figure Transformations

|Transformation|Meaning|
|---|---|
|Rotation|A shape turns by 90°, 180° or another angle|
|Reflection|A figure is flipped horizontally or vertically|
|Shape Count|The number of lines, dots or sides changes|
|Shading|A filled region becomes unfilled, or vice versa|
|Position|An element moves from one corner or position to another|
|Combination|Two figures combine to form a third|
|Alternation|Shapes, directions or shading alternate according to a rule|

### B. Example: Rotation

Suppose an upward arrow rotates 90° clockwise.

Original figure: ↑

After a 90° clockwise rotation: →

**Answer: →**

### C. How to Solve Figure Analogy Questions

1. Compare the first pair of figures.
    
2. Identify the exact transformation.
    
3. Apply the same transformation to the second figure.
    
4. Check direction, orientation, shading and the number of elements.
    
5. Compare the result with the given options.
    

**Exam tip:** Do not focus only on the overall shape. Small details such as dots, internal lines, shaded regions and arrow directions may determine the answer.

## 5. Semantic Analogy

Semantic analogy focuses on the relationship between concepts and their meanings. It overlaps with word analogy, but the emphasis is on understanding the concept rather than simply recalling vocabulary.

### A. Common Semantic Relationships

|Relationship|Example|
|---|---|
|Profession and activity|Teacher : Teaching|
|Instrument and measurement|Thermometer : Temperature|
|Emotion and expression|Joy : Smile|
|Institution and function|Court : Justice|
|Problem and solution|Hunger : Food|
|Object and property|Ice : Cold|
|Symbol and meaning|Red Traffic Light : Stop|
|Study and subject|Biology : Living Organisms|

### B. Worked Example

**Thermometer : Temperature :: Barometer : ?**

A thermometer measures temperature, while a barometer measures atmospheric pressure.

**Answer: Atmospheric pressure**

### C. Exam Strategy

Identify whether the relationship represents:

- Measurement and quantity.
    
- Profession and activity.
    
- Object and property.
    
- Symbol and meaning.
    
- Problem and solution.
    
- Field of study and subject matter.
    

Choose the option that preserves the same logical connection.

## 6. Classification-Based Analogy

Classification-based questions test your ability to identify a shared property among items or determine which option does not follow the same classification rule.

### A. Category-Based Analogy

**Example: Rose : Flower :: Mango : ?**

A rose belongs to the category flower. A mango belongs to the category fruit.

**Answer: Fruit**

### B. Odd One Out — Number Classification

Find the odd one out:

- A. 16
    
- B. 25
    
- C. 36
    
- D. 48
    

Analysis:

- 16 = 4²
    
- 25 = 5²
    
- 36 = 6²
    
- 48 is not a perfect square.
    

**Answer: D. 48**

### C. Odd One Out — Letter Classification

Find the odd one out:

- A. ACE
    
- B. BDF
    
- C. CEG
    
- D. DHL
    

Analysis:

- ACE: Each successive letter advances by 2 positions.
    
- BDF: Each successive letter advances by 2 positions.
    
- CEG: Each successive letter advances by 2 positions.
    
- DHL: The gaps between successive letters are not equal.
    

**Answer: D. DHL**

### D. Common Classification Rules

Look for a shared property involving:

1. Number patterns.
    
2. Alphabetical gaps.
    
3. Categories and subcategories.
    
4. Spelling patterns.
    
5. Common functions.
    
6. Mathematical properties, such as prime numbers or perfect squares.
    

**Exam tip:** Find the property shared by most options rather than selecting an answer based only on appearance.

## 7. Mixed-Operation Analogy

Mixed-operation analogy questions combine two or more mathematical rules. They may involve squares, multiplication, addition, subtraction, digit sums or relationships between digits.

### A. Worked Examples

**Example 1: 3 : 12 :: 5 : ?**

Rule: n × (n + 1)

- 3 × 4 = 12
    
- 5 × 6 = 30
    

**Answer: 30**

**Example 2: 4 : 17 :: 6 : ?**

Rule: n² + 1

- 4² + 1 = 17
    
- 6² + 1 = 37
    

**Answer: 37**

**Example 3: 23 : 5 :: 41 : ?**

Rule: Add the digits.

- 2 + 3 = 5
    
- 4 + 1 = 5
    

**Answer: 5**

**Example 4: 12 : 144 :: 15 : ?**

Rule: Square the number.

- 12² = 144
    
- 15² = 225
    

**Answer: 225**

### B. How to Identify the Rule Quickly

Try the following operations in order:

1. Addition or subtraction.
    
2. Multiplication or division.
    
3. Square or cube.
    
4. n² + 1 or n² − 1.
    
5. n × (n + 1).
    
6. Sum or product of digits.
    
7. Reverse the digits or compare individual digit positions.
    

Always verify the rule against the complete given pair before applying it to the next number.

## Final Practice Strategy for DSSSB

- **Step 1:** Identify the relationship between the given pair.
    
- **Step 2:** Test the simplest logical rule first.
    
- **Step 3:** Apply the same rule to the next pair.
    
- **Step 4:** Verify that the rule fits all relevant elements.
    
- **Step 5:** Eliminate options that do not follow the pattern.
    

**Most important rule:** Analogy questions are about matching relationships, not merely matching individual numbers, letters or words.

# Reasoning Ability — Topic 9: Syllogism (न्यायवाक्य)

## 1. Understanding All, Some and No

There are four basic statement types you must recognise in syllogism questions.

### Type 1: All A are B

**Meaning:** Every A is inside B.

**Example:** All cats are animals.

This means every cat is an animal, but it does not mean every animal is a cat.

### Type 2: No A is B

**Meaning:** A and B have no members in common.

**Example:** No square is a circle.

### Type 3: Some A are B

**Meaning:** At least one member belongs to both A and B.

**Example:** Some students are athletes.

This means at least one student is an athlete.

### Type 4: Some A are not B

**Meaning:** At least one member of A lies outside B.

**Example:** Some students are not athletes.

### Quick Revision Table

|Statement|Meaning|
|---|---|
|All A are B|Every A belongs to B|
|No A is B|A and B do not overlap|
|Some A are B|At least one A is also B|
|Some A are not B|At least one A is outside B|

## 2. Venn Diagram Method

A Venn diagram represents categories as circles. Their placement helps you test whether a conclusion must be true.

### Solved Example 1

**Statements:**

1. All dogs are animals.
    
2. All animals are living beings.
    

**Conclusions:**

I. All dogs are living beings.

II. All living beings are dogs.

**Answer:** Only conclusion I follows.

**Explanation:** Dogs are inside the category of animals, and animals are inside the category of living beings. Therefore, all dogs must be living beings. The reverse conclusion does not follow.

### Solved Example 2

**Statements:**

1. No pen is a pencil.
    
2. Some pencils are wooden.
    

**Conclusions:**

I. Some wooden things are pencils.

II. Some pens are wooden.

**Answer:** Only conclusion I follows.

**Explanation:**

- “Some pencils are wooden” can be restated as “Some wooden things are pencils.”
    
- Nothing establishes that pens are wooden.
    

### Solved Example 3

**Statements:**

1. Some teachers are writers.
    
2. All writers are educated.
    

**Conclusions:**

I. Some teachers are educated.

II. All teachers are educated.

**Answer:** Only conclusion I follows.

**Explanation:** The teachers who are writers must be educated. However, the statements do not establish that all teachers are educated.

## 3. Possibility Cases (संभावना आधारित प्रश्न)

Possibility questions ask whether a conclusion _could be true_ without contradicting the statements. This differs from asking whether it must be true.

### Example 1: A Valid Possibility

**Statements:**

- All roses are flowers.
    
- Some flowers are red.
    

**Conclusion:** Some roses being red is a possibility.

**Answer:** Possible.

**Explanation:** The statements do not prevent roses from being red. However, they do not guarantee that any rose is red.

### Example 2: An Impossible Possibility

**Statement:** No teacher is a student.

**Conclusion:** Some teachers being students is a possibility.

**Answer:** Not possible.

**Explanation:** The conclusion directly contradicts the statement that the two groups do not overlap.

### Example 3: Nested Categories

**Statements:**

- All A are B.
    
- All B are C.
    

**Conclusion:** Some A being C is a possibility.

**Answer:** Possible.

**Explanation:** A lies within B, which lies within C. However, under strict modern logic, these statements alone do not guarantee that A exists.

### Possibility Rules to Remember

|Given Statements|Possibility Conclusion|Result|
|---|---|---|
|All A are B|Some A may be B|Possible if A can exist|
|No A is B|Some A may be B|Impossible|
|All A are B|Some A may be outside B|Impossible|
|Some A are B|Some A may be outside B|Possible|
|Some A are not B|All A are B|Impossible if both are interpreted as definite statements about the same categories|

**Important:** In possibility questions, a conclusion does not have to be guaranteed. It only needs to be compatible with all the statements.

## 4. Conclusion Checking Rules

Use these rules to decide whether a conclusion follows from the given statements.

|Statement|What Follows?|
|---|---|
|All A are B|All A are B; no automatic reverse|
|No A is B|No B is A|
|Some A are B|Some B are A|
|Some A are not B|Some members of A are outside B|
|All A are B and All B are C|All A are C|
|Some A are B and All B are C|Some A are C|
|No A is B and All C are A|No C is B|

### Solved Example 1

**Statements:**

1. All apples are fruits.
    
2. Some fruits are sweet.
    

**Conclusions:**

I. All apples are sweet.

II. Some sweet things are fruits.

**Answer:** Only conclusion II follows.

**Explanation:** The second statement explicitly establishes that some fruits are sweet. It does not establish that all apples are sweet.

### Solved Example 2

**Statements:**

1. All doctors are graduates.
    
2. No graduate is illiterate.
    

**Conclusions:**

I. No doctor is illiterate.

II. Some graduates are doctors.

**Answer:** Conclusion I follows. Conclusion II is not guaranteed under strict modern logic.

**Explanation:**

- Every doctor belongs to the graduate group.
    
- No graduate is illiterate.
    
- Therefore, no doctor is illiterate.
    
- The statements do not explicitly establish that any doctors exist.
    

**Exam note:** In traditional aptitude questions, the expected convention about existence can vary. Follow the instructions and conventions used in your specific test.

## 5. Common Syllogism Mistakes

1. **Reversing an “All” statement:** All A are B does not mean all B are A.
    
2. **Overgeneralising “Some”:** Some A are B does not mean all A are B.
    
3. **Confusing possibility with certainty:** A possible conclusion need not be definitely true.
    
4. **Using outside knowledge:** If a question says all A are B, treat it as a logical rule, even if the category sounds unusual.
    
5. **Assuming existence:** Under strict logic, “All A are B” does not necessarily establish that any A exists.
    
6. **Ignoring the direction of inclusion:** If all A are B, A is the smaller or equal category within B.
    
7. **Treating unrelated categories as connected:** A relationship between A and B does not automatically establish a relationship with C unless the statements support it.
    

---

## 6. Fast-Solving Strategy for the Exam

1. Identify the type of each statement: All, No, Some, or Some not.
    
2. Draw a Venn diagram to represent the relationships.
    
3. Test each conclusion separately.
    
4. Accept a definite conclusion only if it must follow from the statements.
    
5. For possibility questions, check whether the conclusion can be true without contradiction.
    
6. Do not reverse universal statements automatically.
    
7. Check whether the question follows strict modern logic or a stated traditional exam convention about existence.
    

### Final Revision Table

|Statement Pattern|Key Rule|
|---|---|
|All A are B|A is contained within B|
|No A is B|A and B are separate|
|Some A are B|A and B overlap|
|Some A are not B|At least one A lies outside B|
|All A are B; All B are C|All A are C|
|Some A are B; All B are C|Some A are C|
|No A is B|No B is A|
|All A are B|The reverse does not automatically follow|

**Exam reminder:** In syllogism questions, use only the given statements. A conclusion must be logically supported, not merely realistic or likely.

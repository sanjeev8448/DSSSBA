
## 1. North, South, East and West (उत्तर, दक्षिण, पूर्व और पश्चिम)

In a standard direction diagram:

- **North (N):** Upward
    
- **South (S):** Downward
    
- **East (E):** Right
    
- **West (W):** Left
    

The four intermediate directions are:

- **NE:** Northeast (उत्तर-पूर्व)
    
- **NW:** Northwest (उत्तर-पश्चिम)
    
- **SE:** Southeast (दक्षिण-पूर्व)
    
- **SW:** Southwest (दक्षिण-पश्चिम)
    

### Example

A person walks 5 km north and then 3 km east.

The person is now **northeast (NE)** of the starting point.

**Exam trick:** Do not confuse the last direction travelled with the person's overall direction from the starting point.

## 2. Turns: Left and Right (बाएँ और दाएँ मुड़ना)

A turn depends on the direction a person is currently facing.

### Direction and Turn Table

|Facing Direction|Turn Left|Turn Right|
|---|---|---|
|North|West|East|
|East|North|South|
|South|East|West|
|West|South|North|

### Shortcut

- A 90° right turn moves clockwise.
    
- A 90° left turn moves anticlockwise.
    
- A 180° turn means turning around and facing the opposite direction.
    

### Solved Example 1

A man faces North. He turns right, then right again, and then left. Find his final direction.

**Step-by-step:**

1. Start: North
    
2. First right: East
    
3. Second right: South
    
4. Then left: East
    

**Answer: East.**

### Solved Example 2

A person faces South and turns left. Which direction is the person facing?

South → Left → East

**Answer: East.**

### Quick Revision

- North → Right → East
    
- East → Right → South
    
- South → Right → West
    
- West → Right → North
    

For a left turn, follow the reverse sequence.

## 3. Shortest Distance (न्यूनतम दूरी)

Shortest-distance questions ask how far a person is from the starting point after moving in different directions.

When the net horizontal and vertical movements are perpendicular, use the Pythagorean theorem:

**Formula:**

d = √(x² + y²)

Where:

- d = shortest distance from the starting point
    
- x = net east–west displacement
    
- y = net north–south displacement
    

### Solved Example 1

A person walks 3 km north and then 4 km east. Find the shortest distance from the starting point.

**Solution:**

d = √(3² + 4²)

d = √(9 + 16)

d = √25 = 5 km

**Answer: 5 km.**

### Solved Example 2

A person walks 6 km north, 8 km east, and then 6 km south. Find the final position.

**Solution:**

- North–south movement = 6 − 6 = 0 km
    
- East–west movement = 8 km east
    

**Answer:** 8 km east of the starting point.

**Important:** Total distance travelled = 6 + 8 + 6 = 20 km, but the shortest distance from the starting point is only 8 km.

### Solved Example 3

A person walks 10 m west and then 10 m north. Find the shortest distance and direction.

**Solution:**

d = √(10² + 10²)

d = √200

d = 10√2 ≈ 14.14 m

**Answer:** 10√2 m, approximately 14.14 m northwest of the starting point.

### Useful Pythagorean Triples

Memorise these combinations for quick calculations.

|Perpendicular Movements|Shortest Distance|
|---|---|
|3 and 4|5|
|5 and 12|13|
|8 and 15|17|
|6 and 8|10|
|9 and 12|15|

**Exam trick:** First calculate the net movement in each direction. Then apply the formula if the movements are perpendicular.

## 4. Relative Position (सापेक्ष स्थिति)

These questions ask where one person or object is located in relation to another.

For example, if A is north of B, then B is south of A.

### Solved Example 1

Rahul walks 5 m east. Amit walks 5 m north from Rahul's starting point. Where is Amit relative to Rahul?

**Solution:**

- Rahul is 5 m east of the starting point.
    
- Amit is 5 m north of the starting point.
    
- From Rahul to Amit, move west and north.
    

**Answer:** Amit is northwest of Rahul.

### Solved Example 2

P is 4 m north of Q. R is 3 m east of Q. Where is P relative to R?

**Solution using coordinates:**

- Q = (0, 0)
    
- P = (0, 4)
    
- R = (3, 0)
    

From R to P, move 3 m west and 4 m north.

Shortest distance = √(3² + 4²) = 5 m.

**Answer:** P is northwest of R, at a shortest distance of 5 m.

### Solved Example 3

A is east of B. C is south of A. Where is C relative to B?

**Solution:**

- B is the reference point.
    
- A is east of B.
    
- C is south of A.
    

Therefore, C is southeast of B.

**Answer: Southeast (SE).**

### Shortcut: Use Coordinates

Relative-position questions can often be solved more easily by assigning coordinates than by visualising the entire route.

- East: positive horizontal movement
    
- West: negative horizontal movement
    
- North: positive vertical movement
    
- South: negative vertical movement
    

Compare the final coordinates of both people to determine their relative position.

## 5. Shadow-Based Questions (छाया पर आधारित प्रश्न)

Shadow questions use the apparent position of the Sun to infer directions.

For standard reasoning questions, use these simplified assumptions:

- **Morning:** Sun is in the east, so the shadow falls toward the west.
    
- **Evening:** Sun is in the west, so the shadow falls toward the east.
    
- **Noon:** In many simplified Indian exam questions, the Sun is assumed to be toward the south, so the shadow falls toward the north.
    

These are simplified exam conventions. Actual Sun and shadow directions depend on location, date, and time.

### Solved Example 1

A person observes that their shadow is in front of them in the morning. Which direction are they facing?

**Solution:**

1. In the morning, the shadow falls toward the west.
    
2. The shadow is in front of the person.
    
3. Therefore, the person is facing the west.
    

**Answer: West.**

### Solved Example 2

In the evening, a person's shadow falls to their left. Which direction are they facing?

**Solution:**

1. In the evening, the shadow falls toward the east.
    
2. East is on the person's left.
    
3. A person facing north has east on their right.
    
4. A person facing south has east on their left.
    

**Answer: South.**

### Solved Example 3

At the assumed noon position, a person sees their shadow to the north. Which direction is the person facing if the shadow is behind them?

**Solution:**

1. Shadow direction: North.
    
2. The shadow is behind the person.
    
3. Therefore, the person is facing south.
    

**Answer: South.**

### Shadow-Based Question: Quick Revision

|Time|Assumed Sun Direction|Shadow Direction|
|---|---|---|
|Morning|East|West|
|Evening|West|East|
|Noon (simplified exam assumption)|South|North|

**Exam trick:** First determine the direction of the shadow. Then use the words _in front_, _behind_, _left_ or _right_ to determine the person's facing direction.

## 6. Quick Revision Table

|Topic|Rule to Remember|
|---|---|
|Right turn|Clockwise by 90°|
|Left turn|Anticlockwise by 90°|
|Opposite directions|North–South, East–West|
|Shortest distance|Use net displacement, not total path|
|Perpendicular movements|Apply Pythagoras' theorem|
|Relative position|Compare both people from the same reference point|
|Morning shadow|West, under the standard exam assumption|
|Evening shadow|East, under the standard exam assumption|
|Intermediate directions|NE, NW, SE, SW|

## 7. Final Exam Tips

1. Draw a small direction diagram whenever a question involves multiple turns.
    
2. Track the direction the person is currently facing after every turn.
    
3. Keep total distance and shortest distance separate.
    
4. Cancel opposite movements before calculating the final displacement.
    
5. Use Pythagoras' theorem only after finding the net perpendicular movements.
    
6. For relative-position questions, choose one common reference point.
    
7. For shadow questions, establish the shadow's direction before deciding which way the person faces.
    
8. Read the final question carefully: it may ask for the final facing direction, relative position, total distance, or shortest distance.

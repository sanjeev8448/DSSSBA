# GENERAL AWARENESS — INDIAN ECONOMY, BANKING AND INTERNATIONAL ORGANISATIONS

# 1. Indian Economy — Basics

## A. Key Economic Terms

|Term|Meaning|
|---|---|
|Economy|System through which goods and services are produced, distributed and consumed|
|Primary Sector|Agriculture, forestry, fishing and extraction of natural resources|
|Secondary Sector|Manufacturing, construction and processing|
|Tertiary Sector|Services such as banking, transport, education and IT|
|Public Sector|Economic activities owned or controlled by the government|
|Private Sector|Economic activities owned or controlled by private entities|
|Per Capita Income|An income measure divided by population; the exact measure depends on the statistic|
|Fiscal Policy|Government decisions about taxation, spending and borrowing|
|Monetary Policy|Central bank policies affecting interest rates, liquidity and monetary conditions|
|Direct Tax|Tax imposed directly on income or profits, such as income tax|
|Indirect Tax|Tax imposed on transactions or consumption, such as GST|

### Primary, Secondary and Tertiary Sectors

**1. Primary Sector**

- Obtains resources directly from nature.
    
- Examples: Agriculture, fishing, mining and forestry.
    

**2. Secondary Sector**

- Processes raw materials into finished or semi-finished goods.
    
- Includes manufacturing, construction and industrial processing.
    
- Examples: Textile factories, steel production and construction of buildings.
    

**3. Tertiary Sector**

- Provides services rather than directly extracting or manufacturing goods.
    
- Examples: Banking, healthcare, education, transportation, trade and IT services.
    

## B. Other Important Economic Terms

- **Demand:** Quantity of goods or services consumers are willing and able to buy at different prices.
    
- **Supply:** Quantity of goods or services producers are willing and able to sell at different prices.
    
- **Market:** A system in which buyers and sellers exchange goods or services.
    
- **Unemployment:** A situation in which people who are available for and seeking work cannot find work.
    
- **Poverty:** Inability to meet a defined minimum standard of living under the applicable poverty-measurement methodology.
    
- **Subsidy:** Government financial support that reduces costs or assists specified activities or beneficiaries.
    
- **Disinvestment:** Sale of government holdings in a public-sector enterprise.
    
- **Privatisation:** Transfer of ownership or control from the public sector to private ownership or control.
    

### Important Exam Distinction

|Disinvestment|Privatisation|
|---|---|
|Government sells some or all of its stake in a public-sector enterprise.|Ownership or control is transferred to private ownership or control.|
|Can involve selling a minority stake while the government retains control.|Usually involves a transfer of ownership or control.|

# 2. Reserve Bank of India (RBI)

The Reserve Bank of India is India's central bank. It began operations on **1 April 1935** and was nationalised in **1949**.

## A. Important RBI Facts

|Topic|Fact|
|---|---|
|Established|1935|
|Nationalised|1949|
|Headquarters|Mumbai, Maharashtra|
|RBI Act|1934|
|Main role|Monetary policy, currency management, banking regulation and financial stability|
|Currency issued|Banknotes, except the one-rupee note|
|One-rupee note|Issued by the Government of India and bears the Finance Secretary's signature|
|Government's banker|Acts as banker to the Central and State Governments under applicable arrangements|

## B. Main Functions of RBI

1. **Monetary Policy:** Influences interest rates and liquidity to help maintain price stability while supporting economic growth.
    
2. **Currency Management:** Issues banknotes and manages currency circulation.
    
3. **Banking Regulation:** Regulates and supervises banks and specified financial institutions.
    
4. **Banker's Bank:** Holds reserves of banks and supports the banking system.
    
5. **Foreign Exchange Management:** Performs functions under India's foreign exchange framework.
    
6. **Payment Systems:** Regulates and oversees specified payment systems.
    
7. **Government's Banker:** Provides banking services to the Central and State Governments.
    

## C. Monetary Policy Tools

|Tool|Meaning|
|---|---|
|Repo Rate|Rate at which RBI lends short-term funds to banks against eligible collateral under the repo framework|
|Standing Deposit Facility (SDF)|Facility through which eligible banks can deposit funds with RBI without collateral|
|Marginal Standing Facility (MSF)|Overnight borrowing facility for eligible banks under prescribed conditions|
|Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)|Portion of specified bank liabilities maintained as cash reserves with RBI|
|Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)|Portion of specified liabilities maintained in prescribed liquid assets|
|Open Market Operations (OMO)|RBI purchases or sells government securities to influence liquidity|

### How Repo Rate Affects the Economy

- **Repo rate increases:** Borrowing may become more expensive, helping restrain inflationary demand.
    
- **Repo rate decreases:** Borrowing may become cheaper, potentially encouraging spending and investment.
    
- The actual effect depends on how banks transmit rate changes and how the wider economy responds.
    

### Inflation Targeting

India's flexible inflation-targeting framework has used a Consumer Price Index (CPI) inflation target of **4%, with a tolerance band of ±2 percentage points**.

**Exam tip:** Verify the target period applicable to the question rather than assuming the framework never changes.

|Term|Meaning|

|Current Account|Commonly used by businesses requiring frequent transactions|
|Fixed Deposit (FD)|Money deposited for a specified period, usually at an agreed interest rate|

## C. Deposit Insurance

The **Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC)** insures eligible bank deposits up to **₹5 lakh per depositor per bank**, including principal and interest.

Important points:

- The limit applies subject to applicable rules.
    
- Eligible accounts held in the same capacity and right at the same bank are aggregated for determining the insurance limit.
    

## D. Priority Banking Facts for Exams

- **RBI:** Regulates banks and performs central banking functions.
    
- **NPCI:** Operates key retail payment systems such as UPI and IMPS.
    
- **NABARD:** Agriculture and rural development.
    
- **SIDBI:** MSME development.
    
- **DICGC:** Deposit insurance.
    
- **Reserve Bank–Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021:** Integrated the earlier Banking Ombudsman framework into a unified RBI ombudsman framework.
    

# 4. Union Budget

The Union Budget is the Central Government's annual financial statement and includes associated fiscal proposals.

Under **Article 112 of the Constitution of India**, the Annual Financial Statement is laid before Parliament.

## A. Key Budget Terms

|Term|Meaning|
|---|---|
|Revenue Receipts|Receipts that generally do not create liabilities or reduce government assets, such as taxes|
|Capital Receipts|Receipts that create liabilities or reduce assets, such as borrowings and certain disinvestment receipts|
|Revenue Expenditure|Expenditure generally not creating assets directly, such as salaries and many subsidies|
|Capital Expenditure|Expenditure that creates assets or reduces liabilities, according to the budget classification|
|Fiscal Deficit|Total expenditure minus receipts excluding debt-creating borrowings, under the standard budget definition|
|Revenue Deficit|Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts|
|Primary Deficit|Fiscal deficit minus interest payments|
|Direct Tax|Tax paid directly on income or profits|
|Indirect Tax|Tax imposed on transactions or consumption|

## B. Important Formulas

**Fiscal Deficit**

Fiscal Deficit = Total Expenditure − Receipts Excluding Borrowings

**Revenue Deficit**

Revenue Deficit = Revenue Expenditure − Revenue Receipts

**Primary Deficit**

Primary Deficit = Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments

## C. Important Union Budget Facts

- The **Finance Minister** presents the Union Budget.
    
- The **Lok Sabha** has a central role in voting on Demands for Grants.
    
- The **Finance Bill** gives effect to proposed taxation changes.
    
- The **Economic Survey** reviews the economy and is distinct from the Union Budget.
    
- The **Annual Financial Statement** is constitutionally required under Article 112.
    

### Current-Affairs Preparation

For Budget-related questions, revise the relevant financial year's:

- Major announcements.
    
- Fiscal-deficit target.
    
- Tax changes.
    
- Major sectoral allocations.
    
- Named government initiatives.
    

Use the official India Budget portal: [https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/)

**Exam tip:** Always associate a budget figure with its financial year.

# 5. GDP and National Income

**Gross Domestic Product (GDP)** is the value of final goods and services produced within a country's domestic territory during a specified period.

## A. Important National Income Concepts

|Term|Meaning|
|---|---|
|GDP|Value of production within domestic territory|
|GNP|GDP plus net primary income from abroad, under the conventional national-accounting definition|
|NDP|GDP minus consumption of fixed capital, commonly called depreciation|
|Nominal GDP|GDP measured at current prices|
|Real GDP|GDP measured at constant prices to remove the effect of price changes|
|GDP per Capita|GDP divided by population|
|Economic Growth|Increase in real output over time, commonly measured by real GDP growth|

## B. Three Methods of Measuring GDP

1. **Production or Value-Added Method:** Adds value added across production activities.
    
2. **Income Method:** Adds relevant incomes generated in production.
    
3. **Expenditure Method:** Adds expenditure on final goods and services.
    

### GDP Expenditure Formula

GDP = C + I + G + (X − M)

Where:

- **C:** Private consumption expenditure.
    
- **I:** Investment.
    
- **G:** Government final consumption and relevant government expenditure under the accounting framework.
    
- **X:** Exports.
    
- **M:** Imports.
    

### Why Are Imports Subtracted?

Imports are subtracted because they are included in expenditure but are not part of domestic production.

## C. Important Exam Traps

- Nominal GDP may rise because of higher prices even when real output grows slowly.
    
- Real GDP is generally more useful for measuring changes in output over time.
    
- GDP measures production, not how evenly income is distributed.
    
- GDP per capita is an average; it does not mean every person earns that amount.
    
- GDP refers to production within domestic territory, while GNP also accounts for net primary income from abroad.
    

For Indian GDP figures, refer to the national accounts releases of the **Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI)**. Note the base year and whether the figures are advance, provisional or revised estimates.

# 6. Inflation

Inflation is a sustained increase in the general price level of goods and services. As inflation rises, money generally buys fewer goods and services than before.

## A. Types and Measures of Inflation

|Term|Meaning|
|---|---|
|CPI|Consumer Price Index; measures price changes in a consumer basket|
|WPI|Wholesale Price Index; tracks price changes at the wholesale level|
|Headline Inflation|Inflation measured using the overall index|
|Core Inflation|A measure excluding selected volatile components; its precise definition can vary|
|Demand-Pull Inflation|Price pressure caused by demand exceeding available supply|
|Cost-Push Inflation|Price pressure caused by rising input costs|
|Deflation|A sustained fall in the general price level|
|Disinflation|Inflation slows, but the general price level continues to rise|
|Stagflation|High inflation combined with weak economic activity and high unemployment|

## B. Inflation Rate Formula

Inflation Rate (%) = [(Current Index − Previous Index) ÷ Previous Index] × 100

**Example:**

Suppose a price index rises from 120 to 126.

Inflation Rate = [(126 − 120) ÷ 120] × 100

Inflation Rate = 5%

Therefore, the inflation rate is **5%**. This does not mean every product's price increased by 5%.

## C. CPI vs WPI

|CPI|WPI|
|---|---|
|Measures price changes in a consumer basket|Tracks price changes at the wholesale level|
|Important for consumer inflation|Focuses on wholesale prices|
|Used in India's inflation-targeting framework|Has a different composition from CPI|
|Published by MoSPI in India|Published by the Office of Economic Adviser, Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade|

**Important exam fact:** RBI's monetary policy framework uses CPI inflation as its nominal anchor.

# 9. United Nations (UN)

The United Nations was founded on **24 October 1945**, following the Second World War. Its headquarters is in New York City.

## A. Six Principal Organs of the UN

|Organ|Main Function|Location|
|---|---|---|
|General Assembly|Main deliberative and representative organ|New York|
|Security Council|International peace and security|New York|
|Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC)|Economic, social and sustainable-development coordination|New York|
|International Court of Justice (ICJ)|Settles legal disputes between states and gives advisory opinions|The Hague, Netherlands|
|Secretariat|Day-to-day work of the organisation|New York headquarters and other duty stations|
|Trusteeship Council|Oversaw trust territories; suspended regular operations in 1994|New York|

**Important:** The ICJ is the principal organ located in The Hague rather than New York.

## B. Important UN Facts

- **UN Charter:** Signed in 1945.
    
- **UN established:** 24 October 1945.
    
- **UN Day:** 24 October.
    
- **Security Council:** 15 members, consisting of 5 permanent and 10 elected members.
    
- **Permanent members:** China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States.
    
- **Veto power:** Each permanent member can block a substantive Security Council resolution through a negative vote, subject to the Council's voting rules.
    
- **Secretary-General:** Chief administrative officer of the UN and head of the Secretariat.
    
- **ICJ:** Principal judicial organ of the UN.
    

## C. UN Programmes and Related Bodies

|---|---|

|UNESCO|Education, science and culture|

# 10. International Monetary Fund (IMF)

The International Monetary Fund was conceived at the **Bretton Woods Conference in 1944** and began operating in the post-war international monetary system.

## Important IMF Facts

- **Headquarters:** Washington, D.C., USA.
    
- **Main purpose:** International monetary cooperation and financial stability.
    
- Provides policy advice and economic surveillance.
    
- Provides technical assistance.
    
- Provides financial support to member countries facing eligible balance-of-payments problems.
    

## Important IMF Reports

|Report|Main Association|
|---|---|
|World Economic Outlook (WEO)|Global economic trends and forecasts|
|Global Financial Stability Report (GFSR)|Global financial-system risks and stability|
|Fiscal Monitor|Fiscal developments and public-finance issues|

## IMF vs World Bank

|IMF|World Bank|
|---|---|
|Focuses on monetary cooperation and macroeconomic and financial stability|Focuses on long-term development and poverty reduction|
|Supports countries facing balance-of-payments problems|Finances eligible development projects and programmes|
|Publishes the World Economic Outlook|Publishes the World Development Report|
|Headquarters: Washington, D.C.|Headquarters: Washington, D.C.|

**Exam tip:** Both are associated with the Bretton Woods arrangements, but their primary functions differ.

# 12. International Labour Organization (ILO)

The International Labour Organization was founded in **1919** and became a specialised agency of the UN in **1946**.

- **Headquarters:** Geneva, Switzerland.
    
- **Main purpose:** Promotes decent work, social justice and internationally recognised labour rights.
    
- Develops international labour standards through conventions and recommendations.
    
- Has a distinctive **tripartite structure**, bringing together governments, employers and workers.
    
- Publishes the World Employment and Social Outlook and other labour-market reports.
    
- The International Labour Conference is a major forum for adopting labour standards.
    

## Important Labour-Related Concepts

|Term|Meaning|
|---|---|
|Convention|International instrument that can become binding on a country after ratification, subject to its terms|
|Recommendation|Provides guidance and does not require ratification in the same way as a convention|
|Decent Work|Work associated with productive employment, rights, social protection and dialogue|
|Tripartite Structure|Participation of governments, employers and workers|

7. **Reports and Indexes:** Memorise the report–publisher pairs and verify current rankings only when required.

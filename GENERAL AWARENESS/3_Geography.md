# 2. Indian Rivers

Indian rivers are commonly grouped into Himalayan rivers and Peninsular rivers.

## A. Himalayan river systems

These rivers are generally perennial because they receive water from rainfall and, in many cases, snow and glacier melt.

|River|Source/region|Important facts|
|---|---|---|
|Indus|Tibetan Plateau near Lake Manasarovar region|Flows through Ladakh and Pakistan before reaching the Arabian Sea|
|Ganga|Bhagirathi rises at Gaumukh near Gangotri Glacier|Bhagirathi and Alaknanda meet at Devprayag; the river is called Ganga downstream|
|Yamuna|Yamunotri Glacier region|Meets Ganga at Prayagraj|
|Brahmaputra|Tibetan Plateau, where it is called Yarlung Tsangpo|Enters Arunachal Pradesh as Siang/Dihang; flows through Assam as Brahmaputra|
|Sutlej|Tibetan Plateau near the Lake Rakshastal region|One of the major Indus tributaries|
|Chenab|Formed by the Chandra and Bhaga rivers|Major Indus tributary|
|Beas|Beas Kund region, Himachal Pradesh|Joins the Sutlej system|
|Ravi|Himalayas in Himachal Pradesh|Part of the Indus river system|
|Jhelum|Verinag spring, Kashmir region|Flows through the Kashmir Valley|

### Important tributaries of the Ganga

Exam facts:

- The Brahmaputra is known as Tsangpo in Tibet.
    

## B. Peninsular rivers

Many Peninsular rivers are more dependent on monsoon rainfall. Most major east-flowing rivers drain into the Bay of Bengal, while Narmada and Tapi flow westward into the Arabian Sea.

|River|Source/region|Direction and notable fact|
|---|---|---|
|Godavari|Trimbakeshwar, Maharashtra|East-flowing; often called the Dakshin Ganga|
|Krishna|Near Mahabaleshwar, Maharashtra|Flows east into the Bay of Bengal|
|Kaveri/Cauvery|Talakaveri, Karnataka|Flows through Karnataka and Tamil Nadu|
|Mahanadi|Chhattisgarh highlands|Flows east; forms a delta in Odisha|
|Narmada|Amarkantak region, Madhya Pradesh|Flows west through a rift valley|
|Tapi/Tapti|Satpura region, Madhya Pradesh|Flows west into the Arabian Sea|
|Sabarmati|Aravalli region, Rajasthan|Flows through Gujarat toward the Gulf of Khambhat|
|Luni|Aravalli region, Rajasthan|Flows into the Rann of Kutch region|

### East-flowing vs west-flowing rivers

|East-flowing|West-flowing|
|---|---|
|Godavari|Narmada|
|Krishna|Tapi|
|Kaveri|Sabarmati|
|Mahanadi|Luni|

Memory trick: Remember Narmada and Tapi as the two major west-flowing Peninsular rivers commonly tested in basic Indian Geography.

# 5. Climate of India

India mainly experiences a tropical monsoon climate, though climate varies by latitude, altitude, distance from the sea and relief.

## A. Four broad seasons

|Season|Approximate months|Key features|
|---|---|---|
|Cold weather season|December–February|Cooler temperatures; western disturbances bring winter rain to northwestern India|
|Hot weather season|March–May|Rising temperatures; loo in parts of northern India|
|Southwest monsoon|June–September|Main rainy season for much of India|
|Retreating/post-monsoon monsoon|October–November|Withdrawal of southwest monsoon; northeast monsoon brings important rainfall to Tamil Nadu|

## B. Southwest monsoon branches

- Arabian Sea branch: Brings heavy rainfall to the Western Ghats and parts of western India.
    
- Bay of Bengal branch: Brings rainfall to northeastern India and the Ganga plains.
    
- The Western Ghats cause orographic rainfall on their windward side.
    
- The rain-shadow region east of the Western Ghats receives comparatively less rainfall.
    

## C. Important climate terms

- Monsoon: Seasonal reversal of winds.
    
- Loo: Hot, dry summer winds in parts of northwestern and northern India.
    
- Western disturbances: Weather systems that bring winter precipitation to parts of northwestern India.
    
- Rain shadow: A relatively dry area on the leeward side of a mountain range.
    
- El Niño: Warming of the central/eastern equatorial Pacific that can influence the Indian monsoon, but does not determine every year's rainfall by itself.
    

### Rainfall facts

- Mawsynram in Meghalaya is widely recognised among the wettest places in the world by average annual rainfall.
    
- Rajasthan's western desert region receives very low rainfall.
    
- Tamil Nadu receives a significant share of its rainfall from the northeast monsoon in October–December.
    

# 6. Indian Agriculture

Agriculture depends on rainfall, irrigation, soil, temperature and crop-growing seasons.

## A. Cropping seasons

|Season|Sowing/harvest pattern|Major crops|
|---|---|---|
|Kharif|Sown with the onset of monsoon; harvested around autumn|Rice, maize, cotton, jute, soybean, groundnut|
|Rabi|Sown in winter; harvested in spring|Wheat, gram, mustard, peas, barley|
|Zaid|Short summer season between Rabi and Kharif|Watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, some vegetables and fodder crops|

Remember: Rice is a major Kharif crop, while wheat is a major Rabi crop. Actual seasons can vary with region and irrigation.

## B. Types of soil and crops

|Soil|Characteristics|Common crop associations|
|---|---|---|
|Alluvial|Generally fertile; widespread in river plains|Rice, wheat, sugarcane, jute|
|Black (regur)|High moisture retention; widespread in the Deccan region|Cotton, soybean, some pulses|
|Red and yellow|Iron compounds give the characteristic colour|Millets, pulses, groundnut, depending on local conditions|
|Laterite|Formed under intense leaching in high-rainfall regions|Tea, coffee, cashew with suitable management|
|Arid/desert|Sandy, low organic matter and limited moisture|Bajra and drought-tolerant crops where conditions permit|
|Forest/mountain|Varies with altitude, slope and vegetation|Tea, fruits and other regionally suitable crops|

## C. Major crops and producing regions

|Crop|Important regions/states|
|---|---|
|Rice|West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Andhra Pradesh|
|Wheat|Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan|
|Cotton|Gujarat, Maharashtra, Telangana, Rajasthan|
|Sugarcane|Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka|
|Jute|West Bengal and parts of the Ganga-Brahmaputra delta region|
|Tea|Assam, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, Kerala|
|Coffee|Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu|
|Rubber|Kerala and other humid southern regions|
|Millets|Rajasthan, Karnataka, Maharashtra and other dryland regions|
|Pulses|Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh|

These are important producing regions, not a fixed ranking for every year. Production rankings change by crop, season and official reporting period.

## D. Green Revolution

- Focused primarily on increased food-grain production, especially wheat, through high-yielding varieties, irrigation, fertilisers and improved agricultural practices.
    
- Initially had a strong impact in Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh.
    
- M. S. Swaminathan is widely known as the father of the Green Revolution in India.
    
- Norman Borlaug is widely associated with the global Green Revolution.
    

# 7. Minerals and Energy Resources

India's mineral distribution is strongly related to its geological structure.

## A. Major minerals

|Mineral|Important producing regions|
|---|---|
|Coal|Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal, Madhya Pradesh|
|Iron ore|Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka, Jharkhand|
|Bauxite|Odisha, Gujarat, Jharkhand, Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh|
|Manganese|Odisha, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka|
|Mica|Andhra Pradesh, Rajasthan, Jharkhand|
|Limestone|Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh|
|Copper|Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Jharkhand|
|Petroleum|Assam, Gujarat, offshore Mumbai High, Rajasthan|
|Natural gas|Western offshore areas, Assam, Gujarat, eastern offshore basins|
|Uranium|Jharkhand, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and other identified deposits|

## B. Energy resources

- Coal: Major conventional thermal-energy resource.
    
- Petroleum and natural gas: Important fossil fuels and industrial feedstocks.
    
- Hydroelectric power: Generated using flowing or stored water.
    
- Solar power: Particularly promising in sunny regions such as Rajasthan and Gujarat.
    
- Wind power: Important in Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Karnataka, Maharashtra and Rajasthan.
    
- Nuclear power: Uses nuclear reactions to generate electricity; uranium is a key fuel.
    

### Important mineral belts

- Chota Nagpur Plateau: Known for coal, iron ore and several other minerals.
    
- Odisha–Jharkhand–Chhattisgarh belt: Important for iron ore and coal.
    
- Rajasthan: Important for copper, zinc, lead, limestone and other minerals.
    
- Mumbai High: Important offshore petroleum field.
    

Exam tip: Learn the mineral–state pairs first, then revise the energy source and its uses.

|Line|Latitude|Importance|

# 1. Ancient India

## A. Indus Valley Civilization

The Indus Valley Civilization, also called the Harappan Civilization, was a major Bronze Age urban civilization.

|Site|Present location|Important feature|
|---|---|---|
|Harappa|Punjab, Pakistan|Granaries and cemetery remains|
|Mohenjo-daro|Sindh, Pakistan|Great Bath|
|Dholavira|Gujarat, India|Water-management system; city planning|
|Lothal|Gujarat, India|Traditionally identified as a dockyard|
|Kalibangan|Rajasthan, India|Evidence of a ploughed field; fire altars|
|Rakhigarhi|Haryana, India|Major Harappan site|
|Banawali|Haryana, India|Harappan settlement|

Important features

- Planned cities with streets and drainage systems.
    
- Use of standardized weights and measures.
    
- Trade, craft production and seals.
    
- Script remains undeciphered.
    
- Bronze tools and pottery were used.
    

Exam traps: Mohenjo-daro is famous for the Great Bath; Dholavira for water management; Kalibangan for evidence of a ploughed field.

## C. Buddhism and Jainism

Both traditions emerged in the sixth century BCE and questioned aspects of established ritual practices.

Buddhism

- Founder: Gautama Buddha.
    
- Birth: Lumbini, traditionally dated to the sixth century BCE.
    
- Enlightenment: Bodh Gaya.
    
- First sermon: Sarnath.
    
- Mahaparinirvana: Kushinagar.
    
- Core teachings: Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path.
    
- Sacred texts: Tripitaka, in the Buddhist textual tradition.

Jainism

- 24th Tirthankara: Mahavira.
    
- 23rd Tirthankara: Parshvanatha.
    
- Major teachings: Ahimsa, truthfulness, non-stealing, non-possession and celibacy.
    
- Important principles: Anekantavada and Aparigraha.
    
- Jain traditions place great emphasis on self-discipline and liberation.
    

### Buddhism vs Jainism

|Buddhism|Jainism|
|---|---|
|Gautama Buddha is the founder of the Buddhist tradition|Mahavira was the 24th Tirthankara, not the first|
|Four Noble Truths and Eightfold Path|Ahimsa, Anekantavada and Aparigraha|
|Middle Way|Strong emphasis on ascetic discipline in many traditions|

## D. Maurya Empire

|Ruler|Key fact|
|---|---|

|Bindusara|Son and successor of Chandragupta Maurya|
|Ashoka|Kalinga War; inscriptions and promotion of Dhamma|

Ashoka: The Kalinga War is generally dated to around 261 BCE. The suffering caused by the war is associated with Ashoka's emphasis on Dhamma. His edicts were inscribed on rocks and pillars. Most were in Prakrit using Brahmi script, while some northwestern inscriptions used other scripts and languages.

# 2. Medieval India

## A. Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526)

The Delhi Sultanate consisted of five main dynasties.

|Dynasty|Period|Important rulers and facts|
|---|---|---|
|Mamluk/Slave|1206–1290|Qutb-ud-din Aibak, Iltutmish, Razia Sultan, Balban|
|Khalji|1290–1320|Alauddin Khalji; market and military reforms|
|Tughlaq|1320–1414|Muhammad bin Tughlaq; experiments with token currency and transfer of capital|
|Sayyid|1414–1451|Khizr Khan and successors|
|Lodi|1451–1526|Bahlul Lodi, Sikandar Lodi, Ibrahim Lodi|

Important facts:

- Qutb-ud-din Aibak established the Delhi Sultanate in 1206.
    
- Iltutmish consolidated the Sultanate and is associated with the silver tanka and copper jital.
    
- Razia Sultan was the first and only woman to rule as Sultan of Delhi in her own right.
    
- Alauddin Khalji introduced market-control measures and strengthened the military.
    
- Muhammad bin Tughlaq is associated with the attempted transfer of the capital from Delhi to Daulatabad and token currency.
    
- Ibrahim Lodi was defeated by Babur at the First Battle of Panipat in 1526.

## D. Vijayanagara and Maratha Empires

- Vijayanagara Empire: Founded in 1336 by Harihara and Bukka; Hampi was its capital.
    
- Krishnadevaraya: Famous ruler of the Tuluva dynasty; patron of literature and architecture.
    
- Battle of Talikota: 1565; a major defeat for Vijayanagara forces.
    
- Shivaji: Founded and consolidated the Maratha kingdom; crowned in 1674 at Raigad.
    
- Ashtapradhan: Council of eight ministers associated with Shivaji's administration.
    
- Peshwas: Became increasingly powerful in Maratha politics, especially in the eighteenth century.

# 5. Major Events and Movements of the Freedom Struggle

## A. Revolt of 1857

The Revolt of 1857 was a major uprising against Company rule.

Important leaders and centres:

|Leader|Centre/region|
|---|---|
|Bahadur Shah Zafar|Delhi|
|Rani Lakshmibai|Jhansi|
|Nana Sahib|Kanpur|
|Tantia Tope|Central India; associated with Nana Sahib and the wider revolt|
|Begum Hazrat Mahal|Lucknow|
|Kunwar Singh|Bihar|
|Bakht Khan|Delhi|

Causes included political annexations, economic grievances, military discrimination, social and religious anxieties, and the immediate controversy over rifle cartridges.

Consequences: The East India Company's rule ended under the Government of India Act, 1858. Authority passed to the British Crown, and the Governor-General also became the Viceroy.

## B. Partition of Bengal and Swadeshi Movement

- Bengal was partitioned by Lord Curzon in 1905.
    
- The Swadeshi and boycott movements encouraged Indian-made goods and opposition to British goods.
    
- The partition was annulled in 1911.
    
- The capital was transferred from Calcutta to Delhi in 1911.
    

## C. Home Rule Movement

- Associated with Bal Gangadhar Tilak and Annie Besant in 1916.
    
- Demanded self-government within the British Empire.
    
- Helped expand political mobilisation before Gandhi's major mass movements.
    

## D. Gandhi's Early Satyagrahas

|Movement|Year|Issue and place|
|---|---|---|
|Champaran Satyagraha|1917|Indigo cultivators in Bihar|
|Ahmedabad Mill Strike|1918|Textile mill workers' dispute in Gujarat|
|Kheda Satyagraha|1918|Peasants seeking revenue relief in Gujarat|

These campaigns helped establish Gandhi's approach of non-violent resistance and mass mobilisation.

## E. Rowlatt Act and Jallianwala Bagh

- Rowlatt Act: 1919; allowed repressive measures against suspected revolutionary activity.
    
- Jallianwala Bagh massacre: 13 April 1919, Amritsar.
    
- Troops under General Reginald Dyer fired on a gathering, causing mass casualties.
    
- Rabindranath Tagore renounced his knighthood in protest.
    

## F. Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922)

Key features:

- Boycott of government schools, courts, councils and foreign goods.
    
- Promotion of khadi and swadeshi.
    
- Mass mobilisation under Gandhi and the Congress.
    
- Closely associated with the Khilafat movement during its early phase.
    

Why was it withdrawn? Gandhi suspended the movement after the Chauri Chaura incident in February 1922, where violence led to the deaths of policemen.

## G. Civil Disobedience Movement and Dandi March

- Lahore Congress: December 1929; Purna Swaraj became the Congress goal.
    
- 26 January 1930: Observed as Independence Day by nationalists.
    
- Dandi March: Began on 12 March 1930 and culminated in the salt-law breach on 6 April 1930.
    
- Gandhi's Salt Satyagraha became the symbolic beginning of the Civil Disobedience Movement.
    
- Gandhi–Irwin Pact: 1931; led to Gandhi's participation in the Second Round Table Conference.
    

## H. Quit India Movement (1942)

- Launched on 8 August 1942 at Bombay's Gowalia Tank Maidan, now known as August Kranti Maidan.
    
- Gandhi gave the call “Do or Die.”
    
- Congress leaders were arrested soon after the launch.
    
- Underground resistance and local uprisings continued in many regions.
    
- Parallel governments emerged in places including Ballia, Tamluk and Satara, though their forms and durations differed.

### Gandhi's principles

- Ahimsa: Non-violence.
    
- Satyagraha: Resistance based on truth and non-violent action.
    
- Swadeshi: Preference for indigenous goods and institutions.
    
- Sarvodaya: Welfare of all.
    
- Civil disobedience: Deliberately and non-violently resisting unjust laws.

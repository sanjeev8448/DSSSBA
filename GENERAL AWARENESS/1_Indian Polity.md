## 1. The Indian Constitution

The Constitution is the supreme law of India. It establishes the structure of government, distributes powers, and protects citizens' rights.

## 2. Fundamental Rights

Fundamental Rights are guaranteed by Part III of the Constitution. They are enforceable by courts, subject to constitutional restrictions and conditions.

|Right|Articles|What it covers|
|---|---|---|
|Right to Equality|14–18|Equality before law, prohibition of discrimination, equality of opportunity, abolition of untouchability and titles|
|Right to Freedom|19–22|Freedoms under Article 19, protection in criminal cases, life and personal liberty, safeguards against arrest and detention|
|Right against Exploitation|23–24|Prohibition of trafficking, forced labour and specified forms of child labour|
|Right to Freedom of Religion|25–28|Freedom of conscience and religion, subject to constitutional limits|
|Cultural and Educational Rights|29–30|Protection of cultural interests and minority educational institutions|
|Right to Constitutional Remedies|32|Right to approach the Supreme Court to enforce Fundamental Rights|

### Must-remember Articles

- Article 14: Equality before law and equal protection of laws.
    
- Article 17: Abolition of untouchability.
    
- Article 19: Six specified freedoms for citizens, subject to reasonable restrictions.
    
- Article 21: Protection of life and personal liberty.
    
- Article 21A: Free and compulsory education for children aged 6–14 years.
    
- Article 23: Prohibits trafficking and forced labour.
    
- Article 24: Prohibits employment of children below 14 in factories, mines and other hazardous employment covered by the provision.
    
- Article 32: Constitutional remedies before the Supreme Court.
    

Important exam trap: The Right to Property is no longer a Fundamental Right. It is a constitutional legal right under Article 300A.

### Five constitutional writs

|Writ|Purpose|
|---|---|
|Habeas Corpus|Protects against unlawful detention|
|Mandamus|Commands a public authority to perform a public duty|
|Prohibition|Stops a lower court or tribunal from exceeding its jurisdiction|
|Certiorari|Quashes an order of a lower court or tribunal on recognised legal grounds|
|Quo Warranto|Questions the legal authority of a person holding a public office|

Remember: Article 32 empowers the Supreme Court to issue writs for Fundamental Rights. High Courts can issue writs under Article 226 for Fundamental Rights and for other purposes within their jurisdiction.

## 3. Fundamental Duties

Fundamental Duties are listed in Article 51A, Part IVA.

They were added by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976, following the recommendations of the Swaran Singh Committee. The 86th Amendment Act, 2002 added the duty concerning educational opportunities for children.

There are currently 11 Fundamental Duties.

Key duties include:

- Respect the Constitution, National Flag and National Anthem.
    
- Cherish the ideals of the freedom struggle.
    
- Uphold the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India.
    
- Defend the country when called upon.
    
- Promote harmony and renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women.
    
- Preserve India's composite cultural heritage.
    
- Protect the environment and show compassion for living creatures.
    
- Develop scientific temper, humanism and a spirit of inquiry.
    
- Safeguard public property and avoid violence.
    
- Strive for excellence.
    
- As a parent or guardian, provide opportunities for education to children aged 6–14 years.
    

Exam fact: Fundamental Duties are generally non-justiciable by themselves, meaning they are not directly enforceable in court in the same way as Fundamental Rights. Laws can, however, give effect to related obligations.

## 4. Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)

DPSPs are guidelines for governments to promote social and economic welfare. They appear in Part IV, Articles 36–51.

Under Article 37, they are not enforceable by courts, but are fundamental in the governance of the country.

|Article|Important provision|
|---|---|
|39|Principles relating to livelihood, distribution of resources and welfare|
|39A|Equal justice and free legal aid|
|40|Organisation of village panchayats|
|44|Uniform Civil Code|
|45|Early childhood care and education for children below six years|
|46|Promotion of educational and economic interests of weaker sections, especially SCs and STs|
|47|Nutrition, standard of living and public health|
|48A|Protection and improvement of the environment|
|50|Separation of judiciary from executive in public services of the State|
|51|International peace and security|

### Fundamental Rights vs DPSP vs Fundamental Duties

|Feature|Fundamental Rights|DPSP|Fundamental Duties|
|---|---|---|---|
|Part|III|IV|IVA|
|Articles|12–35|36–51|51A|
|Main purpose|Protect rights and freedoms|Guide government policy|Encourage civic responsibility|
|Directly enforceable in court?|Yes, subject to constitutional rules|No|Generally no, by themselves|
|Added by amendment?|Original Constitution, subsequently amended|Original Constitution, subsequently amended|42nd Amendment; one duty added by 86th Amendment|

## 5. President of India

The President is the constitutional head of the Union executive.

|Feature|Key fact|
|---|---|
|Articles|52–62 cover the office and election-related provisions|
|Election|Indirect election by an electoral college|
|Term|5 years|
|Minimum age|35 years|
|Resignation|Addressed to the Vice-President|
|Removal|Impeachment for violation of the Constitution|
|Supreme Commander|Of the Defence Forces|

### Important powers

- Executive: Appoints the Prime Minister and other officials according to constitutional provisions.
    
- Legislative: Summons and prorogues Parliament and gives assent to bills, subject to constitutional rules.
    
- Financial: A Money Bill requires the President's recommendation for introduction in the Lok Sabha.
    
- Judicial: Can grant pardons and related relief under Article 72.
    
- Emergency: Exercises powers under constitutional emergency provisions.
    

The President normally acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister, under Article 74.

Exam trap: The President is elected indirectly, not directly by the people. The impeachment procedure is specified in Article 61.

## 6. Prime Minister and Council of Ministers

The Prime Minister is the head of the government and leads the Council of Ministers.

- Article 74: Council of Ministers to aid and advise the President.
    
- Article 75: Appointment and other provisions relating to ministers.
    
- The President appoints the Prime Minister.
    
- Other ministers are appointed by the President on the Prime Minister's advice.
    
- The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.
    
- A minister who is not a member of either House of Parliament must become a member within six consecutive months to continue as a minister.
    

### President vs Prime Minister

|President|Prime Minister|
|---|---|
|Constitutional head of the Union|Head of government|
|Indirectly elected|Appointed by the President|
|Normally acts on ministerial advice|Leads the Council of Ministers|
|Five-year term, subject to constitutional provisions|Remains in office while commanding the required political support and subject to constitutional rules|

## 7. Parliament of India

Under Article 79, Parliament consists of the President, Rajya Sabha and Lok Sabha.

## Lok Sabha

House of the People — lower House

- Members are directly elected by voters.
    
- Normal term: 5 years, unless dissolved earlier.
    
- Money Bills can be introduced only here.
    
- The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to this House.
    

## Rajya Sabha

Council of States — upper House

- A permanent House; it is not dissolved.
    
- Members generally serve six-year terms.
    
- Approximately one-third of its members retire every two years.
    
- The Vice-President is its ex officio Chairman.
    

### High-priority parliamentary facts

- Article 108: Joint sitting of both Houses in specified circumstances, subject to exceptions.
    
- Article 110: Definition of a Money Bill.
    
- Article 112: Annual Financial Statement, commonly called the Union Budget.
    
- Article 123: President's ordinance-making power when Parliament is not in session, subject to constitutional conditions.
    
- Article 368: Amendment of the Constitution.
    

Money Bill trap: Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend a Money Bill; it may make recommendations within 14 days. The Lok Sabha may accept or reject those recommendations.

The Supreme Court is India's highest judicial body.

|Established|28 January 1950|
|Chief Justice|Head of the Supreme Court|
|Retirement age of Supreme Court judges|65 years|
|Appointment|By the President under constitutional and applicable appointment procedures|
|Article 32|Enforcement of Fundamental Rights|
|Article 131|Original jurisdiction in specified Union–State disputes|
|Article 136|Special Leave Petition jurisdiction|
|Article 141|Law declared by Supreme Court is binding on all courts in India|

### High Courts

- Articles 214–231 contain major provisions concerning High Courts.
    
- High Court judges retire at 62 years.
    
- Under Article 226, High Courts can issue writs for Fundamental Rights and other purposes within their jurisdiction.
    

## 9. Election Commission of India

The Election Commission of India is a constitutional body under Article 324.

Its main functions include supervising, directing and controlling elections to:

- Parliament.
    
- State legislatures.
    
- The offices of President and Vice-President.
    

### Important distinctions

- The Election Commission of India conducts elections to Parliament and State legislatures.
    
- State Election Commissions, under Articles 243K and 243ZA, deal with Panchayat and Municipal elections.
    
- The voting age was reduced from 21 to 18 years by the 61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988.
    

Do not confuse the Election Commission with the Union Public Service Commission or the Finance Commission; they are different constitutional bodies.

## 10. Important Constitutional Bodies

A constitutional body is established by the Constitution itself, rather than solely by an ordinary law of Parliament.

|Body|Article|Main function|
|---|---|---|
|Election Commission of India|324|Conducts specified elections|
|Union Public Service Commission (UPSC)|315–323|Recruitment and advisory functions for public services|
|Finance Commission|280|Recommends distribution of certain tax revenues and grants|
|Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)|148–151|Audits government accounts and expenditure|
|National Commission for Scheduled Castes|338|Safeguards and investigates matters relating to SCs|
|National Commission for Scheduled Tribes|338A|Safeguards and investigates matters relating to STs|
|National Commission for Backward Classes|338B|Constitutional safeguards for backward classes|
|Attorney General of India|76|Chief legal adviser to the Government of India|
|Advocate General of a State|165|Chief legal adviser to the State Government|
|GST Council|279A|Recommends GST-related measures|

### Constitutional vs statutory bodies

- Constitutional body: Established by a constitutional provision. Example: Election Commission.
    
- Statutory body: Established under legislation enacted by Parliament or a State Legislature. Example: National Human Rights Commission, established under the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993.
    

## 11. Important Constitutional Amendments

An amendment changes the Constitution through the procedure in Article 368, where applicable. Some amendments require a special parliamentary majority and, for specified federal provisions, ratification by at least half the State legislatures.

|Amendment|Year|Key provision|
|---|---|---|
|1st|1951|Added provisions including the Ninth Schedule and restrictions-related changes|
|7th|1956|Reorganisation of States and related changes|
|24th|1971|Affirmed Parliament's power to amend the Constitution, including Fundamental Rights, subject to constitutional limits|
|42nd|1976|Known as the “Mini Constitution”; added Socialist, Secular and Integrity to the Preamble; added Fundamental Duties|
|44th|1978|Right to Property ceased to be a Fundamental Right; safeguards concerning emergencies were changed|
|52nd|1985|Anti-defection provisions; Tenth Schedule|
|61st|1988|Voting age reduced from 21 to 18|
|73rd|1992|Constitutional status for Panchayati Raj institutions; Part IX|
|74th|1992|Constitutional status for municipalities; Part IXA|
|86th|2002|Article 21A: Right to Education for ages 6–14; related changes|
|91st|2003|Limits on the size of the Council of Ministers and related anti-defection changes|
|101st|2016|Introduced the constitutional framework for GST|
|102nd|2018|Constitutional status to the National Commission for Backward Classes|
|103rd|2019|Up to 10% reservation for Economically Weaker Sections, subject to constitutional provisions|
|104th|2019|Extended SC/ST legislative reservation and ended the provision for Anglo-Indian nominated seats in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies|
|105th|2021|Restored States' and Union Territories' power to identify socially and educationally backward classes for their own purposes, within the constitutional framework|
|106th|2023|Women's reservation provisions for Lok Sabha, State Legislative Assemblies and Delhi Assembly, subject to the constitutional implementation conditions|

### Amendments worth memorising first

Focus on 42nd, 44th, 52nd, 61st, 73rd, 74th, 86th, 101st, 103rd and 106th.

Important trap: The 106th Amendment was enacted in 2023, but its reservation provisions are subject to the constitutional conditions concerning a census and subsequent delimitation. Do not assume the reserved seats automatically took effect immediately upon enactment.

### A. Physical quantities and SI units

|Resistance|Ohm|Ω|

**Important:** Mass measures the amount of matter, while weight is the gravitational force acting on an object.

### B. Motion and force

- Speed = Distance ÷ Time
    
- Velocity = Displacement ÷ Time, with direction
    
- Acceleration = Change in velocity ÷ Time
    
- Force = Mass × Acceleration, or F = ma
    
- Momentum = Mass × Velocity, or p = mv
    

### Newton's three laws of motion

1. **First law:** An object remains at rest or in uniform straight-line motion unless acted upon by a net external force. This is the law of inertia.
    
2. **Second law:** Net force equals the rate of change of momentum. For constant mass, F = ma.
    
3. **Third law:** Every action has an equal and opposite reaction. The two forces act on different bodies.
    

### Work, energy and power

|Concept|Formula or definition|
|---|---|
|Work|W = Fs cos θ|
|Kinetic energy|KE = 1/2 × mv²|
|Gravitational potential energy|PE = mgh, near Earth's surface|
|Power|P = W/t|
|Efficiency|(Useful output energy ÷ Input energy) × 100%|

When force and displacement are in the same direction, work = force × displacement.

### C. Heat and temperature

- Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference.
    
- Temperature indicates how hot or cold an object is.
    
- Heat transfer occurs through conduction, convection and radiation.
    
- Metals are generally good conductors of heat.
    
- Woollen clothes keep us warm mainly by trapping air, which is a poor conductor.
    
- Black, dull surfaces are generally good absorbers and emitters of thermal radiation.
    
- Water freezes at approximately 0°C and boils at 100°C at standard atmospheric pressure.
    

**Temperature conversions**

- Fahrenheit = (9/5 × Celsius) + 32
    
- Kelvin = Celsius + 273.15
    
- For quick aptitude calculations, Kelvin ≈ Celsius + 273.
    

### D. Light

- Light generally travels in straight lines through a uniform medium.
    
- **Reflection:** Light bounces off a surface.
    
- **Refraction:** Light changes direction when passing between media because its speed changes.
    
- **Dispersion:** White light separates into its constituent colours.
    
- **Concave mirror:** Can form real or virtual images depending on object position.
    
- **Convex mirror:** Produces a virtual, erect and diminished image; used in vehicle rear-view mirrors.
    
- **Convex lens:** Converges parallel rays.
    
- **Concave lens:** Diverges parallel rays.
    

Visible-light colour order:

Violet → Indigo → Blue → Green → Yellow → Orange → Red (VIBGYOR)

Red light has a longer wavelength than violet light in the visible spectrum.

### E. Sound

- Sound is produced by vibrating objects.
    
- Sound requires a material medium and cannot travel through a vacuum.
    
- Frequency is measured in hertz (Hz).
    
- The audible range for a typical healthy young human is approximately 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz.
    
- **Infrasound:** Below 20 Hz.
    
- **Ultrasound:** Above 20,000 Hz.
    
- Sound travels faster in many solids than in gases, although its speed depends on the material and conditions.
    
- An echo is reflected sound.
    

### F. Electricity and magnetism

|Concept|Key point|
|---|---|
|Electric current|I = Q/t|
|Ohm's law|V = IR, under constant physical conditions|
|Electric power|P = VI|
|Electrical energy|E = Pt|
|Series circuit|Same current through components|
|Parallel circuit|Same voltage across branches|
|Fuse|Melts when excessive current flows, helping interrupt a circuit|
|Earthing|Provides a low-resistance path for fault current to help protect people|

**Exam trap:** A transformer operates using electromagnetic induction and ordinarily requires changing current, such as AC. A simple transformer does not operate continuously on steady DC.

## 2. Chemistry Basics

### A. Matter and its states

Matter has mass and occupies space.

|State|Shape and volume|Particle behaviour|
|---|---|---|
|Solid|Fixed shape and volume|Particles closely packed|
|Liquid|No fixed shape; fixed volume|Particles move past one another|
|Gas|No fixed shape or volume|Particles move freely and spread out|
|Plasma|No fixed shape or volume|Contains charged particles|

### Changes of state

- Melting: Solid → Liquid
    
- Freezing: Liquid → Solid
    
- Vaporisation: Liquid → Gas
    
- Condensation: Gas → Liquid
    
- Sublimation: Solid → Gas directly
    
- Deposition: Gas → Solid directly
    

Example: Dry ice, which is solid carbon dioxide, can change directly into gas under suitable conditions.

### B. Elements, compounds and mixtures

|Term|Meaning|Example|
|---|---|---|
|Element|Substance made of one type of atom|Iron, oxygen|
|Compound|Elements chemically combined in a fixed proportion|Water, carbon dioxide|
|Mixture|Substances physically combined|Air, salt water|
|Atom|Smallest unit of an element that retains its chemical identity|Oxygen atom|
|Molecule|Two or more atoms chemically bonded|O₂, H₂O|

**Important:** A compound has a fixed chemical composition. The proportions of substances in a mixture can vary.

### C. Atomic structure

- **Proton:** Positive charge; found in the nucleus.
    
- **Neutron:** No electric charge; found in the nucleus.
    
- **Electron:** Negative charge; occupies regions around the nucleus.
    
- **Atomic number:** Number of protons.
    
- **Mass number:** Number of protons + number of neutrons.
    
- **Isotopes:** Atoms of the same element with the same atomic number but different numbers of neutrons.
    

For a neutral atom, the number of electrons equals the number of protons.

### D. Acids, bases and salts

|Property|Acids|Bases|
|---|---|---|
|Litmus test|Blue litmus turns red|Red litmus turns blue|
|pH at approximately 25°C|Below 7|Above 7|
|Common examples|Hydrochloric acid, vinegar, lemon juice|Sodium hydroxide, limewater|

**Neutralisation:** A reaction between an acid and a base, commonly producing salt and water.

Example:

Hydrochloric acid + Sodium hydroxide → Sodium chloride + Water

Other important substances:

- Vinegar contains acetic acid.
    
- Lemon juice contains citric acid.
    
- Baking soda = Sodium bicarbonate, NaHCO₃.
    
- Washing soda = Sodium carbonate decahydrate, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O.
    
- Common salt = Sodium chloride, NaCl.
    
- Bleaching powder is commonly represented as CaOCl₂ in school-level chemistry.

### F. Metals and non-metals

- Metals are generally good conductors of heat and electricity, malleable and ductile.
    
- Non-metals are generally poor conductors, although graphite is an important exception.
    
- Mercury is a metal that is liquid at ordinary room temperature.
    
- Bromine is a non-metal that is liquid at ordinary room temperature.
    
- Sodium and potassium are highly reactive metals and are stored under suitable protective conditions.
    
- Rusting of iron requires oxygen and moisture; salt can accelerate corrosion.
    

### Important alloys

|Alloy|Main constituents|
|---|---|
|Brass|Copper + zinc|
|Bronze|Copper + tin|
|Steel|Iron + carbon, with other elements in some steels|
|Stainless steel|Iron-based alloy with chromium and usually other elements such as nickel|
|Traditional solder|Tin + lead; lead-free varieties are also common|

### G. Periodic table

- The modern periodic table is arranged by increasing atomic number.
    
- Horizontal rows are called **periods**.
    
- Vertical columns are called **groups**.
    
- Group 1 contains the alkali metals, excluding hydrogen from the usual classification.
    
- Group 17 contains the halogens.
    
- Group 18 contains the noble gases.
    
- The modern periodic law relates elemental properties to atomic number.

## 3. Biology Basics

### A. Cell and its organelles

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.

|Cell part|Main function|
|---|---|
|Cell membrane|Regulates movement of substances into and out of the cell|
|Nucleus|Contains most of the cell's genetic material and helps control cell activities|
|Mitochondria|Produce much of the cell's usable energy through cellular respiration|
|Ribosomes|Protein synthesis|
|Endoplasmic reticulum|Protein and lipid synthesis and processing, depending on type|
|Golgi apparatus|Modifies, sorts and packages proteins and lipids|
|Lysosomes|Intracellular digestion in many animal cells|
|Cell wall|Support and protection in plant cells|
|Chloroplasts|Photosynthesis in plants and algae|
|Vacuole|Storage and water balance; typically large in mature plant cells|

**Important exam facts**

- Mitochondria are often called the “powerhouse of the cell.”
    
- Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll.
    
- Plant cells typically have a cell wall and chloroplasts; animal cells do not have chloroplasts or a cellulose cell wall.
    
- Viruses are acellular and require host cells to replicate.
    

### B. Nutrition and photosynthesis

Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy.

**Equation:**

6CO₂ + 6H₂O —(light and chlorophyll)→ C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

- Plants absorb water through roots and carbon dioxide mainly through stomata.
    
- Chlorophyll captures light energy.
    
- Photosynthesis occurs in chloroplasts.
    
- Cellular respiration releases usable energy from food molecules.
    
- Aerobic respiration generally uses oxygen; anaerobic pathways can operate without oxygen.
    

### C. Human reproduction and genetics

- DNA carries hereditary information.
    
- Genes are segments of DNA that contribute to inherited characteristics.
    
- Chromosomes contain DNA and associated proteins.
    
- Humans typically have 46 chromosomes arranged in 23 pairs in most body cells.
    
- Gametes typically contain 23 chromosomes.
    
- **Mitosis:** Produces two daughter cells with broadly the same chromosome number as the parent cell.
    
- **Meiosis:** Produces cells with half the usual chromosome number and contributes to genetic variation.
    

### D. Classification of living organisms

|Group|Key feature or example|
|---|---|
|Bacteria|Single-celled prokaryotic organisms|
|Protists|Diverse eukaryotic organisms, often single-celled|
|Fungi|Absorb nutrients; examples include mushrooms and yeast|
|Plants|Generally photosynthetic multicellular organisms|
|Animals|Multicellular organisms that obtain nutrients by ingestion|

**Note:** Classification systems vary. Modern biology often uses domains and kingdoms rather than treating every school-level classification as universal.

### E. Vitamins and deficiency diseases

|Vitamin|Main role|Deficiency association|
|---|---|---|
|Vitamin A|Vision and epithelial health|Night blindness|
|Vitamin B1 (thiamine)|Energy metabolism and nerve function|Beriberi|
|Vitamin B3 (niacin)|Energy metabolism|Pellagra|
|Vitamin B9 (folate)|DNA synthesis and cell division|Megaloblastic anaemia|
|Vitamin B12|Nerve function and red blood cell formation|Megaloblastic anaemia and neurological problems|
|Vitamin C|Collagen formation and wound healing|Scurvy|
|Vitamin D|Calcium absorption and bone health|Rickets in children; osteomalacia in adults|
|Vitamin E|Antioxidant functions|Deficiency is uncommon; neurological or muscular problems may occur|
|Vitamin K|Normal blood clotting|Increased bleeding tendency|

### F. Minerals and nutrition

|Nutrient|Function|Deficiency association|
|---|---|---|
|Iron|Haemoglobin formation|Iron-deficiency anaemia|
|Iodine|Thyroid hormone production|Goitre and other iodine-deficiency disorders|
|Calcium|Bones, teeth, muscles and nerve signalling|Impaired bone health|
|Phosphorus|Bones, teeth, ATP and DNA|Deficiency is uncommon in a typical diet|
|Sodium|Fluid balance and nerve impulses|Low blood sodium can cause serious symptoms|
|Potassium|Muscle and nerve function|Low levels can cause weakness and abnormal heart rhythms|

**Exam trap:** Goitre is classically associated with iodine deficiency, but not every case of goitre is caused by iodine deficiency.

## 4. Human Body

### A. Major organs and their functions

|Organ|Main function|
|---|---|
|Brain|Coordinates nervous-system activity and many body functions|
|Heart|Pumps blood through the circulatory system|
|Lungs|Exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide|
|Liver|Processes nutrients, produces bile and performs detoxification functions|
|Kidneys|Filter blood, regulate fluid/electrolyte balance and produce urine|
|Stomach|Begins substantial chemical digestion of proteins|
|Small intestine|Main site of nutrient digestion and absorption|
|Large intestine|Absorbs water and electrolytes and forms stool|
|Pancreas|Produces digestive enzymes and hormones including insulin|
|Skin|Barrier protection, sensation and temperature regulation|
|Spleen|Filters blood and supports immune functions|

### B. Blood and circulation

- **Red blood cells (RBCs):** Carry oxygen using haemoglobin.
    
- **White blood cells (WBCs):** Defend against infections and other threats.
    
- **Platelets:** Help blood clot.
    
- **Plasma:** Liquid component carrying cells, proteins and dissolved substances.
    

### Blood groups

- ABO groups: A, B, AB and O.
    
- The Rh system commonly classifies blood as positive or negative.
    
- In standard red-cell transfusion teaching, O negative is often called the universal red-cell donor and AB positive the universal red-cell recipient.
    

**Important:** Actual transfusions require compatibility testing and clinical protocols.

### C. Digestive system

Typical pathway:

Mouth → Pharynx → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine → Rectum → Anus

Important digestive secretions:

- Saliva contains enzymes such as salivary amylase.
    
- The stomach secretes hydrochloric acid and enzymes involved in digestion.
    
- The liver produces bile; the gallbladder stores and concentrates it.
    
- The pancreas secretes digestive enzymes and bicarbonate.
    
- Most nutrient absorption occurs in the small intestine.
    

### D. Endocrine glands and hormones

|Gland or organ|Hormone or association|
|---|---|
|Pituitary|Several hormones; regulates other endocrine glands|
|Thyroid|Thyroxine (T4) and related thyroid hormones|
|Pancreas|Insulin and glucagon regulate blood glucose|
|Adrenal glands|Adrenaline and cortisol, among other hormones|
|Ovaries|Oestrogen and progesterone|
|Testes|Testosterone|
|Pineal gland|Melatonin, associated with sleep–wake timing|
|Parathyroid glands|Parathyroid hormone regulates calcium balance|

### E. Bones and senses

- Adult human skeleton: commonly described as 206 bones.
    
- Largest bone: Femur.
    
- Smallest bone: Stapes in the middle ear.
    
- Retina: Light-sensitive tissue in the eye.
    
- Iris: Controls pupil size.
    
- Cochlea: Involved in hearing.
    
- Semicircular canals: Help detect rotational head movement and maintain balance.
    
- Skin contains receptors involved in touch, pressure, temperature and pain.

|---|---|---|
|Tuberculosis (TB)|Mycobacterium tuberculosis|Airborne particles from infectious pulmonary TB|
|Malaria|Plasmodium parasites|Female Anopheles mosquitoes|
|Dengue|Dengue virus|Infected Aedes mosquitoes|
|Chikungunya|Chikungunya virus|Infected Aedes mosquitoes|

**Important facts**

- Antibiotics act against susceptible bacteria, not viruses.
    
- Vaccination, sanitation, vector control and appropriate medical care play different roles in disease prevention.
    

### B. Non-communicable diseases

|Condition|Key association|
|---|---|
|Diabetes mellitus|Problems with insulin production or action; high blood glucose|
|Hypertension|Persistently high blood pressure|
|Scurvy|Vitamin C deficiency|
|Rickets|Vitamin D deficiency and impaired bone mineralisation|
|Goitre|Enlargement of the thyroid; iodine deficiency is one possible cause|
|Anaemia|Reduced haemoglobin or red-cell-related oxygen-carrying capacity; causes vary|
|Osteoporosis|Reduced bone strength and increased fracture risk|
|Coronary artery disease|Reduced blood supply to heart muscle, commonly due to atherosclerosis|

### C. Prevention basics

- Wash hands and use safe drinking water.
    
- Follow recommended vaccination schedules.
    
- Prevent mosquito breeding and use appropriate bite prevention.
    
- Handle and cook food safely.
    
- Avoid tobacco and second-hand smoke.
    
- Exercise regularly, eat a balanced diet and get adequate sleep.
    
- Seek professional medical advice for persistent or serious symptoms.

## 6. Environment and Ecology

### A. Ecosystem and food chain

An ecosystem includes living organisms and the non-living environment with which they interact.

- **Producers:** Plants and other organisms that produce organic matter, commonly using sunlight.
    
- **Consumers:** Organisms that obtain energy by eating other organisms.
    
- **Decomposers:** Organisms such as fungi and bacteria that break down dead material and recycle nutrients.
    

Example food chain:

Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle

**Remember:** Energy flows through ecosystems, while nutrients are recycled.

### B. Biodiversity and conservation

- **Biodiversity:** Variety of genes, species and ecosystems.
    
- **Deforestation:** Loss or removal of forests.
    
- **Afforestation:** Establishing forest cover on land that has not recently been forested.
    
- **Reforestation:** Re-establishing forest cover on previously forested land.
    
- **In-situ conservation:** Protecting organisms in their natural habitats, such as national parks.
    
- **Ex-situ conservation:** Protecting organisms outside their natural habitats, such as in seed banks and zoos.
    

### C. Pollution

|Type|Examples of sources|Possible impacts|
|---|---|---|
|Air|Vehicle exhaust, industrial emissions, burning|Respiratory and cardiovascular harm|
|Water|Sewage, industrial discharge, agricultural runoff|Disease, ecosystem damage and eutrophication|
|Soil|Excess chemicals, waste dumping, industrial contamination|Reduced soil quality and contamination of food chains|
|Noise|Traffic, construction and machinery|Hearing problems, stress and sleep disturbance|
|Thermal|Heated industrial discharge|Changes in aquatic oxygen levels and ecosystems|

### Greenhouse effect and climate change

- Greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit infrared radiation.
    
- Important greenhouse gases include carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide and water vapour.
    
- Human activities, especially fossil-fuel combustion and land-use change, are driving current global warming.
    
- Climate change can affect heat extremes, rainfall patterns, sea levels and ecosystems.
    

### Ozone layer vs. greenhouse effect

|Ozone depletion|Greenhouse effect|
|---|---|
|Stratospheric ozone loss increases harmful UV exposure|Greenhouse gases retain heat in the climate system|
|Historically linked strongly to CFCs and related ozone-depleting substances|Driven by increased greenhouse gases, especially from human activities|
|Montreal Protocol is the key international agreement on ozone-depleting substances|Paris Agreement is a key international climate agreement|

**Exam trap:** The ozone layer is beneficial because it absorbs much of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet radiation. It is not the same thing as the greenhouse effect.

### D. Waste management and environmental days

- **Reduce, Reuse, Recycle:** The familiar 3Rs of waste management.
    
- **Biodegradable waste:** Can be broken down by biological processes under suitable conditions.
    
- **Non-biodegradable waste:** Persists or decomposes very slowly under ordinary environmental conditions.
    
- **Eutrophication:** Excess nutrients stimulate algal growth and can reduce dissolved oxygen in water.
    
- **Acid rain:** Associated mainly with sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides reacting in the atmosphere.
    

### Important environmental days

|22 March|World Water Day|
|22 April|Earth Day|

### B. Everyday physics

- **Pressure cooker:** Higher pressure raises the boiling point of water, allowing food to cook at a higher temperature.
    
- **Refrigerator:** Transfers heat from the interior to the surroundings using a refrigeration cycle.
    
- **Electric fuse:** Melts under excessive current to help interrupt a circuit.
    
- **Seat belt:** Reduces injury risk by restraining occupants during sudden deceleration.
    
- **Airbag:** Inflates during certain collisions to help reduce impact forces on occupants.
    
- **Rear-view mirror:** Uses a convex mirror to provide a wider field of view.
    
- **Rainbow:** Involves refraction, dispersion and internal reflection of sunlight in water droplets.
    
- **Lightning:** An electrical discharge; thunder is caused by rapid heating and expansion of air.
    
- **Solar cell:** Converts light energy directly into electrical energy.
    
- **LED:** Emits light when current flows through a suitable semiconductor junction.

### D. Everyday chemistry and materials

- Stainless steel resists corrosion because of its protective chromium-rich surface layer.
    
- Baking powder releases carbon dioxide during baking through chemical reactions, helping dough rise.
    
- Fermentation by microorganisms is used in making foods such as curd and bread.
    
- Soap helps remove grease by forming structures that allow oily material to mix with water.
    
- Activated charcoal is used in some filtration and adsorption applications.
    
- Chlorination is used to disinfect drinking water; the correct dose and contact time matter.
    
- LPG is odorised so leaks can be detected more easily.
    

### E. Technology and health

- Prolonged close screen use can contribute to eye strain and discomfort.
    
- Poor posture can contribute to neck, back and wrist discomfort.
    
- UV radiation can damage skin and eyes; protection depends on exposure conditions.
    
- Excessive noise exposure can damage hearing.
    
- A balanced diet, physical activity, sleep and good hygiene support general health.

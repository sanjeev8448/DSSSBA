

## 1. Basic Time, Speed and Distance

The three fundamental formulas are:

- Speed = Distance / Time
    
- Distance = Speed × Time
    
- Time = Distance / Speed
    

### Example 1: Find Speed

A car covers 180 km in 3 hours.

Speed = 180 / 3 = 60 km/h

**Answer: 60 km/h**

### Example 2: Find Distance

A bike travels at 45 km/h for 4 hours.

Distance = 45 × 4 = 180 km

**Answer: 180 km**

### Example 3: Find Time

A bus covers 240 km at 60 km/h.

Time = 240 / 60 = 4 hours

**Answer: 4 hours**

### Unit Conversion

Memorise these conversions:

- km/h to m/s: Multiply by 5 / 18.
    
- m/s to km/h: Multiply by 18 / 5.
    

|Speed in km/h|Speed in m/s|
|---|---|
|18|5|
|36|10|
|54|15|
|72|20|
|90|25|
|108|30|

Example: Convert 72 km/h to m/s.

Speed = 72 × (5 / 18) = 20 m/s

**Answer: 20 m/s**

**Exam trap:** If distance is given in metres and time in seconds, calculate speed in m/s. Convert only when necessary.

## 2. Relative Speed

Relative speed is the speed at which the distance between two moving objects changes.

### Case A: Moving in Opposite Directions

Add their speeds.

Relative speed = Speed 1 + Speed 2

Example: Two cars move towards each other at 50 km/h and 70 km/h. They are 360 km apart. How long until they meet?

Relative speed = 50 + 70 = 120 km/h

Time = 360 / 120 = 3 hours

**Answer: 3 hours**

### Case B: Moving in the Same Direction

Subtract their speeds.

Relative speed = Absolute difference between the two speeds

Example: A car moving at 80 km/h chases another car moving at 60 km/h. The gap is 40 km.

Relative speed = 80 − 60 = 20 km/h

Time = 40 / 20 = 2 hours

**Answer: 2 hours**

### Case C: Catch-Up and Meeting Problems

- Moving towards each other: Add speeds.
    
- Moving in the same direction: Subtract speeds.
    
- Catch-up time = Initial gap / Relative speed.
    

**Exam trap:** Do not add speeds when two vehicles travel in the same direction and one is chasing the other.

## 3. Trains

Train questions commonly involve crossing a pole, a platform, another train, or a person.

### Case A: Train Crossing a Pole or Stationary Person

The distance covered is the length of the train.

Time = Train length / Speed

Example: A train 150 m long travels at 54 km/h. How long does it take to cross a pole?

Convert speed:

54 × (5 / 18) = 15 m/s

Time = 150 / 15 = 10 seconds

**Answer: 10 seconds**

### Case B: Train Crossing a Platform

The train must cover its own length plus the platform's length.

Time = (Train length + Platform length) / Speed

Example: A 200 m train crosses a 100 m platform at 72 km/h.

Speed = 72 × (5 / 18) = 20 m/s

Total distance = 200 + 100 = 300 m

Time = 300 / 20 = 15 seconds

**Answer: 15 seconds**

### Case C: Two Trains Crossing Each Other

Add their lengths and use relative speed.

For opposite directions:

Time = (Length 1 + Length 2) / (Speed 1 + Speed 2)

For the same direction:

Time = (Length 1 + Length 2) / Absolute difference between speeds

Speeds must be in m/s when lengths are in metres and time is required in seconds.

Example: Two trains of lengths 120 m and 180 m travel in opposite directions at 36 km/h and 54 km/h.

Total length = 120 + 180 = 300 m

Relative speed = 10 + 15 = 25 m/s

Time = 300 / 25 = 12 seconds

**Answer: 12 seconds**

### Remember

- Crossing a pole: Use one train's length.
    
- Crossing a platform: Use train length + platform length.
    
- Crossing another train: Use the sum of both train lengths.
    

## 4. Boats and Streams

Boat-and-stream questions involve a boat moving in still water and the speed of the water current.

- Downstream: The boat moves with the current.
    
- Upstream: The boat moves against the current.
    

Let:

- B = Boat speed in still water
    
- S = Stream speed
    
- D = Downstream speed
    
- U = Upstream speed
    

### Formulas

Downstream speed = B + S

Upstream speed = B − S

Boat speed in still water = (D + U) / 2

Stream speed = (D − U) / 2

### Example 1: Find Both Speeds

A boat travels downstream at 18 km/h and upstream at 10 km/h.

Boat speed in still water = (18 + 10) / 2 = 14 km/h

Stream speed = (18 − 10) / 2 = 4 km/h

**Answer: Boat speed = 14 km/h; Stream speed = 4 km/h**

### Example 2: Calculate Travel Time

A boat's speed in still water is 12 km/h and the stream speed is 3 km/h. How long does it take to travel 45 km downstream?

Downstream speed = 12 + 3 = 15 km/h

Time = 45 / 15 = 3 hours

**Answer: 3 hours**

For the same 45 km upstream:

Upstream speed = 12 − 3 = 9 km/h

Time = 45 / 9 = 5 hours

**Answer: 5 hours**

**Exam trap:** The current increases downstream speed but decreases upstream speed. Under the usual model, upstream speed must be positive for the boat to move against the current.

## 5. Average Speed

Average speed is the total distance divided by total time.

Average speed = Total distance / Total time

Do not automatically take the arithmetic mean of two speeds.

### Case A: Equal Distances at Different Speeds

If a person travels equal distances at speeds u and v:

Average speed = (2 × u × v) / (u + v)

Example: A car travels half the distance at 40 km/h and the other half at 60 km/h. Find its average speed.

Average speed = (2 × 40 × 60) / (40 + 60)

= 4,800 / 100

= 48 km/h

**Answer: 48 km/h**

It is not 50 km/h.

### Case B: Equal Time at Different Speeds

If a person travels for equal amounts of time at speeds u and v:

Average speed = (u + v) / 2

Example: A car travels for 2 hours at 40 km/h and 2 hours at 60 km/h.

Average speed = (40 + 60) / 2 = 50 km/h

**Answer: 50 km/h**

### Case C: Different Distances and Times

A person travels 120 km in 2 hours and another 180 km in 3 hours.

Total distance = 120 + 180 = 300 km

Total time = 2 + 3 = 5 hours

Average speed = 300 / 5 = 60 km/h

**Answer: 60 km/h**

### Quick Comparison

|Situation|Correct method|
|---|---|
|Equal distances|Average speed = (2 × u × v) / (u + v)|
|Equal time intervals|Average speed = (u + v) / 2|
|Different distances and times|Total distance / Total time|

**Memory trick:** Equal distance means use the product formula. Equal time means use the ordinary average.

## 6. Circular Track

Circular-track questions usually involve two runners starting at the same point and moving around a track of fixed length.

Let L = Track length.

### Case A: Running in the Same Direction

The faster runner catches the slower runner when the faster runner gains one complete lap.

Time for first catch = Track length / Absolute difference between speeds

Example: Two runners run around a 400 m track at 6 m/s and 4 m/s in the same direction. When will the faster runner first catch the slower runner?

Relative speed = 6 − 4 = 2 m/s

Time = 400 / 2 = 200 seconds

**Answer: 200 seconds**

### Case B: Running in Opposite Directions

They meet when their combined distance equals one track length.

Time for first meeting = Track length / (Speed 1 + Speed 2)

Example: Two runners move in opposite directions on a 600 m track at 5 m/s and 7 m/s.

Time = 600 / (5 + 7)

= 600 / 12

= 50 seconds

**Answer: 50 seconds**

### Case C: Number of Meetings or Catches

When two runners start together from the same point:

- Same direction: Relative speed is the difference between their speeds.
    
- Opposite directions: Relative speed is the sum of their speeds.
    

The first catch or meeting is calculated as above. For later meetings, multiply the first-meeting time by the required lap or meeting count, subject to the starting conditions in the question.

**Exam trap:** In circular-track problems, use the track's circumference as the relative distance for the first catch or meeting, not the diameter or radius.

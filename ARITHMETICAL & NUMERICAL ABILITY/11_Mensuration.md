
## 1. Basic Concepts and Units

|Quantity|Meaning|Units|
|---|---|---|
|Perimeter|Total length around a 2D shape|cm, m|
|Area|Space covered by a 2D shape|cm², m²|
|Curved Surface Area (CSA)|Area of the curved outer surface|cm², m²|
|Total Surface Area (TSA)|Area of all outer surfaces|cm², m²|
|Volume|Space occupied by a 3D object|cm³, m³|

### Conversions to remember

- 1 m = 100 cm
    
- 1 m² = 10,000 cm²
    
- 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³
    
- 1 litre = 1,000 cm³
    
- 1 m³ = 1,000 litres
    

Use π = 22/7 or 3.14, according to the question.

**Important exam trap:** Length uses linear units, area uses square units, and volume uses cubic units.

## 2. Square

All four sides of a square are equal.

Let the side be a.

|Quantity|Formula|
|---|---|
|Perimeter|4a|
|Area|a²|
|Diagonal|a√2|

### Worked example

A square has a side of 8 cm.

- Perimeter = 4 × 8 = 32 cm
    
- Area = 8² = 64 cm²
    
- Diagonal = 8√2 cm
    

**Shortcut:** If the area is 144 cm², the side is √144 = 12 cm.

## 3. Rectangle

Opposite sides of a rectangle are equal.

Let length = l and breadth = b.

|Quantity|Formula|
|---|---|
|Perimeter|2(l + b)|
|Area|l × b|
|Diagonal|√(l² + b²)|

### Worked example

Length = 12 cm, breadth = 5 cm.

- Perimeter = 2(12 + 5) = 34 cm
    
- Area = 12 × 5 = 60 cm²
    
- Diagonal = √(12² + 5²) = √169 = 13 cm
    

**Exam trap:** Perimeter is measured in linear units; area is measured in square units.

## 4. Triangle

The height of a triangle is perpendicular to its base.

|Quantity|Formula|
|---|---|
|Perimeter|a + b + c|
|Area|1/2 × base × height|
|Semi-perimeter|s = (a + b + c)/2|
|Heron's formula|Area = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)]|

### Worked example

A triangle has a base of 10 cm and a height of 6 cm.

Area = 1/2 × 10 × 6

**Answer: 30 cm²**

If all three sides are known, Heron's formula calculates the area without requiring the height.

### Special triangles

|Type|Formula|
|---|---|
|Equilateral triangle, side a|Area = (√3/4) × a²|
|Equilateral triangle|Perimeter = 3a|
|Right-angled triangle|a² + b² = c²|

### Example: Equilateral triangle

Side = 4 cm.

Area = (√3/4) × 4²

= (√3/4) × 16

**Answer: 4√3 cm²**

## 5. Circle

Diameter d = 2r, where r is the radius.

|Quantity|Formula|
|---|---|
|Diameter|2r|
|Circumference|2πr = πd|
|Area|πr²|
|Semicircle area|1/2 × πr²|
|Semicircle perimeter|πr + 2r|

### Worked example

Radius = 7 cm. Use π = 22/7.

Circumference = 2 × (22/7) × 7

**Answer: 44 cm**

Area = (22/7) × 7²

**Answer: 154 cm²**

**Important:** The perimeter of a semicircle includes the straight diameter. Its curved portion alone is πr.

## 6. Cuboid

A cuboid is a box-shaped solid with length, breadth and height.

Let length = l, breadth = b and height = h.

|Quantity|Formula|
|---|---|
|Volume|lbh|
|Lateral Surface Area (LSA)|2h(l + b)|
|Total Surface Area (TSA)|2(lb + bh + hl)|
|Space diagonal|√(l² + b² + h²)|

### Worked example

Length = 5 cm, breadth = 3 cm, height = 4 cm.

- Volume = 5 × 3 × 4 = 60 cm³
    
- LSA = 2 × 4 × (5 + 3) = 64 cm²
    
- TSA = 2(15 + 12 + 20) = 94 cm²
    
- Space diagonal = √(25 + 9 + 16) = √50 = 5√2 cm
    

**Exam trap:** LSA excludes the top and bottom faces. TSA includes all six faces.

## 7. Cylinder

A cylinder has two circular ends and one curved surface.

Let radius = r and height = h.

|Quantity|Formula|
|---|---|
|Volume|πr²h|
|Curved Surface Area (CSA)|2πrh|
|Lateral Surface Area (LSA)|2πrh|
|Total Surface Area (TSA)|2πr(h + r)|

### Worked example

Radius = 7 cm and height = 10 cm. Use π = 22/7.

**Volume**

V = (22/7) × 7² × 10

**Answer: 1,540 cm³**

**Curved surface area**

CSA = 2 × (22/7) × 7 × 10

**Answer: 440 cm²**

**Total surface area**

TSA = 440 + 2 × (22/7) × 49

**Answer: 748 cm²**

### Open cylinder

If a cylinder is open at the top, its surface area is the curved surface area plus the area of one circular base.

Open-top surface area = 2πrh + πr²

## 8. Cone

A cone has a circular base and a curved surface.

The slant height l is different from the vertical height h.

For a right circular cone:

**Slant height: l = √(r² + h²)**

|Quantity|Formula|
|---|---|
|Volume|(1/3)πr²h|
|Curved Surface Area (CSA)|πrl|
|Total Surface Area (TSA)|πr(l + r)|

### Worked example

Radius = 3 cm, vertical height = 4 cm.

First, calculate the slant height.

l = √(3² + 4²)

= √25

= 5 cm

**Volume**

V = (1/3) × π × 3² × 4

**Answer: 12π cm³**

**Curved surface area**

CSA = π × 3 × 5

**Answer: 15π cm²**

**Total surface area**

TSA = π × 3 × (5 + 3)

**Answer: 24π cm²**

**Exam trap:** Cone volume uses vertical height, whereas its curved surface area uses slant height.

## 9. Sphere and Hemisphere

### A. Sphere

A sphere has one continuous curved surface and no flat faces.

Let radius = r.

|Quantity|Formula|
|---|---|
|Volume|(4/3)πr³|
|Surface area|4πr²|

### Worked example

Radius = 3 cm.

Volume = (4/3) × π × 3³

**Answer: 36π cm³**

Surface area = 4π × 3²

**Answer: 36π cm²**

### B. Hemisphere

A hemisphere is half of a sphere.

|Quantity|Formula|
|---|---|
|Volume|(2/3)πr³|
|Curved Surface Area (CSA)|2πr²|
|Total Surface Area (TSA)|3πr²|

**Exam trap:** The hemisphere's total surface area includes its flat circular base. Its curved surface area does not.

## 10. Complete Formula Sheet

|Shape|Area or surface area|Volume|
|---|---|---|
|Square|Area = a²|—|
|Rectangle|Area = lb|—|
|Triangle|Area = 1/2 × bh|—|
|Circle|Area = πr²|—|
|Cuboid|TSA = 2(lb + bh + hl)|lbh|
|Cylinder|CSA = 2πrh; TSA = 2πr(h + r)|πr²h|
|Cone|CSA = πrl; TSA = πr(l + r)|(1/3)πr²h|
|Sphere|4πr²|(4/3)πr³|
|Hemisphere|TSA = 3πr²|(2/3)πr³|

### Three formulas you must not confuse

1. **Circle:** Area = πr², whereas circumference = 2πr.
    
2. **Cylinder and cone:** Cylinder volume = πr²h; cone volume = (1/3)πr²h.
    
3. **Sphere:** Surface area = 4πr²; volume = (4/3)πr³.
    

## 11. Common DSSSB-Style Word Problems

### Problem A: Painting a room

A room is 5 m long, 4 m wide and 3 m high. Find the area of its four walls, ignoring doors and windows.

Area of four walls = 2h(l + b)

= 2 × 3 × (5 + 4)

= 6 × 9

**Answer: 54 m²**

### Problem B: Filling a tank

A cuboid tank is 2 m long, 1.5 m wide and 1 m high. Find its capacity in litres.

Volume = 2 × 1.5 × 1

= 3 m³

Since 1 m³ = 1,000 litres:

Capacity = 3 × 1,000

**Answer: 3,000 litres**

### Problem C: Melting and recasting

A solid sphere is melted and recast into smaller spheres of the same material without any loss. The total volume remains constant.

If one sphere of radius R produces n spheres of radius r:

(4/3)πR³ = n × (4/3)πr³

Therefore:

**n = (R/r)³**

**Example:** A sphere of radius 6 cm is recast into spheres of radius 2 cm.

n = (6/2)³

= 3³

**Answer: 27 smaller spheres**

### Problem D: Cost of painting

If the area to be painted is 80 m² and the cost is ₹15 per m²:

Total cost = 80 × 15

**Answer: ₹1,200**

**Exam tip:** Always identify which surfaces are included before calculating the area.

## 12. Final Revision: Common Exam Traps

1. Convert all measurements to compatible units before calculating.
    
2. Use square units for area and cubic units for volume.
    
3. For a semicircle's perimeter, add the diameter to the curved length.
    
4. For a cuboid's four walls, use 2h(l + b), not its total surface area.
    
5. For an open-top cylinder, exclude the top circular base.
    
6. For a cone, distinguish between vertical height and slant height.
    
7. For a hemisphere, TSA includes the circular base.
    
8. For melting and recasting, equate the original volume to the combined volume of the new objects.
    
9. For painting or polishing, multiply the relevant surface area by the cost per unit area.
    
10. Check whether the question asks for CSA, LSA, TSA, area or volume before selecting a formula.

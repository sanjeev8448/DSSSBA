
## 1. BODMAS Rule

BODMAS tells you the order in which mathematical operations should be performed.

|Letter|Meaning|Rule|
|---|---|---|
|B|Brackets|Solve expressions inside brackets first|
|O|Orders|Powers, roots and exponents|
|D / M|Division and Multiplication|Same priority; calculate from left to right|
|A / S|Addition and Subtraction|Same priority; calculate from left to right|

**Important exam trap:** Multiplication does not always come before division, and addition does not always come before subtraction. Operations at the same priority are performed from left to right.

### Worked examples

**Example 1**

18 + 6 × 4 − 8 ÷ 2

First perform multiplication and division from left to right.

= 18 + 24 − 4

= 42 − 4

**Answer: 38**

**Example 2**

(12 + 8) ÷ 4 × 3

Solve the brackets first.

= 20 ÷ 4 × 3

= 5 × 3

**Answer: 15**

**Example 3**

2 + 3² × 2

Power first, then multiplication.

= 2 + 9 × 2

= 2 + 18

**Answer: 20**

**Example 4: Nested brackets**

24 ÷ [2 × (3 + 1)]

= 24 ÷ [2 × 4]

= 24 ÷ 8

**Answer: 3**

## 2. Fractions

A fraction is written as a/b, where b ≠ 0.

Here, a is the numerator and b is the denominator.

### A. Addition and subtraction

When the denominators are the same, add or subtract the numerators.

Example:

3/7 + 2/7 = 5/7

When the denominators differ, find their LCM and make the denominators equal.

**Addition example**

2/3 + 1/4

LCM of 3 and 4 = 12.

= 8/12 + 3/12

**Answer: 11/12**

**Subtraction example**

5/6 − 1/4

LCM of 6 and 4 = 12.

= 10/12 − 3/12

**Answer: 7/12**

### B. Multiplication

Multiply the numerators together and the denominators together.

Example:

3/5 × 10/9 = 30/45

Simplify by dividing the numerator and denominator by 15.

**Answer: 2/3**

**Shortcut:** Cancel common factors before multiplying to reduce calculations.

### C. Division

Keep the first fraction, change division to multiplication, and take the reciprocal of the second fraction.

Example:

3/4 ÷ 2/5

= 3/4 × 5/2

**Answer: 15/8**

**Remember:** Never forget to invert the second fraction, not the first.

### D. Mixed fractions

Convert a mixed fraction into an improper fraction.

Formula:

Mixed fraction a b/c = (a × c + b)/c

Example:

2 1/3 = (2 × 3 + 1)/3 = 7/3

**Worked example**

2 1/3 + 1 1/2

= 7/3 + 3/2

= 14/6 + 9/6

**Answer: 23/6**, or 3 5/6.

### E. Comparing fractions

For fractions with positive denominators, cross-multiply.

Compare 5/7 and 3/4.

5 × 4 = 20

3 × 7 = 21

Since 21 > 20:

**3/4 > 5/7**

## 3. Decimals

### A. Decimal addition and subtraction

Align the decimal points before calculating. Add zeros when necessary.

**Addition**

12.45 + 3.7

= 12.45 + 3.70

**Answer: 16.15**

**Subtraction**

15.2 − 7.85

= 15.20 − 7.85

**Answer: 7.35**

### B. Decimal multiplication

Multiply as whole numbers, then place the decimal point using the total number of decimal places in both factors.

Example:

2.4 × 1.5

24 × 15 = 360

There are two decimal places in total.

**Answer: 3.60 = 3.6**

### C. Decimal division

Move the decimal point in both numbers by the same number of places until the divisor becomes a whole number.

Example:

4.8 ÷ 0.6

= 48 ÷ 6

**Answer: 8**

### D. Fraction-to-decimal conversions to remember

|Fraction|Decimal|
|---|---|
|1/2|0.5|
|1/4|0.25|
|3/4|0.75|
|1/5|0.2|
|1/8|0.125|
|1/10|0.1|
|1/20|0.05|
|1/25|0.04|

### E. Decimal and percentage connection

To convert a decimal to a percentage, multiply by 100.

0.35 × 100 = **35%**

To convert a percentage to a decimal, divide by 100.

18% ÷ 100 = **0.18**

## 4. Surds and Basic Operations

A surd is an irrational root expression that cannot be simplified to a rational number.

Examples: √2 and √3.

However, √9 = 3 is not a surd because its value is rational.

### A. Perfect squares to memorise

|Number|Square|Number|Square|
|---|---|---|---|
|1|1|6|36|
|2|4|7|49|
|3|9|8|64|
|4|16|9|81|
|5|25|10|100|
|11|121|15|225|
|12|144|20|400|
|13|169|25|625|

**Exam tip:** Memorise squares from 1 to 30 for faster calculations.

### B. Simplifying square roots

Take out the largest perfect-square factor.

**Example 1**

√72 = √(36 × 2)

= √36 × √2

**Answer: 6√2**

**Example 2**

√50 = √(25 × 2)

= √25 × √2

**Answer: 5√2**

### C. Addition and subtraction of surds

Only like surds can be combined.

Example:

3√2 + 5√2 = **8√2**

But 3√2 + 2√3 cannot be combined into a single like term.

**Worked example**

√12 + √27

= √(4 × 3) + √(9 × 3)

= 2√3 + 3√3

**Answer: 5√3**

### D. Multiplication and division

For non-negative a and b:

√a × √b = √(a × b)

**Example 1**

√3 × √12 = √36

**Answer: 6**

**Example 2**

√48 ÷ √3 = √(48 ÷ 3)

= √16

**Answer: 4**

### E. Basic identities

- (√a)² = a, for a ≥ 0.
    
- √a × √a = a, for a ≥ 0.
    
- (a + b)(a − b) = a² − b².
    

**Common trap:**

√(a + b) ≠ √a + √b in general.

Example:

√(9 + 16) = √25 = 5

But:

√9 + √16 = 3 + 4 = 7

Therefore, the two expressions are not equal.

## 5. Approximation

Approximation means replacing an exact value with a nearby, simpler value to calculate quickly or estimate an answer.

### A. Rounding rules

- If the next digit is 0–4, keep the rounding digit unchanged.
    
- If the next digit is 5–9, increase the rounding digit by 1.
    

### Examples

|Number|Nearest integer|Rounded to 2 decimal places|
|---|---|---|
|7.43|7|7.43|
|7.68|8|7.68|
|12.756|13|12.76|
|0.846|1|0.85|

**Important:** When rounding to two decimal places, inspect the third decimal digit.

Example: 12.756 becomes 12.76 because the third decimal digit is 6.

### B. Estimate calculations

Example:

49.8 × 20.2

Approximate:

49.8 ≈ 50

20.2 ≈ 20

Therefore:

50 × 20 = **1,000**

The exact value is 1,005.96, so 1,000 is a good estimate.

### C. Approximate square roots

Estimate √70.

We know:

8² = 64

9² = 81

Therefore:

8 < √70 < 9

Since 70 is closer to 64 than to 81, √70 is a little above 8.

More precisely:

**√70 ≈ 8.37**

### D. Choosing the nearest option

Suppose a question asks for an approximate value of:

(398 × 51) ÷ 20.1

Round to convenient numbers:

398 ≈ 400

51 ≈ 50

20.1 ≈ 20

Then:

(400 × 50) ÷ 20

= 20,000 ÷ 20

**Estimated answer: 1,000**

Choose the option nearest to 1,000.

Approximation is useful when the question explicitly asks for an estimate or when the answer options are widely spaced. Use exact calculation if the options are close.

## 7. Final Exam Traps

1. **BODMAS:** Division and multiplication have equal priority. Work from left to right.
    
2. **Fractions:** Do not add denominators directly when adding fractions.
    
3. **Division of fractions:** Take the reciprocal of the second fraction.
    
4. **Decimals:** Align decimal points during addition and subtraction.
    
5. **Surds:** √a + √b is not generally equal to √(a + b).
    
6. **Like surds:** Simplify roots first before deciding whether they can be combined.
    
7. **Rounding:** Inspect the digit immediately after the required rounding place.
    
8. **Approximation:** Use exact calculation if answer options are close together.

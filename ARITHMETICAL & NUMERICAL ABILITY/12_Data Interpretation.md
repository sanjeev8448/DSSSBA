
## 1. Tables

Tables present numerical data in rows and columns. Before calculating, always read the heading, units, row labels and column labels.

Consider the following table showing student enrolment in five courses.

|Course|Boys|Girls|Total|
|---|---|---|---|
|A|40|60|100|
|B|50|70|120|
|C|60|40|100|
|D|30|50|80|
|E|70|30|100|
|**Total**|**250**|**250**|**500**|

### Common question types

**Q1. What is the total number of students in courses B and D?**

120 + 80 = **200 students**

**Q2. What percentage of students in course A are girls?**

Percentage = (Girls in A ÷ Total students in A) × 100

= (60 ÷ 100) × 100

**Answer: 60%**

**Q3. Find the ratio of boys to girls in course C.**

60 : 40 = **3 : 2**

**Q4. What is the average number of students per course?**

Average = Total students ÷ Number of courses

= 500 ÷ 5

**Answer: 100 students**

**Exam tip:** Do not confuse a row total with a column total. If values are given in thousands or lakhs, retain the stated units throughout the calculation.

## 2. Bar Graphs

A bar graph uses bars to compare categories. Always check the vertical-axis scale carefully. Each marking might represent 10, 50, 100 or another interval.

Consider this illustrative data showing books sold by a shop.

|Month|Books sold|
|---|---|
|January|100|
|February|150|
|March|120|
|April|200|
|May|180|

### Worked questions

**Q1. In which month were the highest sales recorded?**

April, with 200 books.

**Q2. Find the total sales.**

100 + 150 + 120 + 200 + 180 = **750 books**

**Q3. Find the difference between April and January.**

200 − 100 = **100 books**

**Q4. Calculate the percentage increase from January to April.**

Percentage increase = [(New value − Original value) ÷ Original value] × 100

= [(200 − 100) ÷ 100] × 100

**Answer: 100%**

**Common trap:** Percentage increase uses the original value as the denominator, not the final value.

## 3. Pie Charts

A pie chart represents a total as a circle of 360°. Each sector represents a share of the total.

### Essential formulas

Sector angle = (Category value ÷ Total value) × 360°

Category value = (Sector angle ÷ 360°) × Total value

### Worked example

Suppose the total monthly household expenditure is ₹20,000, distributed as follows.

|Category|Percentage|
|---|---|
|Rent|35%|
|Food|30%|
|Education|15%|
|Transport|10%|
|Other expenses|10%|
|**Total**|**100%**|

**Q1. How much is spent on food?**

Food expenditure = 30% of ₹20,000

= 30/100 × 20,000

**Answer: ₹6,000**

**Q2. What is the angle of the rent sector?**

Angle = 35/100 × 360°

**Answer: 126°**

**Q3. What is the ratio of education to transport expenditure?**

15 : 10 = **3 : 2**

### Percentage-to-angle conversions

|Percentage|Pie-chart angle|
|---|---|
|10%|36°|
|12.5%|45°|
|20%|72°|
|25%|90°|
|30%|108°|
|50%|180°|
|75%|270°|
|100%|360°|

**Exam tip:** If the question gives the sector angle instead of the percentage, divide the angle by 360° and multiply by the total.

## 4. Line Graphs

A line graph shows how a quantity changes over time. It is useful for comparing sales, production, population, income or marks across different years.

Consider this illustrative production data.

|Year|Production (units)|
|---|---|
|2021|100|
|2022|140|
|2023|120|
|2024|180|
|2025|220|

### Worked questions

**Q1. In which year was production highest?**

2025, with 220 units.

**Q2. Find the increase from 2021 to 2025.**

220 − 100 = **120 units**

**Q3. Calculate the percentage increase.**

Percentage increase = [(220 − 100) ÷ 100] × 100

**Answer: 120%**

**Q4. Find the average annual production.**

Average = (100 + 140 + 120 + 180 + 220) ÷ 5

= 760 ÷ 5

**Answer: 152 units**

**Exam tip:** A line graph may contain multiple lines for different companies. Compare the correct line and year before using the values.

## 5. Percentage-Based DI

Percentage-based Data Interpretation asks you to calculate a percentage of a total, compare values, or find percentage increases and decreases.

### Essential formulas

Percentage = (Part ÷ Total) × 100

Percentage change = [(New value − Original value) ÷ Original value] × 100

- A positive result indicates an increase.
    
- A negative result indicates a decrease.
    

### Worked example

A company produces 800 units in 2024 and 1,000 units in 2025.

**Q1. What is the percentage increase?**

Percentage increase = [(1,000 − 800) ÷ 800] × 100

= (200 ÷ 800) × 100

**Answer: 25%**

**Q2. What percentage of the 2025 production was produced in 2024?**

= (800 ÷ 1,000) × 100

**Answer: 80%**

The answers differ because the denominators are different.

**Q3. Production decreases from 1,000 to 800 units. What is the percentage decrease?**

Percentage decrease = [(1,000 − 800) ÷ 1,000] × 100

**Answer: 20%**

An increase from 800 to 1,000 is 25%, but a decrease from 1,000 to 800 is 20%. The percentage depends on the original value.

### Mental calculation shortcuts

|Percentage|Quick method|
|---|---|
|10%|Divide by 10|
|5%|Divide by 20|
|25%|Divide by 4|
|50%|Divide by 2|
|75%|Multiply by 3/4|
|12.5%|Divide by 8|
|20%|Divide by 5|

## 6. Average-Based DI

Average-based DI asks for the mean of values shown in a table or graph.

**Formula:**

Average = Sum of values ÷ Number of values

### Worked example

A shop's sales over four days are 120, 150, 180 and 150 items.

Average = (120 + 150 + 180 + 150) ÷ 4

= 600 ÷ 4

**Answer: 150 items**

### Missing-value questions

The average production of five factories is 80 units. Four factories produce 60, 70, 90 and 100 units. Find the production of the fifth factory.

First calculate the total production.

Total = Average × Number of observations

= 80 × 5

= 400 units

Subtract the known values.

Fifth factory = 400 − (60 + 70 + 90 + 100)

= 400 − 320

**Answer: 80 units**

**Shortcut:** Total = Average × Number of observations. This is often faster than adding or subtracting individual values first.

## 7. Ratio-Based DI

Ratio-based DI compares quantities expressed in the same units.

Suppose a company has 240 male employees and 160 female employees.

### A. Simplifying a ratio

240 : 160

Divide both terms by their HCF, 80.

**Answer: 3 : 2**

### B. Finding quantities from a ratio

The ratio of boys to girls is 3 : 2, and the total number of students is 250.

Total parts = 3 + 2 = 5

One part = 250 ÷ 5 = 50

Therefore:

- Boys = 3 × 50 = 150
    
- Girls = 2 × 50 = 100
    

**Answer: 150 boys and 100 girls**

### C. Comparing ratios

Company A sells 180 units and Company B sells 240 units.

A : B = 180 : 240

Divide both terms by 60.

**Answer: 3 : 4**

### D. Ratio and percentage connection

If boys and girls are in the ratio 3 : 2, the percentage of boys is:

Percentage of boys = [3 ÷ (3 + 2)] × 100

= 3/5 × 100

**Answer: 60%**

**Common trap:** For a ratio a : b, the percentage represented by the first part is [a ÷ (a + b)] × 100, not (a ÷ b) × 100.

## 9. Final Revision Sheet

|DI type|Key formula or method|
|---|---|
|Tables|Read row labels, column labels and units|
|Bar graphs|Compare categories using the correct axis scale|
|Pie charts|Sector angle = (Category value ÷ Total) × 360°|
|Percentage|(Part ÷ Total) × 100|
|Percentage increase|[(New − Original) ÷ Original] × 100|
|Percentage decrease|[(Original − New) ÷ Original] × 100|
|Average|Sum ÷ Number of observations|
|Missing average value|Total = Average × Number of observations|
|Ratio|Simplify by dividing terms by their HCF|
|Quantity from ratio|Divide total by total ratio parts, then multiply by the required part|

**Final exam tips**

1. Read the units and graph scale before calculating.
    
2. Identify whether the question asks for a total, difference, average, ratio or percentage.
    
3. For percentage change, use the original value as the denominator.
    
4. For pie charts, remember that the complete circle represents 360°.
    
5. For ratio questions, add the ratio parts when finding a share of the total.
    
6. Use approximation for speed only when the answer options allow it.

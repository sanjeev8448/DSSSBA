# ARITHMETICAL & NUMERICAL ABILITY — PERCENTAGE

## 1. Basic Percentage

**Definition:** Percentage means “per hundred.” The symbol is %.

Formula: x% = x / 100

Example: 25% = 25 / 100 = 1/4

### Important Percentage Conversions

|Percentage|Fraction|Decimal|
|---|---|---|
|10%|1/10|0.10|
|12.5%|1/8|0.125|
|20%|1/5|0.20|
|25%|1/4|0.25|
|33⅓%|1/3|0.333...|
|40%|2/5|0.40|
|50%|1/2|0.50|
|60%|3/5|0.60|
|75%|3/4|0.75|
|80%|4/5|0.80|

### Formula 1: Find a Percentage of a Number

Formula: x% of N = (x / 100) × N

Example: Find 25% of 640.

Calculation: (25 / 100) × 640 = 160

Answer: 160

Shortcut: Since 25% = 1/4, calculate 640 ÷ 4 = 160.

### Formula 2: Find What Percentage One Number Is of Another

Formula: Percentage = (Part / Total) × 100

Example: 45 is what percentage of 180?

Calculation: (45 / 180) × 100 = 25%

Answer: 25%

### Formula 3: Find the Original Total

If 30% of a number is 150, find the number.

Formula: Original number = (Given value × 100) / Percentage

Calculation: (150 × 100) / 30 = 500

Answer: 500

**Exam trick:** Identify whether the question asks for the part, the percentage, or the original total.

## 2. Percentage Increase and Decrease

### A. Percentage Increase

Formula: Percentage increase = (Increase / Original value) × 100

New value = Original value × (1 + Rate / 100)

Example: A salary increases from ₹20,000 to ₹25,000.

Increase = 25,000 − 20,000 = ₹5,000

Percentage increase = (5,000 / 20,000) × 100 = 25%

Answer: 25% increase

### B. Percentage Decrease

Formula: Percentage decrease = (Decrease / Original value) × 100

New value = Original value × (1 − Rate / 100)

Example: A product's price falls from ₹800 to ₹680.

Decrease = 800 − 680 = ₹120

Percentage decrease = (120 / 800) × 100 = 15%

Answer: 15% decrease

### C. Successive Percentage Changes

Formula: Net change (%) = a + b + (a × b / 100)

Treat an increase as positive and a decrease as negative.

Example 1: Increase by 20%, then increase by 10%.

Net change = 20 + 10 + (20 × 10 / 100) = 32%

Answer: 32% increase

Check using ₹100:

- After 20% increase: ₹120
    
- After 10% increase: ₹132
    

Example 2: Increase by 20%, then decrease by 20%.

Net change = 20 − 20 + (20 × −20 / 100) = −4%

Answer: 4% decrease

Check using ₹100:

- After 20% increase: ₹120
    
- After 20% decrease: ₹96
    

**Important shortcut:** An increase of x% followed by a decrease of x% results in a net loss of x² / 100 percent.

Example: Increase by 10%, then decrease by 10%.

Net loss = 10² / 100 = 1%

## 3. Population Problems

### Population Growth

Formula: Population after n years = Initial population × (1 + Rate / 100)^n

Where:

- Initial population = population at the beginning
    
- Rate = annual growth percentage
    
- n = number of years
    

Example: A town has a population of 20,000. It grows by 10% per year. Find its population after two years.

Year 1: 20,000 × 1.10 = 22,000

Year 2: 22,000 × 1.10 = 24,200

Answer: 24,200

The second year's increase is calculated on 22,000, not the original 20,000.

### Population Decrease

Formula: Population after n years = Initial population × (1 − Rate / 100)^n

Example: A town has 50,000 people and its population decreases by 10% in one year.

Calculation: 50,000 × 0.90 = 45,000

Answer: 45,000

## 4. Income and Expenditure

Formula: Savings = Income − Expenditure

### Example 1: Income Increases

A person's monthly income is ₹30,000. It increases by 20%. Find the new income.

New income = 30,000 × 1.20 = ₹36,000

Answer: ₹36,000

### Example 2: Expenditure and Savings

A person earns ₹40,000 per month and spends 75% of their income. Find the monthly savings.

Expenditure = 40,000 × 75 / 100 = ₹30,000

Savings = 40,000 − 30,000 = ₹10,000

Answer: ₹10,000, which is 25% of income.

### Example 3: Income Increases but Expenditure Stays Constant

A person's income is ₹50,000 and expenditure is ₹40,000. Income increases by 20%, while expenditure remains unchanged. Find the percentage increase in savings.

Original savings = 50,000 − 40,000 = ₹10,000

New income = 50,000 × 1.20 = ₹60,000

New savings = 60,000 − 40,000 = ₹20,000

Increase in savings = 20,000 − 10,000 = ₹10,000

Percentage increase = (10,000 / 10,000) × 100 = 100%

Answer: 100% increase in savings.

**Exam trap:** A 20% increase in income does not necessarily mean a 20% increase in savings. Calculate savings before and after the change.

## 5. Marks-Based Percentage Questions

Formula: Percentage obtained = (Marks obtained / Maximum marks) × 100

### Example 1: Calculate Percentage

A student scores 420 out of 600.

Percentage = (420 / 600) × 100 = 70%

Answer: 70%

### Example 2: Marks Required to Pass

A student must score 40% of 500 marks to pass.

Passing marks = 500 × 40 / 100 = 200

If the student scores 175:

Shortfall = 200 − 175 = 25 marks

Answer: 25 marks short.

### Example 3: Additional Marks Required

A student scores 60% of 800 marks. How many additional marks are needed to reach 70%?

Marks scored = 800 × 60 / 100 = 480

Target marks = 800 × 70 / 100 = 560

Additional marks = 560 − 480 = 80

Answer: 80 marks.

**Important distinction:** An increase from 60% to 70% is an increase of 10 percentage points. Relative to the original 60%, the increase is (10 / 60) × 100 = 16.67%.

## 6. Election-Based Percentage Questions

Election questions involve votes polled, valid votes, rejected votes, and winning margins.

### Example 1: Two Candidates

In an election, candidate A receives 60% of 20,000 valid votes. Candidate B receives the remaining votes. Find the winning margin.

Candidate A's votes = 20,000 × 60 / 100 = 12,000

Candidate B's votes = 20,000 − 12,000 = 8,000

Winning margin = 12,000 − 8,000 = 4,000

Answer: 4,000 votes.

### Example 2: Rejected Votes

A total of 25,000 votes are polled. If 4% are invalid, how many valid votes remain?

Invalid votes = 25,000 × 4 / 100 = 1,000

Valid votes = 25,000 − 1,000 = 24,000

Answer: 24,000 valid votes.

### Example 3: Winner's Percentage of Valid Votes

A candidate receives 13,500 votes out of 24,000 valid votes.

Percentage = (13,500 / 24,000) × 100 = 56.25%

Answer: 56.25%

**Exam trap:** Check whether the percentage is calculated from total votes polled, valid votes, or votes received by a particular candidate.

## 7. Percentage Comparison

### Formula: A Is What Percentage More Than B?

Percentage more = ((A − B) / B) × 100

The denominator is B because B is the reference value.

Example: A earns ₹60,000 and B earns ₹50,000.

Percentage more = (10,000 / 50,000) × 100 = 20%

Answer: A earns 20% more than B.

### Formula: A Is What Percentage Less Than B?

Percentage less = ((B − A) / B) × 100

Example: A earns ₹40,000 and B earns ₹50,000.

Percentage less = (10,000 / 50,000) × 100 = 20%

Answer: A earns 20% less than B.

### Important Comparison Trap

If A is 25% more than B, B is not 25% less than A.

Suppose B = 100.

A = 100 + 25 = 125

B is less than A by:

(125 − 100) / 125 × 100 = 20%

Therefore:

- A is 25% more than B.
    
- B is 20% less than A.
    

### Percentage Comparison Shortcuts

If A is x% more than B:

Percentage by which B is less than A = [x / (100 + x)] × 100

If A is x% less than B:

Percentage by which B is more than A = [x / (100 − x)] × 100

Example: A is 20% less than B. By what percentage is B more than A?

Calculation = [20 / (100 − 20)] × 100 = 25%

Answer: 25%.

|Question type|Formula|

### Fast Mental Calculation Tricks

- **10%:** Divide by 10.
    
- **5%:** Find 10%, then divide by 2.
    
- **1%:** Divide by 100.
    
- **25%:** Divide by 4.
    
- **50%:** Divide by 2.
    
- **75%:** Find three-quarters.
    
- **12.5%:** Divide by 8.
    
- **33⅓%:** Divide by 3.
    
- **Useful shortcut:** x% of y = y% of x.
    

Example: 16% of 25 = 25% of 16 = 4.

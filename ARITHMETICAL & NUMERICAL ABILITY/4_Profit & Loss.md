
## 1. Cost Price (CP) and Selling Price (SP)

- **Cost Price (CP):** The price at which an item is purchased. If additional expenses such as transport or repair are included in the question, add them to the purchase cost when calculating the effective CP.
    
- **Selling Price (SP):** The price at which the item is sold.
    

### Basic Conditions

- Profit: SP > CP
    
- Loss: SP < CP
    
- No profit, no loss: SP = CP
    

### Example

A shopkeeper buys a bag for ₹800 and sells it for ₹950.

Profit = SP − CP = 950 − 800 = ₹150

If the same bag were sold for ₹720:

Loss = CP − SP = 800 − 720 = ₹80

## 2. Profit Percentage

Profit percentage is calculated on the cost price, not the selling price.

### Formulas

Profit = SP − CP

Profit % = (Profit / CP) × 100

Profit = (Profit % / 100) × CP

SP = CP × (1 + Profit % / 100)

CP = (SP × 100) / (100 + Profit %)

### Example 1: Calculate Profit Percentage

An item costs ₹500 and is sold for ₹600.

Profit = 600 − 500 = ₹100

Profit % = (100 / 500) × 100 = 20%

**Answer: 20% profit**

### Example 2: Calculate Selling Price

An item costs ₹800 and is sold at a profit of 25%.

SP = 800 × (125 / 100) = ₹1,000

**Answer: ₹1,000**

### Quick Shortcuts

- 20% profit: SP = 1.2 × CP
    
- 25% profit: SP = 1.25 × CP
    
- 50% profit: SP = 1.5 × CP
    

## 3. Loss Percentage

Loss percentage is also calculated on the cost price.

### Formulas

Loss = CP − SP

Loss % = (Loss / CP) × 100

SP = CP × (1 − Loss % / 100)

CP = (SP × 100) / (100 − Loss %)

### Example 1: Calculate Loss Percentage

A watch costs ₹1,200 and is sold for ₹900.

Loss = 1,200 − 900 = ₹300

Loss % = (300 / 1,200) × 100 = 25%

**Answer: 25% loss**

### Example 2: Calculate Cost Price

An item is sold for ₹720 at a loss of 10%.

SP represents 90% of CP.

CP = (720 × 100) / 90 = ₹800

**Answer: ₹800**

**Common exam trap:** A 20% loss does not mean CP = SP × 1.2. Instead, SP is 80% of CP, so divide SP by 0.8 to find CP.

## 4. Marked Price (MP)

The marked price is the listed or tagged price of an item before discount.

A shopkeeper may mark an item above its CP and then offer a discount.

### Formulas

Discount = MP − SP

Discount % = (Discount / MP) × 100

### Example

An item costs ₹800. The shopkeeper marks it at ₹1,200 and sells it for ₹1,080.

- CP = ₹800
    
- MP = ₹1,200
    
- SP = ₹1,080
    
- Discount = 1,200 − 1,080 = ₹120
    
- Discount % = (120 / 1,200) × 100 = 10%
    
- Profit = 1,080 − 800 = ₹280
    
- Profit % = (280 / 800) × 100 = 35%
    

**Remember:** Discount percentage is calculated on MP, while profit percentage is calculated on CP.

## 5. Discount

A discount is the reduction offered on the marked price.

### Formulas

Discount = (Discount % / 100) × MP

SP = MP × (1 − Discount % / 100)

### Example: Calculate Selling Price

A shirt is marked at ₹2,000 and has a 15% discount.

Discount = (15 / 100) × 2,000 = ₹300

SP = 2,000 − 300 = ₹1,700

**Answer: ₹1,700**

### Find MP When SP and Discount Are Given

An item sells for ₹1,360 after a 15% discount.

SP represents 85% of MP.

MP = (1,360 × 100) / 85 = ₹1,600

**Answer: ₹1,600**

### Quick Reference: Discount and Selling Price

|Discount|SP as a percentage of MP|
|---|---|
|10%|90%|
|15%|85%|
|20%|80%|
|25%|75%|
|30%|70%|
|40%|60%|

**Shortcut:** If the discount is d%, then SP is (100 − d)% of MP.

## 6. Successive Discount

A successive discount means applying two or more discounts one after another. The second discount is calculated on the price remaining after the first discount, not on the original marked price.

### Formula for Two Successive Discounts

Equivalent single discount = a + b − (a × b / 100)

Here, a and b are the two discount percentages.

### Example 1: Discounts of 20% and 10%

An item is marked at ₹1,000 and receives successive discounts of 20% and 10%.

First discount = (20 / 100) × 1,000 = ₹200

Price after first discount = ₹800

Second discount = (10 / 100) × 800 = ₹80

Final SP = 800 − 80 = ₹720

Equivalent single discount:

20 + 10 − (20 × 10 / 100)

= 30 − 2

= 28%

Total discount = ₹280, or 28% of ₹1,000, not 30%.

### Example 2: Discounts of 30% and 20%

Equivalent discount = 30 + 20 − (30 × 20 / 100)

= 50 − 6

= 44%

**Answer: 44%**

### Three Successive Discounts

Final SP = MP × (1 − a / 100) × (1 − b / 100) × (1 − c / 100)

### Example: Three Discounts of 10%

A ₹2,000 item receives discounts of 10%, 10%, and 10%.

Final SP = 2,000 × 0.9 × 0.9 × 0.9

= ₹1,458

Equivalent discount = [(2,000 − 1,458) / 2,000] × 100

= 27.1%

**Answer: 27.1% discount**

### Quick Reference: Successive Discounts

|Discounts|Equivalent single discount|
|---|---|
|10% and 10%|19%|
|20% and 10%|28%|
|20% and 20%|36%|
|30% and 20%|44%|
|25% and 20%|40%|

**Important:** The order of percentage discounts does not change the final price. A 20% discount followed by a 10% discount gives the same result as a 10% discount followed by a 20% discount.

## 7. Mixed Profit, Loss and Discount Problems

These questions combine CP, MP, discount, and profit percentage.

### Example

A shopkeeper buys an item for ₹600, marks it 50% above CP, and gives a 20% discount. Find the profit percentage.

**Step 1: Calculate MP.**

MP = 600 × 1.5 = ₹900

**Step 2: Apply the discount.**

SP = 900 × 0.8 = ₹720

**Step 3: Calculate profit.**

Profit = 720 − 600 = ₹120

**Step 4: Calculate profit percentage.**

Profit % = (120 / 600) × 100 = 20%

**Answer: 20% profit**

### Formula for Markup Followed by Discount

If the markup is m% above CP and the discount is d% on MP:

SP = CP × (1 + m / 100) × (1 − d / 100)

This formula is useful for determining whether a shopkeeper earns a profit or suffers a loss after offering a discount.

## 8. Formula Revision Sheet

|Concept|Formula|
|---|---|
|Profit|SP − CP|
|Loss|CP − SP|
|Profit percentage|(Profit / CP) × 100|
|Loss percentage|(Loss / CP) × 100|
|SP at p% profit|CP × (100 + p) / 100|
|SP at l% loss|CP × (100 − l) / 100|
|Discount|MP − SP|

|SP after d% discount|MP × (100 − d) / 100|

|MP after m% markup|CP × (100 + m) / 100|

## 9. Last-Minute Exam Traps

- Profit and loss percentages are calculated on CP.
    
- Discount percentage is calculated on MP.
    
- Always distinguish CP, MP, and SP before calculating.
    
- A discount does not automatically mean a loss.
    
- Successive discounts are not added directly.
    
- A shopkeeper can still earn a profit after offering a discount if the SP exceeds CP.
    
- In mixed questions, calculate MP first, apply the discount to find SP, and then calculate profit or loss.


## 1. Basic Concept of Work

If a person completes a job in x days, the work completed in one day is:

One-day work = 1 / x

For a complete job, total work is taken as 1 unit.

### Example 1

A can complete a job in 10 days.

- One-day work of A = 1 / 10
    
- Work in 4 days = 4 / 10 = 2 / 5
    
- Remaining work = 1 − 2 / 5 = 3 / 5
    

### Example 2

B completes a job in 15 days. How much work does B complete in 6 days?

Work completed = 6 / 15 = 2 / 5

**Answer: 2 / 5 of the job**

**Important:** More time means a lower daily work rate, assuming the total work and efficiency stay constant.

## 2. Work Efficiency

Efficiency means the amount of work completed per unit of time.

Efficiency is inversely proportional to time taken.

For the same job:

Efficiency ratio = Inverse of time ratio

### Example

A completes a job in 12 days, while B completes it in 18 days. Find their efficiency ratio.

A : B = (1 / 12) : (1 / 18)

Multiply both terms by 36:

A : B = 3 : 2

**Answer: 3 : 2**

A is 1.5 times as efficient as B.

### Using Efficiency Units

If A takes 12 days and B takes 18 days, the total work can be taken as the LCM of 12 and 18, which is 36 units.

- A's efficiency = 36 / 12 = 3 units/day
    
- B's efficiency = 36 / 18 = 2 units/day
    

This makes combined-work questions much easier.

## 3. Individual Work

These questions ask how long one person takes to finish a job or how much work they complete in a specified time.

### Example 1: Completing Part of a Job

A can complete a job in 20 days. How long will A take to complete 35% of the job?

Time for 35% = 20 × (35 / 100)

= 7 days

**Answer: 7 days**

### Example 2: Finding Total Time

A completes 3 / 8 of a job in 6 days. How many days will A take to complete the whole job at the same rate?

Total time = 6 × (8 / 3)

= 16 days

**Answer: 16 days**

**Shortcut:** If the rate stays constant, time taken is proportional to the fraction of work completed.

## 4. Combined Work

When two or more people work together, add their daily work rates.

For A and B, who individually finish the job in A days and B days:

1 / T = (1 / A) + (1 / B)

Here, T is the time taken together.

### Example 1: A and B Working Together

A can complete a job in 12 days and B in 18 days. How long will they take together?

Combined daily work = (1 / 12) + (1 / 18)

Taking the LCM, 36:

Combined daily work = 3 / 36 + 2 / 36 = 5 / 36

Time taken = 36 / 5 = 7.2 days

**Answer: 7.2 days**

### Combined-Work Shortcut

For two people who finish the same job individually in A and B days:

Time together = (A × B) / (A + B)

For the example above:

Time = (12 × 18) / (12 + 18)

= 216 / 30

= 7.2 days

### Example 2: One Person Joins Later

A can finish a job in 10 days and B in 15 days. A works alone for 2 days, then B joins. Find the total time.

**Step 1: Calculate A's work in 2 days.**

Work completed = 2 × (1 / 10) = 1 / 5

**Step 2: Calculate the remaining work.**

Remaining work = 1 − 1 / 5 = 4 / 5

**Step 3: Calculate their combined daily work.**

Combined daily work = (1 / 10) + (1 / 15)

= 1 / 6

**Step 4: Calculate the time for the remaining work.**

Time = Remaining work / Combined daily work

= (4 / 5) / (1 / 6)

= 24 / 5 = 4.8 days

**Step 5: Calculate total time.**

Total time = 2 + 4.8 = 6.8 days

**Answer: 6.8 days**

**Exam trap:** If someone joins late, calculate the work completed before they join, then calculate the time required for the remaining work.

## 5. Men and Women

These questions compare workers with different efficiencies. Use the relationship given in the question rather than assuming all workers have equal productivity.

### Example 1: Workers and Days

If 4 men can complete a job in 12 days, how many days will 6 equally efficient men take?

Total work = 4 × 12 = 48 man-days

Required time = 48 / 6 = 8 days

**Answer: 8 days**

### Example 2: Equivalent Workers

If 2 men do as much work as 3 women in one day, how many women are equivalent to 8 men?

Given:

2 men = 3 women

Multiply both sides by 4:

8 men = 12 women

**Answer: 12 women**

### General Formulas

For equally efficient workers doing the same fixed job:

M1 × D1 = M2 × D2

When efficiencies differ:

Total work = Number of workers × Efficiency per worker × Time

**Common trap:** The number of workers and the time taken are inversely proportional only when the job and worker efficiency remain constant.

## 6. Pipes and Cisterns

Pipes-and-cisterns questions use the same principles as time and work.

- An inlet pipe fills a tank, so its rate is positive.
    
- An outlet pipe empties a tank, so its rate is negative.
    

### Case A: Two Inlet Pipes

Pipe A fills a tank in 6 hours, and Pipe B fills it in 12 hours. How long will they take together?

Combined rate = (1 / 6) + (1 / 12)

= 2 / 12 + 1 / 12

= 1 / 4 tank per hour

Time = 1 / (1 / 4) = 4 hours

**Answer: 4 hours**

### Case B: One Inlet and One Outlet

An inlet fills a tank in 8 hours, while an outlet empties it in 12 hours. If both are open, how long will the tank take to fill?

Net rate = (1 / 8) − (1 / 12)

= 3 / 24 − 2 / 24

= 1 / 24 tank per hour

Time = 24 hours

**Answer: 24 hours**

The outlet reduces the net filling rate.

### Case C: Two Outlets

Two outlets empty a tank in 6 hours and 12 hours, respectively. How long will they take together?

Combined emptying rate = (1 / 6) + (1 / 12)

= 1 / 4 tank per hour

Time = 4 hours

**Answer: 4 hours**

### Important Formulas

Net rate = Total inlet rates − Total outlet rates

Time = 1 / Net rate

This assumes the net rate is positive when calculating filling time.

**Exam trap:** Never add an outlet's rate as positive when the question asks how long a tank takes to fill.

## 7. Work and Wages

Wages are distributed according to the amount of work done, not simply the number of days worked.

When workers have different efficiencies:

Wage ratio = Efficiency × Time ratio

### Example 1: Equal Working Time

A and B work for the same number of days. A is twice as efficient as B. If the total wages are ₹9,000, find each person's share.

Efficiency ratio = 2 : 1

Since they work for equal time, work ratio = 2 : 1

Total parts = 2 + 1 = 3

A's wages = (2 / 3) × 9,000 = ₹6,000

B's wages = (1 / 3) × 9,000 = ₹3,000

**Answer: A receives ₹6,000 and B receives ₹3,000.**

### Example 2: Different Efficiencies and Durations

A completes 4 units of work per day for 6 days, while B completes 3 units per day for 4 days. Total wages are ₹1,200. Divide the wages.

A's work = 4 × 6 = 24 units

B's work = 3 × 4 = 12 units

Work ratio = 24 : 12 = 2 : 1

A's wages = (2 / 3) × 1,200 = ₹800

B's wages = (1 / 3) × 1,200 = ₹400

**Answer: A receives ₹800 and B receives ₹400.**

**Shortcut:** Calculate each worker's total contribution first. Divide the wages in the same ratio as their contributions.

## 8. Formula Revision Sheet

|Question type|Formula or method|
|---|---|
|One-day work|1 / Number of days|
|Work in d days|d / Total days|
|Efficiency ratio|Inverse of time ratio|
|Two people working together|T = (A × B) / (A + B)|
|Multiple people working together|Add daily work rates|
|Workers and days, equal efficiency|M1 × D1 = M2 × D2|
|Inlet and outlet|Add inlet rates; subtract outlet rates|
|Wage distribution|Proportional to work completed|
|Work contribution|Efficiency × Time|

## 9. Last-Minute Revision

- One-day work = 1 / Total days.
    
- Combined work requires adding daily work rates.
    
- Efficiency and time are inversely proportional for the same job.
    
- If someone joins late, calculate the completed work and remaining work separately.
    
- For equally efficient workers, more workers mean less time for a fixed job.
    
- Inlet pipes have positive rates; outlet pipes have negative rates.
    
- Wages are proportional to work contributed.
    
- Always check whether the question asks for total time, remaining time, work completed, or wage share.

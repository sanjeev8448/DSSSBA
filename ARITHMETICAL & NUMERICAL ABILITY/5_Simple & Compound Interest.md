
## 1. Basic Terms: Principal, Rate, Time and Amount

**Principal (P)**

The original amount borrowed or invested.

Example: You deposit ₹10,000. The principal is ₹10,000.

**Rate (R)**

The interest rate, usually expressed as a percentage per year.

Example: 8% per annum means 8% per year.

**Time (T)**

The period for which money is borrowed or invested, commonly measured in years.

Example: 6 months = 6 / 12 = 0.5 years.

**Amount (A)**

The total money at the end of the period.

Formula: Amount = Principal + Interest

A = P + Interest

## 2. Simple Interest (SI)

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal throughout the investment period.

### Formula

SI = (P × R × T) / 100

Where:

- P = Principal
    
- R = Annual rate in percent
    
- T = Time in years
    

Amount after simple interest:

A = P + SI

### Example 1: Calculate SI

A person invests ₹5,000 at 8% per annum for 3 years. Find the SI and total amount.

SI = (5,000 × 8 × 3) / 100

SI = ₹1,200

Amount = 5,000 + 1,200 = ₹6,200

**Answer: SI = ₹1,200; Amount = ₹6,200**

### Example 2: Time Given in Months

Find SI on ₹12,000 at 10% per annum for 6 months.

Convert months into years:

T = 6 / 12 = 0.5 years

SI = (12,000 × 10 × 0.5) / 100

SI = ₹600

**Answer: ₹600**

**Exam trap:** If the rate is annual, convert months into years before using the formula.

### Example 3: Find the Rate

Simple interest on ₹4,000 for 2 years is ₹800. Find the annual rate.

Formula: R = (SI × 100) / (P × T)

R = (800 × 100) / (4,000 × 2)

R = 10%

**Answer: 10% per annum**

### Rearranged SI Formulas

- Principal: P = (SI × 100) / (R × T)
    
- Rate: R = (SI × 100) / (P × T)
    
- Time: T = (SI × 100) / (P × R)
    

These formulas are useful when a question asks you to find the missing principal, rate, or time.

## 3. Compound Interest (CI)

Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus the interest already accumulated. This is why it is often described as "interest on interest."

### Formula for Annual Compounding

Amount = P × (1 + R / 100)^T

Compound Interest = Amount − Principal

CI = A − P

### Example 1: Calculate CI

A person invests ₹10,000 at 10% per annum for 2 years, compounded annually.

**Year 1**

Interest = 10% of ₹10,000 = ₹1,000

Amount after Year 1 = ₹11,000

**Year 2**

Interest = 10% of ₹11,000 = ₹1,100

Amount after Year 2 = ₹12,100

Therefore:

CI = 12,100 − 10,000 = ₹2,100

**Answer: CI = ₹2,100; Amount = ₹12,100**

### Shortcut Using the Formula

Amount = 10,000 × (1 + 10 / 100)^2

Amount = 10,000 × (1.1)^2

Amount = ₹12,100

CI = 12,100 − 10,000 = ₹2,100

### Example 2: Three Years

Find the amount and CI on ₹8,000 at 5% per annum for 3 years, compounded annually.

Amount = 8,000 × (1.05)^3

Amount = 8,000 × 1.157625

Amount = ₹9,261

CI = 9,261 − 8,000 = ₹1,261

**Answer: Amount = ₹9,261; CI = ₹1,261**

**Important:** In compound interest, each year's interest is added to the balance before calculating the next year's interest.

## 4. Simple Interest vs. Compound Interest

|Feature|Simple Interest|Compound Interest|
|---|---|---|
|Interest calculated on|Original principal|Accumulated balance|
|Interest each year at a fixed rate|Same amount|Usually increases|
|Interest formula|SI = (P × R × T) / 100|CI = A − P|
|Amount formula|A = P × (1 + R × T / 100)|A = P × (1 + R / 100)^T for annual compounding|
|For one year at the same rate and principal|Equal to CI|Equal to SI|
|For more than one year at a positive rate|Usually lower|Usually higher|

**Key exam point:** If the principal, rate, and duration are the same, annual CI and SI are equal after one year. With a positive rate and a period longer than one year, CI exceeds SI.

## 5. Difference Between SI and CI

For two years, with the same principal and annual rate:

Difference = CI − SI

Difference = P × (R / 100)^2

This shortcut applies to two years with annual compounding.

### Example: Two-Year Difference

Find the difference between SI and CI on ₹10,000 at 10% per annum for 2 years.

Difference = 10,000 × (10 / 100)^2

Difference = 10,000 × 0.01

Difference = ₹100

**Answer: ₹100**

Check using the individual amounts:

- SI = (10,000 × 10 × 2) / 100 = ₹2,000
    
- CI = ₹2,100
    
- Difference = 2,100 − 2,000 = ₹100
    

### Three-Year Difference

For three years, with annual compounding:

Difference = P × [3 × (R / 100)^2 + (R / 100)^3]

This formula assumes the same principal and annual rate.

Example: For ₹10,000 at 10% per annum for 3 years:

SI = (10,000 × 10 × 3) / 100 = ₹3,000

CI = ₹3,310

Difference = 3,310 − 3,000 = ₹310

**Answer: ₹310**

## 6. Annual vs. Half-Yearly Compounding

### Annual Compounding

Interest is added to the balance once per year.

Amount = P × (1 + R / 100)^T

For example, 10% per annum for 2 years means two compounding periods at 10% each.

### Half-Yearly Compounding

Interest is added twice per year.

Therefore:

- Rate per period = R / 2
    
- Number of periods = 2 × T
    

Formula:

Amount = P × (1 + R / 200)^(2 × T)

CI = Amount − P

### Example: Half-Yearly Compounding

Find the CI on ₹10,000 at 10% per annum for 1 year, compounded half-yearly.

Rate per half-year = 10% / 2 = 5%

Number of periods = 2 × 1 = 2

**First half-year**

Amount = 10,000 × 1.05 = ₹10,500

**Second half-year**

Amount = 10,500 × 1.05 = ₹11,025

CI = 11,025 − 10,000 = ₹1,025

**Answer: CI = ₹1,025; Amount = ₹11,025**

### Comparison of Interest Methods

|Method|Final amount|Interest|
|---|---|---|
|Simple interest for 1 year at 10%|₹11,000|₹1,000|
|Annual compounding at 10%|₹11,000|₹1,000|
|Half-yearly compounding at 10% nominal annual rate|₹11,025|₹1,025|

**Exam trap:** For half-yearly compounding, do not use the full annual rate in every period. Halve the rate and double the number of periods.

### Quarterly Compounding

If interest is compounded quarterly:

- Rate per quarter = R / 4
    
- Number of periods = 4 × T
    

Formula:

Amount = P × (1 + R / 400)^(4 × T)

## 8. Last-Minute Revision

- Principal is the original amount invested or borrowed.
    
- Rate is usually expressed as a percentage per annum.
    
- Time must be in years for the standard SI formula.
    
- Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal.
    
- Compound interest includes interest accumulated in previous periods.
    
- For one year, SI and annual CI are equal at the same rate and principal.
    
- For two years, CI − SI = P × (R / 100)^2.
    
- Half-yearly compounding: halve the rate and double the number of periods.
    
- Quarterly compounding: divide the rate by four and multiply the number of years by four.
    
- Always check whether the question asks for interest alone or the final amount.

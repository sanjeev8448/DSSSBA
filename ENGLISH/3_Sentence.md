## 1. Sentence Improvement

In sentence improvement questions, a sentence contains an incorrect or awkward phrase. You must select the option that makes it grammatically correct without changing its intended meaning.

### Rules to remember

|Rule|Incorrect|Correct|
|---|---|---|
|Subject–verb agreement|He go to school.|He goes to school.|
|Tense|I have met him yesterday.|I met him yesterday.|
|Comparison|She is senior than me.|She is senior to me.|
|Verb pattern|He enjoys to read.|He enjoys reading.|
|Infinitive|She made me to cry.|She made me cry.|
|Redundancy|Return back the book.|Return the book.|
|Conditional|If I was rich, I would travel.|If I were rich, I would travel.|
|Parallel structure|She likes reading, dancing and to sing.|She likes reading, dancing and singing.|

Exam tip: First identify the underlined or suspicious phrase. Check the tense, subject–verb agreement, preposition, verb form, comparison, and meaning before choosing an answer.

### Common verb patterns

|Verb or expression|Correct pattern|Example|
|---|---|---|
|Enjoy, avoid, finish|Verb + `-ing`|He avoids wasting time.|
|Want, decide, hope|To + base verb|She decided to study.|
|Make/let + object|Base verb|They made him wait.|
|Look forward to|Verb + `-ing`|I look forward to meeting you.|
|Used to|Base verb|He used to play cricket.|
|Be used to|Noun/verb + `-ing`|He is used to waking early.|
|Prefer|Prefer X to Y|I prefer tea to coffee.|

Remember: in look forward to meeting, “to” is a preposition, so it takes a gerund (`meeting`), not an infinitive (`to meet`).

## 2. Fill in the Blanks

These questions test grammar, vocabulary, prepositions, articles, conjunctions, and logical meaning.

### A. Tense and verb forms

- She ___ to the office every day. → goes
    
- They ___ their work already. → have completed
    
- By the time we arrived, the train ___. → had left
    
- If he studies hard, he ___ the exam. → will pass
    

### B. Articles

- He is ___ honest man. → an
    
- She studies at ___ university. → a
    
- ___ Sun rises in the east. → The
    

Choose `a` or `an` according to the sound, not simply the first letter.

### C. Prepositions

- She is interested ___ science. → in
    
- He is good ___ mathematics. → at
    
- We have lived here ___ 2020. → since
    
- I have waited ___ two hours. → for
    
- He is senior ___ me. → to
    

### D. Conjunctions and logical meaning

- ___ he was tired, he continued working. → Although
    
- Hurry up, ___ you will miss the bus. → or
    
- You cannot succeed ___ you work hard. → unless (meaning: if you do not work hard)
    
- Neither Rahul ___ Amit was present. → nor
    

### E. Vocabulary and collocations

|Expression|Correct usage|
|---|---|
|Make a decision|Not usually “do a decision”|
|Pay attention|Not “give attention” in standard exam usage|
|Heavy rain|Not “strong rain”|
|Take responsibility|Correct collocation|
|Raise a question|Correct collocation|
|Commit a mistake|Prefer “make a mistake” in standard English|

Strategy: Read the entire sentence first. Predict the type of word required—verb, noun, preposition, or conjunction—then check which option fits both grammar and meaning.

## 3. Sentence Rearrangement

Sentence rearrangement questions give you a set of jumbled words or sentences. You must arrange them to form a grammatically correct, logical sentence or paragraph.

### A. Rearranging words

Example:

`every / reading / day / improves / your vocabulary`

Correct sentence: Reading every day improves your vocabulary.

Use these clues:

1. Find the subject: who or what is the sentence about?
    
2. Find the main verb.
    
3. Place the object or complement after the verb, where required.
    
4. Position time, place, and manner phrases naturally.
    
5. Check capitalization and punctuation.
    

Example:

`the exam / because / he studied hard / he passed`

Correct sentence: He passed the exam because he studied hard.

### B. Rearranging sentences into a paragraph

A paragraph generally follows this logical pattern:

1. Introduction

Introduces the topic or main idea.

2. Explanation or supporting details

Gives reasons, examples, or evidence.

3. Conclusion or result

Finishes the idea or states the outcome.

### Important clues for rearrangement

- Pronouns: “He,” “she,” “it,” and “they” usually refer to someone or something introduced earlier.
    
- Articles: “A” or “an” often introduces something for the first time; “the” often refers to something already identified.
    
- Connectors: “However” indicates contrast; “therefore” indicates a result; “furthermore” adds information.
    
- Sequence words: “First,” “then,” “next,” and “finally” establish order.
    
- Cause and effect: A reason often comes before its result, although the sentence structure can vary.
    

Example paragraph:

P. Finally, the students submitted their projects.

Q. They first collected the necessary information.

R. The teacher assigned a project to the class.

S. After collecting the information, they prepared their reports.

Correct order: R → Q → S → P

## 4. Correct Usage

Correct usage tests whether a word, phrase, idiom, or grammatical structure is used properly in context.

### Commonly tested distinctions

|Words|Correct distinction|Example|
|---|---|---|
|Affect / Effect|Affect is usually a verb; effect is usually a noun.|Sleep affects performance.|
|Advice / Advise|Advice is a noun; advise is a verb.|She advised me to follow his advice.|
|Principal / Principle|Principal means chief or head; principle means a rule or belief.|The principal explained the principle.|
|Stationary / Stationery|Stationary means not moving; stationery means writing materials.|The stationary vehicle was beside the stationery shop.|
|Accept / Except|Accept means receive or agree; except means excluding.|Everyone accepted the invitation except Rahul.|
|Lose / Loose|Lose means fail to keep or win; loose means not tight.|Do not lose the loose screw.|
|Its / It's|Its is possessive; it's means “it is” or “it has.”|It's a dog with its toy.|
|Fewer / Less|Fewer for countable items; less for amounts.|Fewer students, less water.|
|Between / Among|Between is commonly used for two; among for a group.|Between the two options; among the students.|
|Your / You're|Your indicates possession; you're means “you are.”|You're holding your book.|

### Common idioms and expressions

|Expression|Meaning|
|---|---|
|A piece of cake|Very easy|
|Once in a blue moon|Very rarely|
|Break the ice|Start a conversation or reduce awkwardness|
|Hit the nail on the head|Say or identify exactly the right thing|
|At the eleventh hour|At the last moment|
|In hot water|In trouble|
|Under the weather|Feeling unwell|
|Spill the beans|Reveal a secret|
|Call it a day|Stop working for the day|
|Burn the midnight oil|Work or study late into the night|

### Common errors to avoid

- Incorrect: Discuss about the issue.

Correct: Discuss the issue.

- Incorrect: He is capable to do it.

Correct: He is capable of doing it.

- Incorrect: Despite of the rain, we went out.

Correct: Despite the rain, we went out. Alternatively: In spite of the rain, we went out.

- Incorrect: One of the best student.

Correct: One of the best students.

- Incorrect: She did not knew the answer.

Correct: She did not know the answer.

- Incorrect: He is more wiser than his brother.

Correct: He is wiser than his brother.

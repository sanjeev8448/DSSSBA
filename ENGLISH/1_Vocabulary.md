## 1. Synonyms

A synonym is a word that has the same or nearly the same meaning as another word. The correct choice depends on the context.

|Word|Meaning|Synonym|
|---|---|---|
|Abundant|Available in large quantities|Plentiful|
|Accurate|Correct and precise|Exact|
|Adversity|Difficulty or hardship|Misfortune|
|Benevolent|Kind and generous|Charitable|
|Candid|Honest and direct|Frank|
|Diligent|Hardworking and careful|Industrious|
|Eloquent|Fluent and persuasive|Articulate|
|Fragile|Easily broken|Delicate|
|Hostile|Unfriendly|Antagonistic|
|Inevitable|Certain to happen|Unavoidable|
|Meticulous|Very careful about details|Thorough|
|Obsolete|No longer in use|Outdated|
|Resilient|Able to recover quickly|Tough|
|Valiant|Brave|Courageous|
|Vivid|Clear and lively|Graphic|

### Important exam distinctions

- Diligent = hardworking; intelligent = quick to understand.
    
- Candid = honest and direct; polite = courteous. They are not synonyms.
    
- Meticulous = careful about details; neat = tidy.
    
- Resilient = able to recover; resistant = able to withstand something.
    
- Eloquent = able to express ideas effectively; loquacious = talking a lot.
    

## 2. Antonyms

An antonym is a word with the opposite meaning.

|Word|Meaning|Antonym|
|---|---|---|
|Abundant|Plentiful|Scarce|
|Ancient|Very old|Modern|
|Artificial|Man-made|Natural|
|Benevolent|Kind|Malevolent|
|Conceal|Hide|Reveal|
|Expand|Increase in size|Contract|
|Explicit|Clearly stated|Implicit|
|Frugal|Economical|Extravagant|
|Genuine|Real|Counterfeit|
|Humble|Modest|Arrogant|
|Optimistic|Hopeful|Pessimistic|
|Permanent|Lasting|Temporary|
|Rigid|Inflexible|Flexible|
|Transparent|See-through / clear|Opaque|
|Victory|Success in a contest|Defeat|

### Commonly confused pairs

- Accept / Reject: receive or agree / refuse.
    
- Complement / Supplement: complete something / add something to it.
    
- Emigrate / Immigrate: leave a country / enter another country to settle.
    
- Imply / Infer: suggest indirectly / draw a conclusion.
    
- Precede / Succeed: come before / come after or achieve success, depending on context.
    

## 3. One-word substitution

One-word substitution means replacing a phrase with one word that expresses the same idea.

|Phrase|One-word substitution|
|---|---|
|A person who loves books|Bibliophile|
|A person who hates mankind|Misanthrope|
|A person who loves mankind|Philanthropist|
|A person who speaks many languages|Polyglot|
|A person who cannot read or write|Illiterate|
|A person who writes dictionaries|Lexicographer|
|A person who compiles an encyclopaedia or dictionary|Compiler|
|A person who studies stars and celestial objects|Astronomer|
|A person who studies plants|Botanist|
|A person who studies animals|Zoologist|
|Government by the people|Democracy|
|Government by a king or queen|Monarchy|
|A place where birds are kept|Aviary|
|A place where bees are kept|Apiary|
|A place where dead bodies are kept temporarily|Mortuary|
|A speech delivered without preparation|Impromptu|
|Something that cannot be avoided|Inevitable|
|Something that cannot be read|Illegible|
|Something that cannot be heard|Inaudible|
|Something that cannot be corrected|Incorrigible|

Memory trick: Learn words by their roots. For example, bibliophile combines biblio (book) and phile (love).

## 4. Word meaning and vocabulary usage

A word can have different meanings depending on the sentence. In the exam, use the surrounding words to identify the intended meaning.

|Word|Meaning|Example sentence|
|---|---|---|
|Reluctant|Unwilling or hesitant|He was reluctant to leave his job.|
|Prudent|Wise and careful|It is prudent to save money regularly.|
|Ambiguous|Having more than one possible meaning|The instructions were ambiguous.|
|Inevitable|Certain to happen|Change is inevitable.|
|Deteriorate|Become worse|His health began to deteriorate.|
|Alleviate|Make something less severe|The medicine helped alleviate the pain.|
|Scrutinise|Examine carefully|The officer scrutinised the documents.|
|Impartial|Fair; not favouring either side|A judge must remain impartial.|
|Obsolete|No longer useful or current|The old machine is obsolete.|
|Plausible|Seeming reasonable or believable|She gave a plausible explanation.|
|Astute|Clever at understanding situations|The astute manager noticed the problem.|
|Tedious|Boring and tiring because it takes time|Data entry can be tedious.|
|Subtle|Not obvious or easy to notice|There was a subtle difference between them.|
|Mitigate|Make less harmful or serious|The measures helped mitigate the risk.|
|Exacerbate|Make a problem worse|Careless comments can exacerbate the conflict.|

### Words frequently confused in usage

|Word|Meaning|Example|
|---|---|---|
|Affect|Usually a verb meaning to influence|Sleep affects concentration.|
|Effect|Usually a noun meaning a result|The effect was immediate.|
|Advice|Noun: recommendation|She gave useful advice.|
|Advise|Verb: recommend|I advise you to practise daily.|
|Principal|Main; or head of a school|The principal addressed the students.|
|Principle|A rule or belief|Honesty is an important principle.|
|Stationary|Not moving|The vehicle remained stationary.|
|Stationery|Writing materials|I bought stationery.|
|Complement|Complete or go well with something|The colours complement each other.|
|Compliment|Praise|He received a compliment.|

## 5. Prefixes and suffixes — a shortcut to meanings

Prefixes and suffixes help you infer the meaning of unfamiliar words.

|Prefix/suffix|Meaning|Example|
|---|---|---|
|un-|Not / opposite|Unfair|
|dis-|Opposite / absence|Disagree|
|mis-|Wrongly|Misinterpret|
|pre-|Before|Preview|
|anti-|Against|Antisocial|
|inter-|Between|International|
|-less|Without|Fearless|
|-ful|Full of|Hopeful|
|-able|Capable of|Readable|
|-phobia|Fear of|Claustrophobia|
|-logy|Study of|Biology|
|-cide|Killing|Pesticide|

Important: Prefixes can provide clues, but they do not always reveal the complete meaning. Use context as well.

## 6. How to solve vocabulary MCQs

1. Read the entire sentence, not just the target word.
    
2. Identify whether the question asks for a synonym, antonym, meaning, or usage.
    
3. Eliminate options that do not fit the context or grammatical form.
    
4. For one-word substitutions, focus on the key idea in the phrase.
    
5. For unfamiliar words, use roots, prefixes, suffixes, and context clues.

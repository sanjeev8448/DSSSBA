## 2. Idioms vs phrases

A phrase is a group of words that functions as a unit. An idiom is an expression with a conventional meaning that often cannot be understood literally.

|Expression|Type|Meaning|
|---|---|---|
|In the morning|Ordinary phrase|During the morning|
|A piece of cake|Idiom|Something very easy|
|Under the table|Ordinary phrase in a literal context|Below a table|
|Under the weather|Idiom|Feeling unwell|
|In black and white|Often idiomatic|In written or printed form|
|By and large|Idiom|Generally; on the whole|
|Pros and cons|Fixed expression|Advantages and disadvantages|
|In a nutshell|Idiom|In a very brief form|
|Now and then|Idiom/fixed expression|Occasionally|
|Safe and sound|Fixed expression|Safe and unharmed|

An expression's type can depend on its context. For example, “under the table” is literal when describing a location, but it can be idiomatic in a context involving secret or improper payments.

## 3. How to solve idiom questions

Use these three steps:

1. Recognise the expression. Read the complete sentence rather than translating each word separately.
    
2. Identify the intended meaning. Ask what the expression communicates in that context.
    
3. Check the usage. The idiom should fit the sentence grammatically and logically.
    

Example:

“He visits his hometown once in a blue moon.”

- Literal interpretation: once during a blue moon.
    
- Intended meaning: very rarely.
    

Another example:

“The manager asked him to get to the point instead of beating around the bush.”

Here, “beating around the bush” means avoiding the main topic.
